**Art that leads settles the card (2026-11-18).** Bryan: the collection and deck scanners get "stuck about 50% of the time". Measured [ran] on 124 of his own scan photos from production's `scan_samples` (640 px card crops with their quads and labels; the art bundle `gallery-20261106T183001884398Z` run offline with the same `embed.onnx` and `search.onnx` and the same scoring, which reproduces the scores the phone logged): the right card is the best match on 96% of them, but scores a median **0.78** (10th percentile 0.65), against 0.97 on the repo's synthetic eval. Only **36%** reach 0.82 (`LOCK_ONCE`, `ART_SURE`). Below that the scanner read the title on every frame (`needsTitle`), and a fair match (0.62) locked only after four frames whose title couldn't be read (`UNTITLED_FRAMES`): that was the stall.

What separates right from wrong on real photos is the **lead**: the best match's score minus the best score of a *different* card (other printings and illustrations of the same card, and either face of a two-faced card, aren't rivals). Every wrong best match had a lead under 0.06, the foil Westvale Abbey taken for Wei Night Raiders among them (0.645, with Honor the Fallen 0.611 next). A rule "best score at least *a*, lead at least *m*", on one frame:

| Rule | Known cards locked (124 photos) | Wrong locks | Unknown cards locked wrongly (124) |
|---|---|---|---|
| *a* ≥ 0.82, *m* ≥ 0.03 (before: `ART_SURE`, `ART_CLOSE`) | 36.3% | 0 | 0 |
| *a* ≥ 0.65, *m* ≥ 0.10 (**chosen**) | **84.7%** | **0** | **0** |
| *a* ≥ 0.65, *m* ≥ 0.08 | 87.9% | 0 | 0 |
| *a* ≥ 0.62, *m* ≥ 0.08 | 90.3% | 0 | 2 |
| *a* ≥ 0.60, *m* ≥ 0.05 | 93.5% | 1 | 12 |

"Unknown cards" hides every artwork of the photo's card, as if the bundle lacked it: the best (wrong) match then scored a median 0.584 (max 0.726), with a median lead of 0.018 (max 0.102). The phone sees only the search's best 5 artworks (with the set lock's pool and the supplement merged in), so when no other card is listed, the 5th score stands in for the rival (nothing unlisted scores above it); on these photos that changed nothing, as no photo had five artworks of one card on top.

Now (`LEAD_FLOOR` 0.65, `LEAD_MARGIN` 0.10, `artLead`, `leads` in `fuse.ts`): a match that leads is treated like a sure one. Its title isn't read, and it settles which card. The live camera takes it once the same card has led on two frames in a row (`LEAD_FRAMES`, `LeadRun`: beyond what the single-frame data asks, and cheap with no title read between them; counted by card, not artwork, as the set lock can swap the best match between a card's in-set and outside artworks); a photo takes it on its one frame. Never when locked to sets whose art the index mostly lacks (`leadCounts` in `setlock.ts`: a card from the same set could lead for one it doesn't know, a case the photos didn't test), nor while a new lock's pool is still being set and that share isn't known yet (`poolReady`), nor while a card filed by its name is still held; the 0.82 rule is as it was, and the two-frame fallback at 0.62 with `UNTITLED_FRAMES` stays for matches that don't lead. In the collection the printing is then settled exactly as before. In identity-only scans (deck check, deck scan, box scan) a leading match has no lookalike close enough to need its title read, and Keep going counts it by itself (`Found.leads`, as for `titled`; where it has lookalikes, like `titled` it waits for a tap under the name just filed until the view has been clear). Scanner diagnostics now note each frame's lead, and which rule settled the card (`lock`: sure, leads, twice, title or text) in the record's `scan`, so slow-find records can show how often each fires. Since 20 Nov 2026 their timing records give the same for every card settled, not only the slow ones (`get_scan_timings`).

Limits: one person's single session, all successful scans (samples are saved only on add or correct), so biased toward easier cards; real stuck scans weren't captured because Scanner diagnostics were off. A check on Bryan's phone is still to do. The scripts and data are outside the repository (`~/Developer/.agent-tasks/scanner-model-lab/`, `scripts/lockrule.py`, `unknown.py`, `realeval.py`); the photos are real people's data and stay there.

**Deck checks: Keep going, speed and slow finds (2026-11-17).** Bryan, checking a deck on his phone: about a third of the cards waited for "Count it and check next", scanning was "crazy slow" and "laggy", it sat on "Hold still", and the last card's "Belongs" stayed up while the scanner was on the next card. Causes found in the code, not measured on the phone [inferred]:
- **Lookalikes stopped Keep going.** In an identity-only scan (deck scan, deck check, box scan, swap check) a card found by its art was counted by itself only if no other card's art scored within 0.2 of it. With his right cards at 0.64 to 0.70, nearly every card had one, even when a clear title read had named it. Now a card whose art and title agree (`Found.titled`) is counted by itself, and in identity-only scans art sure on its own (0.82) but with lookalikes has its title read first, falling back to the old untitled find when the title isn't clear. A card found by art and title under the name just filed waits for a tap until the view has been clear of cards (`filedName`), so the art hold letting go on one blurry frame can't count the same card twice.
- **Weak reads agreeing now count.** A title read short of clear (0.7, 0.04 ahead of the next name: `TITLE_AGREES`, `AGREE_MARGIN` in `fuse.ts`) settles the card when it names one of the art's matches, and so does a whole-card text read at 0.6 or more (`TEXT_VETO`). Before, both were ignored. Only in identity-only scans, and not while a card filed by its name is held: in the collection, a lookalike with one printing would be filed by itself (review of 17 Nov 2026).
- **The endless "Hold still".** The art's own fallback (four frames of the same match) and the title close-up both waited for a whole-card text read that found nothing at all; a noisy read naming some card at low confidence held both back for as long as the card was held. Now, in identity-only scans, only a read at `TEXT_VETO` or more holds them back, and a fair match (0.62) gets its four frames before the close-up opens; the collection scanner is unchanged.
- **Speed.** The camera streamed 4K and every frame cut the card at up to 2400 px to read its title, for the collector line that identity-only scans never read. They now ask for 1080p and cut the card at 1200 px (`IDENTITY_SIDE`); the title is read at `NAME_DENSITY` either way.
- **The verdict over the camera** shows for 2.5 s after a card is counted (`VERDICT_SHOWN_MS`) and goes as soon as the scanner reads another card (the `overlay` prop's `another`).
- **Slow finds are kept** by Scanner diagnostics ("slow-find"): a card that took over 3 s to find has what each frame read while looking (the art's top three, the title reads and their match, the whole-card read) in its record's `seek`, and a card never found is kept with no card when it leaves the view or the scanner pauses. At most 25 a session, so they don't push the other records out (the server keeps 200 per account); a look started before diagnostics were turned off, or under another account, is never sent. Before, a record started only once a card was found, so a stall before that left nothing to look at.

**Hold and read (2026-11-15).** Bryan, on his Pixel 10 Pro: "I want to just hold and scan the card and get all the details of the card... I don't want to have to move the card", with the close-up kept as something to ask for, not a step every time. So when several printings share the art and the first read of the collector line doesn't settle it, the scanner keeps reading (`heldRead.ts`, the hold loop in `ScannerView`): each frame it finds the card again with the art recognizer, cuts the card from the camera's full-resolution frame (`around`, up to 2400 px; the camera asks for 3840 × 2160) and reads the collector line the three ways `readCollectorLines` does. A read that proves the printing settles it. Otherwise three frames must name the same printing (`AGREE`; the close-up asks for two, from several times more pixels). Frames that read nothing don't break a run; a frame naming another printing starts a new one. With Close-ups on, the close-up box takes over after six frames (`CLOSEUP_AFTER`); with it off, the scanner reads for up to 40 frames and then leaves the picker. The held card has to stay the best match in view (within 0.02 of it, or 0.06 with the set lock), so a different card held up next isn't read against this card's printings. If the card is out of view for four frames in a row, or four reads in a row fail, it stops; it also pauses while "Wrong card" is open. The picker and "Read the collector number up close" stay shown throughout. A card found by its text (no art match) still goes to the close-up at once. Not measured on a phone yet; the frame counts are guesses to tune with real scans. Scan settings also names the text reader in use (ML Kit, PaddleOCR or Tesseract; `ENGINE_LABEL` in `ocrEngine.ts`), so you can tell which one a device has.

**Reading by evidence, Wrong printing, reading after a typed name, and the stuck band (2026-11-15).** Bryan, on his Pixel 10 Pro: "the printing read gives up too early"; he kept pressing retry. When the scanner settled a printing on its own it was sometimes wrong, with no quick way to say so, before or after the card was filed. When he typed a card's name (a stack whose art wasn't recognised) he wanted the printing read from the live camera too, without the close-up box. And he saw "placeholder text" in the scanner. A consult on the scanner (same day) added: accumulate evidence instead of counting agreeing frames; skip frames too blurred or glared to read; read the title when the art isn't sure. So:

- **Evidence, not a run of agreeing frames** (`evidence.ts`). Each candidate printing says what its collector line should show: number with any promo suffix, set code (The List's: the original's, from "MOM-214"), rarity letter, language. Every crop of every frame is scored against that, token by token. A token read as printed scores 1; one matching only through OCR lookalikes (0/O/D/Q, 1/I/L/T, 5/S, 8/B, 2/Z, 6/G, 4/A, U/V) scores 0.7; one lookalike edit away scores 0.3. The best per field in a frame is weighted (number 1.5, set 1, rarity 0.25, language 0.25) and added to the printing's total.
  - A token counts for one field only: the number comes from a token that looks like a number (digits, or digit lookalikes with at least one real digit, and at most a suffix letter), the set code from one with a letter in it, never the same token for both. (Review of 15 Nov 2026: "SLD" had matched SLD #510's number through lookalikes.)
  - A printing is settled when its total is at least 2.4, it leads the next by 1.5, it holds 0.9 of a softmax posterior (temperature 3), each field it prints was read at least 0.6 in some frame, and at least one of them was read exactly as printed with the other at least through lookalikes. Lookalike-only reads ("O14S W0C") order the picker and show progress, and never settle (nor does Keep adding file them). Frames before M15 (July 2014) print no set code, so there an exactly read number is enough.
  - So one clean read of set code and number still settles at once, as a proving read did. A stray frame naming another printing no longer resets anything. A set code read in one frame adds up with a number read in another. A set code alone never settles it, however often it's read; a number alone does only when it's enough (below, "The number that matters").
  - **Twins:** a printing from The List prints the original's set and number, and OCR easily drops a promo suffix (107p, 107s). When another candidate shows the same set and number, reading them is no proof, and the card is left to the picker, unless the leader's own suffix was read. A card found by its text has its collector line weighed the same way, as one frame (`proofOf`): it proves the printing only when the evidence would settle it, never from the text reader's confidence, which is about the card (re-review of 15 Nov 2026: "O14S W0C • EN" under a clear "Arcane Signet" scored 0.97 and had been taken as proof of WOC #145). Otherwise that printing goes first in the list and the read is the first frame of hold and read. Card identity still comes from confidence (a text read locks on 0.95 once or 0.6 twice; identity-only hosts file a text-found card at 0.8): that names the card, not its printing. A set's promo set ("psoi" for SOI, "pwoe" for WOE: prerelease and promo-pack cards; or any set the API names as a promo set's parent) prints the set's own code, so SOI 281 and the prerelease promo 281s are twins, as are WOE 82 and 82p. Mystery Booster (mb1, fmb1, mb2) and The List print the original printing's set and number; The List's numbers say which ("MOM-214"), Mystery Booster's don't, so a Mystery Booster printing among the candidates is a twin of all of them: nothing settles by itself unless its own code was read exactly [inferred]. `/api/scan/printings` doesn't send `parent_set_code` yet (the server has it in `calendar_sets`); until it does, promo sets are known by the "p" + set code pattern.
  - **The whole bottom strip** is read: after the bottom-left cuts, the bottom right too (`RIGHT_COLLECTOR_REGION`), where some showcase, borderless and promo frames and two-faced cards' backs put the line. A "number/total" counts only with a plausible set size as the total (at least 20, and at least the number: "281/297"); anything else with a slash is power and toughness (the "9/7" of a back face) and is dropped (`withoutTotals`).
  - The first read's crops are its first frame, and that evidence carries into hold and read. The close-up box weighs its reads the same way.
  - Settling by look (2026-11-20, an opt-in look option) never goes against this evidence: not when another printing's set code or number was read exactly, or the evidence settled on another (`pointsAway`; "Settling by look").
  - All the weights and thresholds are first guesses [inferred]; `evidence.test.ts` covers lookalikes, a stray frame, split fields, one field alone, the margin, The List and promo suffixes.
- **What it has read so far** shows in the sheet while it reads: "Read the set code (WOC), not the number yet", "Read the number (145), not the set code yet", "Read WOC 145; checking it against the other 31 printings", or that another printing shows the same.
- **Blurred and glared frames are skipped before reading** (`quality.ts`): the variance of the Laplacian of the collector strip, cut at twice the size and averaged 2 × 2 so sensor noise doesn't pass for edges, and the share of its pixels clipped to white.
  - Measured on synthetic frames of 15 cards, 900 px tall, at six blurs [ran] (`eval/quality.test.ts`, PaddleOCR): every frame that read scored 70 or more (blur up to σ 1.8); from σ 2.4 one frame in 45 read, and they scored 56 to 84.
  - So the strip must score 65, which skips only frames that wouldn't have read there. The two overlap, so it can't catch every unreadable frame. Clipped: at most 20% [inferred].
  - A skipped frame keeps the card counted as in view but isn't a frame read toward the budgets.
- **Time, not frames, as the outer cap** (`heldRead.ts`, `HOLD_BUDGET`, every number in one place). The first budgets were frame counts: 6 frames before the close-up took over, 40 before giving up, 4 missed frames to stop. On a phone reading three crops a frame that came to one or two seconds, ten at most, and under a second without the card. Now:
  - with Close-ups on, the close-up box takes over after 8 s and at least 6 frames read;
  - with it off, the scanner reads for 25 s and at least 12 frames before leaving it to the picker;
  - the card may be out of view (or another card seen) for 2.5 s before an attempt stops, and 8 failed reads in a row stop it too;
  - a card not yet held up (a typed name) gets 10 s to appear;
  - the frame counts keep a slow phone reading long enough, but at twice the time an attempt is over whatever the count or the frames said (every frame skipped as blurred, or every read failing; `capped`), and every verdict, from a read, a skipped frame or a failed read alike, is acted on.

  These are guesses [inferred]: frame rate differs by phone and by text reader.
- **Every retry is a full attempt.** The attempt is tied to the card as found and a retry counter. A new card, any change to it, Wrong printing, coming back from the close-up box ("Back to reading the whole card", new) or **Keep reading** (in the printing prompt once an attempt has given up; "Read printing" before one has run) starts again with the whole budget and fresh evidence.
- **Wrong printing.** When the scanner settled the printing itself (read from the collector line, the only English printing, or the only one in the locked sets), the found card says "Wrong printing?" instead of "Change printing".
  - It reopens the list, marking the misread "Read as this". When the first list held only that printing, the list is every printing of the card. It reads the held card again as a fresh attempt, and Keep adding won't file that card by itself.
  - **After filing,** the camera's "Added …" chip offers Wrong printing next to Undo, where the host can correct a copy (`onCorrect`; Collection). The card comes back with its list, the filed printing marked "Added as this", plus "Change to this printing" and "Keep it as it was". Tapping a printing changes the copy already filed (`printingFix` in `session.ts`, the Added list's own fix): this session's copies leave the wrong printing's row and are added under the right one, so none is left behind and none is added twice. The chip then says "Changed to …" (or "Kept …" when the host says the copy already was that printing) with Undo for that copy, and "Scan anyway" stays a new copy of the corrected printing. When the host can't confirm the change, the chip says so and points to the Added list, which holds the change and tries it again, with no Undo, Wrong printing or Scan anyway on a printing the scanner can't vouch for.
  - The Added list's "Change printing" is now "Wrong printing?", with the filed printing marked "Added as this".
  - A kept scan photo of a correction says "corrected", with what the scanner said as the guess (`sampleKind`).
- **Reading after a typed name, and for a card read as text.** These go to the same hold and read as a card found by its art, limited to that card's printings, instead of the close-up box at once.
  - The card isn't found again by its art (that's what failed), so it's found by where it is: the detector's outline, which is the card's even when its art isn't known. `pickHeldQuad` takes the largest, nearest the middle; `sameHeldCard` lets it drift in the hand but not jump (under 35% of its diagonal and 40% in size between frames).
  - A frame where it jumps, or where the art is clearly another card (0.82 or more), counts as the card not seen. With no recognizer (text mode), the on-screen guide is where the card is.
  - A pool search over the typed card's own artworks (as the set lock does) isn't used yet.

Now the title is read first whenever the art is under 0.82, or the scanner is locked to sets whose art the index mostly doesn't know and the match isn't one of theirs (`readTitleFirst` in `setlock.ts`, from the Star Trek fix), or two different cards score within 0.03 (`needsTitle`). The upright title is read and fused with the art first; with no clear title, the card's whole text is read one orientation per frame (sideways battles, upside down), as before.
  - Evidence from Bryan's phone (a kept scan photo, 15 Nov 2026, 21:03 UTC; the camera delivered 2160 × 3840): a foil Westvale Abbey // Ormendahl (SOI 281) in a blue box locked as Wei Night Raiders (PTK 89) at 0.658 on two frames, with its title "Westvale Abbey" plainly readable.   - A card just filed by its name stays held (`blockedName`): neither its art, nor a title read naming it, nor the two-frame fallback files it again (`sameCardName`, from the title-first change, now matching on any face so a back face's art counts; `fusedHeld`). **The camera: which lens, how close, and auto-zoom (2026-11-15).** Bryan, on his Pixel 10 Pro: he couldn't choose among its cameras (main, ultrawide, 5× telephoto, front), and the view "isn't zoomed in, even mildly". The scanner consult (same day) added: zoom in until the card fills the view, say "move closer" until the collector letters are big enough, try a still photo when reads stall, and focus on the collector corner. All in the web view; nothing here needs native code.
- **Camera picker** (`cameras.ts`, Scan settings). The cameras' labels are blank until the camera is allowed, so the list is read once it opens. They get plain names:
  - Android's "camera2 0, facing back": the lowest-numbered back camera is "Main", other back cameras are "Back camera N", and the front one is "Front";
  - Safari's lens names: "Back Camera" is "Main", plus "Ultrawide", "Telephoto", "Front", and "Back (switches lens)" for the dual and triple virtual cameras.

  The choice is remembered per device (`yama.scan.camera`: its device id, and its label as a fallback, since ids can change). It's offered only when there's more than one camera. A chosen camera that has gone, or won't open, quietly falls back to the back camera. Without a choice, the main back lens opens (`defaultCamera`): an ultrawide focuses close but sees the card small, and a telephoto can't focus as close as a card is held.
- **What a Pixel lists** [read: Android's logical multi-camera docs; inferred for the Pixel 10 Pro, not seen]. Android hides a phone's separate lenses behind one logical back camera that switches lens itself as it zooms, so Chrome and the app's web view usually list one back camera and one front. Then the picker has only those two, and lens switching is the camera's own zoom's business. A camera whose own zoom goes to 5× or more (`LENS_SWITCH`) gets a note under the picker: zooming that far may switch to the telephoto, which can't focus on a card this close. The + and − buttons and a pinch stop at 4× (`HAND_MAX`, whatever the camera's own top end), and auto-zoom stops at 2.5× (`AUTO_MAX`) [inferred].
- **What the camera delivers** is logged to the console when it opens (`cameraInfo`: the settings and capabilities, width and height, zoom range, focus modes, torch, and whether focus points are supported), and kept in the scanner's state for the diagnostics to come. Scan settings shows one line: "Camera in use: Main, 2160 × 3840, zoom 1× to 10×".
- **Auto-zoom** (`zoomPolicy.ts`). With the camera's own zoom only, never the digital one: a digital zoom only enlarges the picture, so a card filling it is fewer of the frame's pixels.
  - It aims for the card at 60% of the visible view's height. It steps in under 45% and out over 85%, by at most 1.15× at a time, after 3 steady frames and at least 0.7 s apart, and never past 2.5×.
  - A pinch, + or − hands zoom to the person for the rest of the session.
  - The numbers are guesses [inferred]: the view and the hand will tell.
- **"Move closer"** (`closer.ts`). While the printing is read, the chip says "Move closer, so the set code and number can be read" when the collector line's capitals would be under 20 px tall in the full-resolution frame. The estimate is card height × 0.0128 (the collector cut is two lines of its 8% of the card; a capital is about a third of a line's band), so that means a card under about 1,560 px tall. ML Kit asks for characters about 16 px or more [read: ML Kit's text recognition guidance]. On synthetic frames, reads held at a card 700 px tall and failed under 450 [ran, earlier]. The 20 px target is [inferred].
- **Focus.** Continuous focus where the camera offers it, applied as soon as it opens. While the held card is read, focus is pointed once at its collector corner where the browser supports points of interest (`getSupportedConstraints().pointsOfInterest`); elsewhere nothing changes.
- **One still photo when reads stall.** After 5 frames read without settling, the scanner takes one still with `ImageCapture.takePhoto()` where the browser has it (Chrome on Android; the app's web view may not [inferred]), finds the card in it with the art recognizer, and reads it into the same evidence as a frame. Once per attempt; without ImageCapture or the recognizer it does nothing.
- **Switching camera** stops everything reading the old one at once (review of 15 Nov 2026): the scanning loop, hold and read and the close-up each register how to stop (`haltReaders`), and a switch calls them before any result from the old camera can be used. The video is detached, `live` stays off until the new camera delivers a frame, and the attempt in progress starts again with fresh evidence. A first read of the card's collector line still under way when the camera switches (`settle`) is dropped: each switch bumps a camera generation that `settle` checks after every wait, so the printings still load, but nothing read by the old camera chooses one, carries on as evidence or files the card; the held card is read again on the new camera. Zoom and torch changes report whether they reached the camera in use, and are shown only when they did (a 4× meant for a camera with its own zoom can't land on one without). A pinch still queued for the last camera is dropped when another opens; every zoom is clamped to the open camera's range (and 4×) right before it's applied, and the zoom shown is the one the camera reports (`getSettings().zoom`) where it reports one. A camera request is checked for cancellation before it's made, and a stream that arrives after the scanner moved on is stopped. A saved camera whose id has changed is found again by its label (`resolveSaved`) and remembered under its new id; it's forgotten only when neither matches.
- **Constraints one at a time.** Focus, zoom and the torch go through one queue (`serialApplier`), each change merged with whatever was applied last when it runs, so a focus point and a zoom step can't undo each other.
- **The still photo** is given 2 s (`withTimeout`) and runs alongside the frames rather than holding them up; the attempt's time cap ends the attempt whatever the still is doing.
- **Native code**: none needed for this. A native camera (CameraX, with a fixed physical lens, focus metering and full-resolution stills) is still the consult's item 6, only if the web view's numbers fall short; it would need an APK update.

**Scanner diagnostics, and replaying real scans (2026-11-15).** Bryan wanted to see what the scanner saw when it read a printing wrong, and the scanner consult's first item was a replayable record of it. The kept scan photos are 640 px, which puts the collector line at about 7 px, too small to replay a printing read. ARCHITECTURE.md, "Scanner diagnostics", has the data flow and the limits; "Scanner timings and full-resolution crops" has what changed on 20 Nov 2026.
- **Turning it on.** "Scanner diagnostics" in Scan settings, per device, off by default. Records are kept in the account and readable by their owner only; unlike Save scan photos, they're never shared. "Delete them" (shown while it's on) deletes them all, timing records included. Turning it off stops the card in hand at once (its record keeps nothing more and is never sent); a record is sent only while the switch is on and the account that started it is still signed in (it carries that account's request signal, api.ts); its card photo is the card alone, warped upright from its corners, never the frame or photo around it; and an admin's View as can't read them (`view_as.rs`, `HIDDEN`).
- **When a record is kept** (`diagnostics.ts`, `ScanRecord`):
  - the printing was picked from the list;
  - Wrong card, or Wrong printing (before or after filing; after filing it's a second record);
  - hold and read gave up, or the close-up box took over;
  - a title overruled the art.

  A card that went smoothly sends no struggle record, only its timing record (below). The record goes when the card is filed, skipped, called wrong, or the scanner closes.
- **What it holds:**
  - Images:
    - `strip`: the card's bottom edge, corner to corner (card y from 0.86 to 1: both collector corners), warped upright at the card's own resolution in the frame (`stripOf`, at most 2,400 px wide);
    - `symbol` (since 20 Nov 2026): the set symbol's area, the type line's right end (card y 0.50 to 0.63, x 0.70 to 1, `SYMBOL`), warped upright from the same frame at the card's own resolution (`symbolOf`, at most 800 px wide), for measuring a printing model on real captures;
    - `strip-last`: the same from the last frame read while the card was held;
    - `card`: a 640 px photo.
  - JSON:
    - every frame's reads per crop (`first`, `hold`, `still`, `closeup`), with the strip's sharpness and glare, the card's height in pixels, the zoom, and the evidence after it;
    - frames skipped as blurred;
    - each attempt's budget summary;
    - the art's top matches and scores, and the title reads with what they decided (`scan`);
    - the candidate printings (`options`);
    - the camera's name, settings and capabilities, its zoom and torch, and the text reader;
    - what was misread, and how it ended (`final`).
- **Timing records, for every card settled (2026-11-20).** The struggle records keep only cards that struggled, so nothing measured how fast ordinary scans settle, nor the lead rule (`LEAD_FRAMES`) and Yama's art model live. Now, while the switch is on, every card the scanner names sends one small record with no images (`TimingRecord` in `diagnostics.ts`, `server/src/scan_timings.rs`), when the card is done with (filed, skipped, called wrong, or the scanner closes):
  - `ms`: from the card appearing in view (the moment the first frame the detector saw it in was taken, before that frame was read; not counting the card just filed while it's still held) to the card being named, and `frames`, the frames read in that time; a photo or a typed name has no time;
  - `rule`: what named it: `sure` (art at 0.82), `leads` (the lead rule on `LEAD_FRAMES` frames), `twice` (0.62 on `UNTITLED_FRAMES` frames), `title` (a title read), `text` (the card's text), `closeup` (the title close-up) or `typed` (a typed name);
  - `top` and `lead`: the score of the art's top match on the frame that named the card (the in-set one with the set lock) and its lead over the next different card (a title or text read may name another of the art's matches);
  - `printing` and `printing_ms`: how the printing was settled (`only`, `set` for the only one in the locked sets, `collector`, `look` for the look option that settles when sure, `picked`, or `corrected` when the person said the scanner's printing was wrong or filed another), and the time from the card being named to that; none in deck and box scans (`mode` `identity`, against `printing` in the collection);
  - `camera` (the delivered resolution), the art model (`ArtRecognizer.embed`, recorded as the bundle with `+yama-art-…`) and `models`, the option each slot ran (art, look, text, lock; "Models you can switch"), so the lead rule can be compared on and off; `detail.auto` when Keep adding filed it, `card_corrected` after Wrong card (the record stays the first card's, its rule and time; a printing settled for the wrong card is dropped), `printing_rejected` after Wrong printing (the way the rejected printing was settled; it no longer counts as settled, and a pick then counts as `corrected`), and `after_filing` for a Wrong printing after filing (a record of its own).

  The time stops for a pause (Scan settings, the set picker, a hidden tab) and starts again after it; it isn't started by the card just filed while it's still in view.

  "Delete them" leaves nothing on its way to arrive after it (photos, struggle records and timing records alike): on the phone it stops anything not yet sent from being sent and waits for uploads already sent to be answered before it deletes; on the server the deletion records its moment (`scan_clears`) and an upload that began before it is refused in the transaction that would save it; and a folder of images left with no row (an upload dropped mid-way) goes at the next Delete them once it's 10 minutes old.

## The number that matters (2026-11-21)

Bryan, 21 Nov 2026: "when it's reading the bottom left text, I think it's trying to be too perfect. It struggles with the spaces and the little brush stroke thing really throws it off… Even though it sees the number, like the 286, if that's enough to know for a particular card, we're good." And: "It also seems to give up really fast", and the outline "feels a teeny bit laggy".

- **The number alone** (`numberAlone`, `evidence.ts`). The printings to tell apart are the ones sharing the card's art, which nearly always have different numbers. So it settles without the set code when all of these hold. The leader's number is read exactly in its printed form, at least three characters ("0022", "286"; a bare "22" is as likely power and toughness glued together, and a year, 1900 to 2099, the copyright line's), on two frames (`NUMBER_ALONE_FRAMES`). No other candidate's number matches it through lookalikes or one edit away (a dropped or misread digit), nor any printing with that art the set lock left out of the candidates (the card may be one of them). Nothing read names another candidate's set code, even through lookalikes, or its number exactly. And no set code read where it's printed, before the language, contradicts the leader's (a card from a set the candidates leave out, say under the set lock). The leader isn't a Mystery Booster printing, whose line prints another printing's number. Every other threshold (total, margin, posterior) and the twin guards apply as before. Its verdict carries `alone: true` in Scanner diagnostics. One frame is never enough on the number alone, since one frame can be a misread.
- **Spacing** (`tokens`). Besides the runs OCR read, a zero-padded number split by a space on one line ("02 86") is read whole (two plain numbers side by side are more often power and toughness without the slash), and a number with the printing's own rarity letter glued on ("0286M" for a mythic 286) counts as the number; a glued letter that isn't the printing's rarity stays part of it, so a read 139S still proves the 139s promo. Set codes split or glued by OCR ("M 19", "MOMEENJRYAN", the bullet or the brush mark read as a letter) are never proof: built from runs that may be something else (the rarity letter and the number, an artist's name such as "DOMENICO"), they could prove a set code nobody read (Codex reviews of #180). They only hold a number-only settle back when they name another set (`printedSets`); a card whose set code is lost that way settles on its number when that's enough.
- **Waiting** (`HOLD_BUDGET`). Bringing the phone close to the corner can put the art half out of view, where it isn't found as the card; the attempt now stops after 6 s without the card (was 2.5 s), and the close-up box takes over after 12 s (was 8 s). These are guesses as before [inferred].
- **What the sheet shows.** Each printing to pick leads with its number, then its set code and year (pre-2015 cards print no set code). The close-up shows what was read ("Read the number (286), not the set code yet"), not the raw text, which changed with every stray space; the raw text is there with Scanner diagnostics on.
- **The outline.** The corners come from the card finder the art recognizer runs anyway, so the box costs no extra recognition; but it was drawn through React state, re-rendering the whole scanner on every frame. It's now drawn straight onto the SVG. (Later the same day it became corner marks that show before the card is named and glide between the finder's passes: "Outline, focus, frame rate and card backs".)
- **Not measured on real strips.** Kept photos are 640 px (the line is about 7 px), and no diagnostic records with full-resolution strips are kept yet, so the change is covered by `evidence.test.ts` only. With Scanner diagnostics on, records of real scans can be replayed through `eval/real.test.ts`.

## The printing look (2026-11-20)

Most collection scans need the printing among several that share one artwork: 98 of the 120 test photos ("Scanner models of our own", docs/plans/scanner-models.md). The collector line settles it when it can be read; otherwise the person picks, from a list that put the newest printing first. A model that compares the card with each candidate printing's own picture already does better than that order, so it now orders the list. By default it doesn't settle a printing or change when the collector line does: one person's 640 px photos are too little evidence for that, and a later change may add it as evidence once full-resolution scans are measured. An opt-in option settles the sure ones ("Settling by look").

- **The models.** Two release assets of [`scanner-look-20261120`](https://github.com/bryanroscoe/yama/releases/tag/scanner-look-20261120), each 9,292,149 bytes, fp32, ONNX opset 17, pinned in `MODELS` (server/src/scan.rs) and downloaded into the bundle's folder as `look-<sha>.onnx`: `yama-look-v1.onnx` (sha256 `152f52c100f0080c98e0ce7063b6fe9444e0c8654b1142772339af84138b79e7`), the look slot's default `look-v1`, and `yama-look-v0.onnx` (sha256 `58acfa166105c3001445589ec11db98d5ed61f1a70a63dc133dca00fa6bd271d`), the option `look-v0`. Both have the same graph; the weights differ. Same interface as the bundle's `embed.onnx`: `scene` (H × W × 4 RGBA uint8) and `quad` (4 × 2 float32, the card's corners from top-left, clockwise, in scene pixels); outputs `symbol` and `card` (1 × 128, L2-normalised). Inside it warps the card itself (bilinear GridSample): the set symbol's area (card y 0.525 to 0.605, x 0.76 to 0.975) to 160 × 80 and the whole card to 320 × 448, standardises each channel of each, and runs two MobileNetV3-Small networks with GeM pooling and a 128-number head. Trained by Yama in the scanner lab from torchvision's ImageNet MobileNetV3-Small weights, on Scryfall pictures and simulated sleeved phone captures (InfoNCE among each artwork's printings): v0 on 19,475 printings that share art; v1's set-symbol network on the graph's own crops of all 78,063 English paper printings that share an artwork (the real photos' artworks and card names held out; 79.5% on a held-out simulated set), with v0's whole-card network. **Licence:** the models are Yama's ([LICENSE](../LICENSE)); their base weights are torchvision's, BSD-3-Clause (Copyright (c) Soumith Chintala 2016); the licence's full text, which goes with the models, is in [COPYRIGHT](../COPYRIGHT) and the release notes. The training scripts and their guide are in `scanner-lab/` (`look_*.py`, [LOOK.md](../scanner-lab/LOOK.md)). A new model is a new release asset with the same interface, added as an option (and made the default once measured better).
- **The card's side.** Once the look model has loaded, the art worker keeps the last 3 recognizer frames with a card in them (the same 640 px frame and quad the art match used, copied into buffers it reuses, so no garbage at camera rate), under each artwork the frame matched at 0.5 or more and within 0.15 of its best match anywhere and in the set lock's pool, whatever the bundle says about its printings (an artwork reprinted since the bundle counts one there). So a card settled on a match that wasn't the best (a title read, Not this card) has its frames too (Codex review of #172), while a card merely on another's list doesn't. A frame with no card, or one led by another card, clears them, and so does a photo (a reset of the recognizer, as after filing, doesn't): the next card may be another copy of the same art in another printing, while a card filed and then said to be the wrong printing is still in view. When a card is found whose art several printings share and the art's list doesn't settle it (`settle`, ScannerView.tsx), its frames are handed to the look's own worker (`look.worker.ts`, a second WASM instance, so the comparing never holds up the camera's frames in the art worker), embedded, and averaged (each part renormalised; look.ts, `meanEmbedding`). Frames from the hold-and-read loop count too.
- **The printings' side.** Each printing's Scryfall `normal` picture (the back's for a back face) is fetched from cards.scryfall.io (which sends `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, so a worker can read its pixels), decoded with `createImageBitmap` onto an `OffscreenCanvas`, and run with its own corners as the quad, three at a time. The fetch says `no-store`: the service worker serves a picture it already keeps but doesn't add these to the offline image cache (`public/sw.js`), which a big scan would otherwise churn. Each printing's embedding is kept per model file in memory (the last 1,500) and in a Cache API of its own (`yama-look-v1`, `/look-cache/<model>/<picture URL>`, about 1 KB each, the newest 4,000, trimmed when the look loads and every 100 added; another model's are dropped once, when the model changes). A browser without `OffscreenCanvas` or `createImageBitmap` in workers (iOS before 16.4) doesn't load the look, and Scan settings says so; a failed load ends the look's worker.
- **Score and order.** 0.8 × the symbol's cosine + 0.2 × the whole card's (look.ts, `lookScore`). The scores are kept apart from the found card's state, whose changes would restart the hold-and-read loop (review of #172). Once the look has compared every printing it could, the list is reordered, once: the printings a collector-line read put first stay first (`Choice.readFirst`), then the rest by look score, best first (`lookOrder`), and the best is marked "Looks closest" (a quiet ring and those words; `lookBest`). Nothing moves while scores come in, a list touched before the look finished keeps its order, and a look that was stopped orders nothing. The look stops when another card is found, the printing is chosen (settled or tapped), or the card is done with; Wrong printing compares the list again from the card's frames if it's still in view, or reuses the look that finished for the view the card was found in (a Scan anyway copy shares its first copy's), and the printing said to be wrong goes last and is never the guess. Reached by keyboard, as by touch, the list keeps its order. Scanners where only the card matters (deck checks, deck and box scans) don't load it.
- **Recorded.** A Scanner diagnostics record of the card has `look`, written as the scores come (so a record sent before the look ends has what it had, with `stopped`): the model, each option's score (null if not scored), and once it ends how many frames were averaged and how long that took, how many came from the cache, and the time spent fetching, decoding and embedding the rest; `models.look` names the option that ran.
- **Measured [ran, 20 Nov 2026].** On the 98 real sleeved photos (one tester's 640 px kept scan photos with the phone's own quads; people's data, outside the repository) of artworks several printings share: the right printing scores highest **86.7%** with v1 (85 of 98) and 57.1% with v0 (56 of 98), in the lab's Python onnxruntime and the same counts when replayed in Node through onnxruntime-web 1.31 (WASM, one thread) with the app's own `look.ts` code (`eval/look.test.ts`). With v1 the right printing or a strict visual twin of it (the same picture and the same set-symbol area, which only the collector number separates; 30 of the 98 have one) scores highest 95.9% (the lab's figure). The list's old order put the right one first 40.8%; chance is 24.7%; CollectorVision, run privately as a benchmark, 75.5% and 84.7%. Parity on two synthetic scenes (no card images in the repository; the expected outputs, `eval/look-parity-v0.json` and `-v1.json`, one per model picked by its SHA-256, come from `scanner-lab/look_parity.py` with Python's onnxruntime 1.32, which remakes them byte for byte): largest difference 3.3e-6 for each. With the photos decoded the same way, scores agree to 1.1e-4; decoded by jpeg-js instead of OpenCV they differ by up to 0.03, with the same ranking.
- **Cost [ran, M3 Pro, Node, one WASM thread].** About 24 to 83 ms to embed one printing's picture or one camera frame, either model (the higher with the machine busy; Python's multithreaded CPU run takes about 13 ms), so a typical group of 4 printings with 3 frames is under a second here, and slower on a phone, in the look's own worker. A `normal` picture is 107 to 156 KB (about 0.5 s each on a cold connection from this Mac; browsers reuse the connection). The model is a 9.3 MB download once per device, cached; not converted to fp16. Not measured: a phone's speed, and the decode in a browser.

## Settling by look (2026-11-20, opt-in)

Bryan (20 Nov 2026): "We can get more… get all of these merged and move it along as much as you can", and "we want to be able to try a lot of things here and switch between models when things break". The lab found a rule an app can run that would have settled about half the shared-art photos with no wrong ones (docs/plans/scanner-models.md, "The printing look"), so it's built as an option to try, not as the default.

- **The option.** `look-v1-sure` in `MODELS` (server/src/scan.rs), "Yama's look model v1, settles when sure": v1's own pinned file (`look-152f52c100f0.onnx`), so nothing more to download and the same embedding cache, and switching between it and v1 changes nothing in the look's worker (`ArtRecognizer.useLook`). Not the default. The server lists it only to builds that run the rule (`GET /api/scan/bundle?can=settle`; it marks it `settles`): a build from before it would run it as plain v1 and record it as this. That still happens on an older build opened offline from bundle info this one kept on the device (an Android app rolled back), which is rare and leaves records naming the option that didn't run the rule. `YAMA_SCAN_WITHDRAW=look-v1-sure` withdraws it like any option, and a device that chose it runs the look's default meanwhile; withdrawing `look-v1` alone leaves it on offer (the same weights), so withdraw both if v1's weights are at fault.
- **The rule** (look.ts, `lookSettles`; `LOOK_SETTLE_LEAD` 0.10). When the look has finished and scored every printing in the list (the pool it orders: a printing whose picture couldn't be fetched or scored, or a look that stopped, means no settle), and the closest leads the next by 0.10 or more, the closest is settled. It's the raw lead over the next printing, visual twins included, that `scanner-lab/look_eval.py`'s first table measures: at 0.10 v1 settled 45 of the 98 photos alone, none wrong; at 0.08, 54 with one wrong. A strict twin scores close to its twin, so twins stay with the collector line.
  - **Never against the collector line.** Not when anything it read for this card points to another printing: another printing's set code or number read exactly as printed where the look's printing's differs, or evidence settled on another (evidence.ts, `pointsAway`, over every evidence gathered for the card: the text reader's line, the first read, hold and read, the close-up). Lookalike-only reads point nowhere. So the rule waits while the first read of the collector line is under way, and is weighed once, when the look has finished and that read is done.
  - **The collector line first.** A printing the collector line settled stays its (`how: "collector"`): it can settle before the look finishes, and an agreeing read changes nothing.
  - **Once settled**, it's `how: "look"`, with `sure` and `settledId` as a collector-line settle has: the list closes, Keep adding files it by itself, "Wrong printing" is under More (and beside Undo once filed), and the collector line stops reading the card (the hold-and-read loop and any close-up of the collector line end). The "?" says it looks most like that printing, well ahead of the others.
  - **Wrong printing** works as for a collector-line settle: the list reopens in the look's order with the printing said to be wrong marked "Read as this" and last, the collector line reads again, and a tap files the right one. The look never settles that card again.
  - Not weighed while Wrong card is open or the scanner is paused (it is once that ends), and never in scanners where only the card matters (deck checks, deck and box scans), which run no look at all. Not settled once the person has touched the list before the look finished (they're choosing; review of #178).
  - **The option running now decides** (Codex review of #178): a switch in Scan settings while a look runs applies to that card. Only an option that settles, on the same model file the look ran on, settles it; a switch to plain v1 settles nothing.
- **Recorded.** The rule is weighed under every look option, so the default's scans say what it would have done. A Scanner diagnostics record has `lookSettle` (`settled`, the option, the printing's id, set and number, and the lead; or why it didn't: `unscored`, `lead`, `read-other`, `option` for an option that doesn't settle, `touched`), its `look` note has the lead whenever every printing was scored, and `final.how` and the `misread` note say `look`. The timing record's `printing` is `look` (the server accepts it beside `only`, `set`, `collector`, `picked` and `corrected`), and `detail` keeps `look_lead` (the closest printing's lead over the next, rounded, whenever every printing was scored), `look_option` (the look option whose rule was weighed: a switch since the card was named shows here, while `models` keeps the choice when it was named), `look_sure` (what the rule said, on the raw lead and the collector line's reads) and `look_top_filed` (whether the look's closest is the printing filed first). A Wrong printing after a look settle is counted as `printing_rejected: "look"`, before filing or after. `get_scan_timings` adds `looks`: per look option (`look_option`, else `models.look`; the collection's records only), its cards, the time to settle the printing overall and by way, how often each way was then called wrong, `sure_leads` (cards the rule would have settled, by `look_sure`) and `sure_leads_filed_otherwise` (of those, filed as another printing than the look's closest at first, or changed by Wrong printing after filing). So the option's time to a printing and its corrections compare with the default's.
- **What it rests on.** One tester's 98 sleeved photos at 640 px, through the lab's Python onnxruntime (the app's own code gives the same rankings: "The printing look", "Measured"). 0 wrong of 45 is still consistent with about one in 15 wrong (the 95% upper bound), and the threshold was chosen on the same photos. It becomes the default only once Scanner diagnostics' timing records from real scans support it: `sure_leads_filed_otherwise` against `sure_leads`, under the option and under the default.
- **Tests.** `look.test.ts` (the lead, every printing scored, twins, a read pointing elsewhere), `evidence.test.ts` (`pointsAway`), `ScannerView.lookSettle.test.tsx` (settled and filed by Keep adding, Wrong printing after it, twins and an unscored printing left to the list, a conflicting or agreeing first read, a collector settle kept as the collector line's, a touched list, a switch either way while the look runs, the default only ordering, deck scans running no look, and the timing records), `artClient.look.test.ts` (switching between options on one file), server/src/scan.rs and scan_timings.rs (the option and the records).

## Models you can switch (2026-11-20)

Bryan (20 Nov 2026): "We should have the ability to switch scanners on the fly if need be by the way we want to be able to try a lot of things here and switch between models when things break." So each part of the scanner that has a model, or a rule that could have been a model, is a **slot** with **options**, listed by the server and chosen per device.

| Slot | Options (id) | Default | Where it runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Card finder (`detector`) | ManaVault's card finder (`bundle-detector`, the bundle's `detector.onnx`), Yama's card finder, 20 Nov (`yama-corners-20261120`; "Speed") | `bundle-detector` | the art worker: where the card's corners are |
| Art match (`art`) | ManaVault's art model (`bundle`, the bundle's `embed.onnx`), Yama's art model, 18 Nov (`yama-art-20261118`), the same with two cuts first (`yama-art-20261118-cascade`; "Speed") | `yama-art-20261118` | the art worker: the query side of the art search |
| Printing look (`look`) | Off (`off`), Yama's look model v0 (`look-v0`), v1 (`look-v1`), v1 that settles when sure (`look-v1-sure`, opt-in; "Settling by look") | `look-v1` | its own worker (`look.worker.ts`), ordering the printing list ("The printing look") |
| Text reader (`text`) | Automatic (`auto`), Google ML Kit (`mlkit`, the Android app only), PaddleOCR (`paddle`), Tesseract (`tesseract`) | `auto` | `ocrEngine.ts` |
| Settling by art (`lock`) | Art that leads (`lead`: 0.82, or 0.65 and 0.10 ahead on two frames), Sure scores only (`sure`: 0.82, or 0.62 on two frames once the title couldn't be read; the rule before 18 Nov) | `lead` | `fuse.ts`, the scanning loop |

- **The registry** is `MODELS` in `server/src/scan.rs`: each option has its slot, id, label, a sentence on what it is, its licence, where its model comes from (the app's own code, one of the bundle's files, or a file of ours pinned by URL, size and SHA-256), the bundle recogniser it fits (Yama's art model fits only galleries from checkpoint `eb655846…`), whether it's the slot's default, and whether it's the slot's **base** (what the slot does with nothing of ours: `bundle-detector`, `bundle`, `off`, `auto`, `sure`), which can't be withdrawn, so a slot always has an option. A file of ours is downloaded once into the bundle's folder as `<prefix>-<first 12 of the sum>.onnx` (never `embed.onnx`), checked as the bundle's files are, and served from there immutable; each downloads in the background on its own (a failure is retried after ten minutes and only means that option isn't on offer meanwhile), and a download that finishes drops the files of pins no longer listed, keeping the newest other one of each kind for a phone that read the bundle info just before. A test checks every slot has one base and at most one default, ids are unique and plain, and every file is pinned whole.
- **`GET /api/scan/bundle`** lists, under `models`, each slot's options on offer (id, label, note, licence, file and size) and its default. On offer means: not withdrawn, fitting the bundle (its manifest's `recogniser.sha256`), and with its file downloaded; with no bundle yet, only the slots that need nothing downloaded (text, lock, the look's Off). The default is the slot's default if it's on offer, else its base. `files.embed` stays the art slot's default, for web builds from before this.
- **Withdrawing one.** `YAMA_SCAN_WITHDRAW=look-v1,paddle` (option ids, comma-separated) in `yama.env` on the server, then recreate the container (`docker compose up -d` in its directory): no new code. A withdrawn option isn't listed or downloaded, so every phone that chose it runs its slot's default from its next scanner open, and goes back to it once it's listed again (the choice stays on the device). Withdrawing a text reader also takes it out of Automatic's order; Tesseract stays the last resort whatever is withdrawn, as it needs nothing but the app. `YAMA_SCAN_QUERY_MODEL=0` still works, and now withdraws every art model of ours. A base option can't be withdrawn. When a slot's default is withdrawn or not downloaded yet, the default before it stands in if it's on offer (`STANDS_IN`: `look-v0` for `look-v1`), else the base. Withdrawing an id withdraws that option only: `yama-art-20261118-cascade` has `yama-art-20261118`'s weights, and `look-v1-sure` has `look-v1`'s, so withdraw both if those weights are at fault. Options that share a file (`look-v1-sure` runs `look-v1`'s) download it once.
- **On the phone** (`models.ts`, `ModelsSetting.tsx`): Scan settings has a "Models" part listing each slot with more than one option this device can run, as "Default (its name)" or a named option, with the option's note and what runs now ("Running Yama's art model, 18 Nov."). The choice is kept per device (`yama.scan.models`) and applies at once: another art model or card finder is loaded into the running art worker and swapped in once it's compiled and warmed (`ArtRecognizer.useArt` and `useDetector`, `Recognizer.setEmbed` and `setDetector`; a new scanner starts with the ones chosen, and falls back to the defaults if they don't load; frames meanwhile use the one before, switches run one after another so the last one asked for wins, and one that fails to load leaves the one before running and says so); another text reader stops the one running and starts with the next read, keeping the card names loaded; the lock rule applies from the next frame. Nothing reloads and the session (the cards added, the set lock, the camera) stays. A choice the server doesn't offer now (withdrawn, not downloaded yet, or a reader this device hasn't got, such as ML Kit in a browser) runs the default and says so; a scanner opened offline whose chosen art model isn't cached starts with the bundle info's own. The phone runs any card finder, art or look option that has a file (a card finder or art model with the bundle graph's interface, every look model look.ts's; the server lists one that needs more, such as the cascade's `cuts` or the rule of the look that settles when sure (`settle`), only to apps that say they can: `GET /api/scan/bundle?can=cuts,settle`, and an option with `needs` can never be a default, nor `files.embed`) but only text readers and lock rules this build has code for, so a newer server can add them without breaking older apps; builds from before the look stage (20 Nov 2026) offer the look's Off only. When a choice made here isn't on offer, its slot is shown with it marked "not on offer now" even if the default is the only option left. The art worker's cache keeps every file the server lists in a slot, so switching back needs no download.
- **Records.** Every kept scan photo and Scanner diagnostics record names each slot's choice when it was made, as `models` (`{"detector": "bundle-detector", "art": "yama-art-20261118", "look": "off", "text": "auto", "lock": "lead"}`; `"none"` for the card finder and art while the scanner reads text only), kept in a `models` column next to `bundle` (`scan_samples`, `scan_diagnostics`; migration 0101), returned by the owner's diagnostics routes and MCP tools, and in `scan-samples-export`'s manifest. The server keeps known slots with plain ids only, and an id it doesn't know as said (a tab open across a change). `bundle` still names the art model by its file, as before.
- **Adding a model** (a CollectorVision printing model once its licence is granted, https://github.com/HanClinto/CollectorVision/issues/63; models made to match CardVision, app.mtgscan.org): publish the file as a release asset of this repository (not marked latest), add a `ModelOption` to `MODELS` with all of URL, size and SHA-256, its licence and, for an art query model, the recogniser it was trained against, and run the recognizer eval with `SCANNER_EMBED` (art) or the look's parity test (look). It's offered once the server has downloaded it; make it the default only once it's measured better.

## Outline, focus, frame rate and card backs (2026-11-21)

Bryan (21 Nov 2026), after trying CardVision's Android beta: "It's by far the best one I've seen and it's the one that we should use as our gold standard." The plan (docs/plans/scanner-match-cardvision.md) lists what it does; these are the parts of its stage 1 that need no native code and are on for everyone. Ideas only: none of CardVision's code, models or data is used.

- **The outline** (`outline.ts`, `CardOutline.tsx`; the plan's item 1). The card finder's corners used to reach the screen with the frame's whole result, after the art was matched. Now the recognizer says where the finder placed the card as soon as it has (`Recognizer.identify`'s `onSeen`, the art worker's `seen` message, `ArtRecognizer.identify`'s second argument), sooner by the art match's time, tens of milliseconds a frame ("Speed"), and the outline is drawn from that. Each card is a track in `OutlineTracker`: it eases towards the corners last reported (closing 63% of the gap every 70 ms), fades in over 120 ms and out over 220 ms, and is stepped by `requestAnimationFrame`, so it moves at the screen's rate though the finder answers a few times a second. `CardOutline` writes the SVG's paths directly, with no React render per frame, and stops asking for frames when nothing is drawn. The shape is four corner marks, each a curve through the corner (a card's corner radius, a twentieth of its short side) with two short arms, in white over a darker line so it shows on a white-bordered card; `outlinePath(quad, "whole")` gives the whole rounded outline instead. The tracker takes up to four cards a pass (the web finder gives one; the native finder will give more), keeps each card its own outline by nearness, and turns a quad whose "top" the finder flipped so the outline doesn't spin. With `prefers-reduced-motion` it neither eases nor fades. It is an overlay: the camera picture is never filtered or faded, and nothing in it feeds back into a verdict.
- **The focus pin** (`cameraFocus.ts`, `camera.tsx`; item 14). Where the camera's track offers a `manual` focus mode, a `focusDistance` range and an automatic mode to go back to (Chrome on Android; not Safari, not desktop webcams as a rule), the camera view has a focus button under zoom. Off is continuous autofocus, as before. On pins the lens and shows a slider from the camera's nearest focus to one metre, even in the ratio of distances so the close end has as much room as the far end; it starts where it was last pinned, else where the camera is focused now. The pin and its distance are kept on the device (`yama.scan.focus`) and go with the next camera opened, within that camera's range. While pinned, the scanner's own tap-to-focus on the collector line is left alone. The pin is sent with the flashlight and zoom in one `applyConstraints`, as those are (`serialApplier`).
- **60 frames a second** (`frameRate.ts`; item 14). After the camera opens, if its capabilities say 60 and it's delivering less, the scanner asks for `frameRate` 60 (at least 50) with the width and height it's delivering now as the least it will take, so a camera that can only do 60 on a smaller picture refuses and stays as it is; if the picture came back smaller all the same, the rate is given back. A deck or box scan's 1080p stream is the likely place for it; the collection's 4K stream rarely has it. Scan settings shows "Ask the camera for 60 frames a second" where the camera offers it (on by default, per device, `yama.scan.fps60`), since a shorter exposure is grainier in a dim room.
- **The frame rate delivered** is measured from the video's own presented frames (`requestVideoFrameCallback`, over the last two seconds; `FrameRateMeter`), because a browser can accept a rate and deliver another. Scan settings says it ("Camera in use: Main, 1080 × 1920, 60 frames a second"), a struggle record's `camera` carries it as `fps` beside the setting (`frameRate`, `maxFrameRate`) and `focusPinned`, and a timing record carries `detail.fps` and `detail.focus_pinned`. No server change: both are free-form JSON there.
- **Card backs** (`cardBack.ts`; item 18). A standard back is a flat orange-brown oval in a darker olive-brown frame, with nothing bluer than the oval but the logo and the Deckmaster box. `backSigns` samples a 24 × 24 grid inside the card's quad on the frame the finder already looked at and measures five things, each the same whichever corner the finder calls the top: the share of the middle that is orange-brown, the share of the middle within a quarter of its median brightness (the oval is flat; artwork isn't), the share of the card whose weakest channel is blue, whether all four corner blocks are warm and how bright they are against the middle, and the share of the card plainly bluer than the middle. The thresholds (`BACK`) are numbers in code. They were set [ran] against Scryfall's scan of the back, fetched at build time and not kept: it passes at 672 to 112 px wide and at every turn of the quad, and 358 of 432 variants of it pass (red and blue gains 0.75 to 1.6, half to one and a half times the brightness, sensor noise to σ 16, a grey lift; the ones that fail are dim and washed out together). Against 883 scans of card fronts (gold, old brown artifacts, red, green, lands, white, colourless and full art), each under five colour casts, none passes [ran]; before the teal share and the frame's lower bound were added, 8 old brown lands and one other passed under a strong warm cast. The repository holds no card image: the tests draw a back and fronts from flat shapes in the real proportions (`eval/syntheticCards.ts`). A back is not a blank surface to the recognizer: the real one scores 5.2 to 12.4 on `cardDetail` at those sizes, over `MIN_DETAIL` (4). The scanner says "Card back, turn the card over" only when the card looks like a back **and** the art's best match scores under 0.62 (`saysCardBack`, `LOCK_TWICE`), so a front the art knows fairly well is never called a back; it then reads no title and asks for no close-up for that frame. A back in an opaque sleeve is a plain surface and stays "no card". Not built: the plan's item 20 (reading upside down only when upright is doubtful), which is the card finder's own four turns and belongs with the native finder.

Checked [ran]: unit tests for each part, and the outline, the focus slider and the message drawn over a still picture in Chrome at phone size. Not checked: a phone camera. Whether Chrome on Android keeps 60 frames a second after a later flashlight, zoom or focus change is not known, which is what the measured rate in the records is for.

## Set lock, Skip, flashlight and zoom, scan photos (2026-11-06)

Bryan (2026-11-06), after trying Convoke's webcam scanner: the scanner should be "more consistent, less finicky, and a little bit more user-friendly"; the close-up step for the printing "was just not good". Convoke publishes nothing about its recognition beyond marketing (100,000+ labelled real game frames, retrained from players' reports); its model is paid and closed (DeckCheck licenses it). So we build on what we have, and start collecting our own labelled photos.

- **Set lock** (`setlock.ts`, `SetLockPicker.tsx`). The chip at the top of the camera says "Any set" or "Only ECC". Pick up to four sets (search by name or code, newest first; `GET /api/scan/sets`), kept per device (`yama.scan.sets`). The server sends each set's printings with their artworks (`GET /api/scan/set?code=`). The recognizer then also searches just those artworks each frame (`Recognition.pool`, below). The scanner goes on the best in-set match while it scores within 0.06 of the best match anywhere (`judgeLocked`): the right card scores the same in both, since the pool is part of the gallery, and a card that isn't in the set scores well above any lookalike in it. The printing is then chosen among the locked sets' printings with that art. Usually that's one, so it's filed with no corner to read ("It's the only printing of it in …"), and Keep adding adds it by itself. A card whose printings (of that art) aren't in the locked sets shows "X isn't in …. Is it a card from another set?". Yes settles it as usual, ignoring the lock; Wrong card offers the alternatives. A typed name never asks. When the art isn't known (a set newer than the bundle), the title is read against the locked sets' card names only, which is far easier than against all 35,000. After three cards in a row from one set, the scanner offers to lock to it.
- **Searching part of the gallery** (`gallery.ts`). The bundle's search graph is `score_i = gallery_i · embeddings[frames_i] − penalties_i`, then top 5. `readGallery` reads those three tensors out of `search.onnx` (a small protobuf reader) and scores any subset the same way in plain JavaScript. Checked against the graph on the pinned bundle with random embeddings: the same top 5 and the same scores to four decimals, about 20 ms for all 51,843 artworks on an M3 Pro [ran]. Unit test: `setlock.test.ts`. If a future bundle's graph changes shape, the lock falls back to the top 5's in-set matches.
- **Skip and Wrong card.** Skip goes to the next card and saves nothing: for checking a card's value. "Not this" used to do the same thing, and Bryan used it to skip. Wrong card opens the other cards the art might be, a name box and "Scan it again". While the printing isn't settled, the card shows the range of its English printings' market prices ("$0.25 to $3.10, by printing").
- **Flashlight and zoom** (`camera.tsx`). The flashlight shows where the browser exposes the torch (Chrome on Android; not Safari). Zoom (+ and −, 1× to 3×, or to 4× with the camera's own zoom) uses the camera's zoom where the browser exposes it. Otherwise it's digital: the view is enlarged, and the recognizer is given the middle of the frame at more of its pixels, which helps a small or far card.
- **Collector line reads** (`readCollectorLines`). Instead of one cut of the corner, up to three: at the edges the snapper found, at the detector's own corners, and a wider cut down to the card's bottom edge. The first that proves the printing wins.
- **Full-resolution crops with each scan photo (2026-11-20).** Bryan, 20 Nov 2026: "We can get more." The kept photo is 640 px, so the set symbol is about 20 px and the collector line about 7 px; a printing model, which tells apart printings that share one artwork by their set symbol, needs labelled full-resolution crops. So each photo kept also keeps two crops of the card, cut once when the photo is kept (never per frame) from the frame the scanner read it in, warped upright from the card's corners at the card's own resolution there (never enlarged; `cropsOf` in `samples.ts`): `symbol`, the set symbol's area (card y 0.50 to 0.63, x 0.70 to 1, at most 800 px wide), and `strip`, the collector strip (`stripOf`'s cut, at most 2,400 px wide), JPEG at quality 0.85. Only the card, never the frame around it. A crop that can't be made, or is over 1 MB, is left out and the photo still goes. They're files on the server (`scan-samples/<owner>/<id>/<name>.jpg`, out of the main database; written in a task of its own so a client gone mid-upload can't leave them half done, and a folder left with no row by a stopped server goes at the next Delete them), listed in the photo's row (`crops`, `crop_bytes`, migration 0102) and kept, shared and deleted exactly as their photo: the admin reads a shared photo's crops at `/api/admin/scan-samples/{id}/crops/{name}` and on the Scan photos page, and `yama-server scan-samples-export` writes them as `<id>.symbol.jpg` and `<id>.strip.jpg` with each one's file, width, height and bytes in the photo's `crops` in `manifest.jsonl`. The frame is the one the card was found in, cut at up to 2,400 px on its long side (1,200 in deck and box scans, which read only the name; 1,500 in text-only mode, without the art recognizer, where a sharper cut would mean copying the full frame on every frame read), which is the camera's own resolution for a card up to about half the height of a 2160 × 3840 frame (a larger card is cut a little smaller). Storage [inferred]: on the 125 test photos (Pixel 10 Pro, 2160 × 3840) the photo is a median 57 kB; at its own bit rate a card about 1,100 to 1,500 px wide in the frame gives a symbol crop of roughly 25 to 45 kB and a strip of 90 to 170 kB, so about 115 to 215 kB more per photo, two to four times the photo. The caps now count them: the newest 2,000 photos, no more than 100 MB of photos (in the database, as before) and no more than 400 MB of photos and crops together. Measure the real sizes from the first export.
- **Scan photos** (`samples.ts`, `server/src/scan_samples.rs`, migration 0068). On unless "Save scan photos" is turned off, per device (it was off by default until 2026-11-15, when Bryan asked for it on: the kept photos are the evidence the misread and diagnostics work needs; a device that had turned it off keeps it off). Since the same day the photos are shared with Yama's developer (Bryan: "I want them to send it to me"): the switch, What's new and the privacy page say so, the scanner sends `shared: true`, and an admin reads them on the admin area's Scan photos page, through `GET /api/admin/scan-samples` (paged, `?owner=&since=&before=`) and `/api/admin/scan-samples/{id}/image`, or exports them on the server with `yama-server scan-samples-export DIR [--since DATE] [--owner USER_ID]` (JPEGs and `manifest.jsonl` with user id, date, kind, label and guess). Only the card photo is shared, never a full frame. Photos saved before (marked 0 by migration 0093; the switch had promised they were shown to no one) are never shown to an admin. Then each card added (or corrected, or marked wrong and scanned again) keeps the part of the frame around it, at most 640 px as a JPEG of about 30 to 60 kB, with its corners, what the scanner said and how sure it was, what the person said, the camera's resolution and the bundle version. Private to the account, capped at its newest 2,000 and 100 MB (and since 20 Nov 2026 400 MB with their crops), and limited to 600 uploads an hour. Deleted from the scanner ("Delete them") or with the account. Nothing is sent anywhere else. This is the labelled real-photo set that "Left for a real phone test" asks for: an eval set first, then fine-tuning data if we train our own model.

Not measured yet: none of this has run on a phone camera. The lock's 0.06 margin and the thresholds still need the real photos the switch now collects.

## Browser OCR: PP-OCRv5 (2026-11-13)

Bryan (2026-11-13): "Tesseract.js was so-so but we need something better... it'd be great if we could do something even better just from the browser. I know other apps can." Decision: the browser's OCR is now PaddleOCR's PP-OCRv5 English recogniser with the PP-OCRv3 text detector, run by onnxruntime-web (already loaded for the art recognizer) in its own worker. Tesseract stays as the fallback: when the build has no models, when they fail to load, or when the worker dies or fails three reads in a row. The Android app's ML Kit is chosen in the same place (`ocrEngine.ts`), ahead of both; in the app without it, PaddleOCR reads from the models bundled in the APK.

How it reads (`paddle.ts`): the detector (2.4 MB) finds the words in the crop as slightly turned boxes; boxes on one line are merged and the recogniser (7.8 MB) reads the line in one run, straightened and scaled to 48 px high, with greedy CTC decoding. A whitelist (the collector line's uppercase set) masks the classes that may win, like Tesseract's. A title read keeps the line with the most text; a collector block keeps its lowest six lines, top to bottom. A line's direction comes from its longest words (a short word's is mostly noise). When the detector finds nothing, the crop is split into lines by rows of ink. Confidence is the mean probability of the kept characters, on Tesseract's 0 to 100 scale. Only the title close-up reads it (to decide whether to try another crop); the printing is settled by the catalog lookup and the title check, as before.

Serving: `web/scripts/paddle-models.mjs` pins the three files (recogniser, its dictionary, detector) to one commit of `monkt/paddleocr-onnx` on Hugging Face with their sizes and SHA-256 sums. The build (`copy-ocr.mjs`) downloads them once into `node_modules/.cache/yama-ocr` (or `YAMA_OCR_CACHE`), checks them, and copies them to `/ocr/paddle-<checksums>/` with a NOTICE, so a new model gets a new address (served immutable; a missing path under `/ocr/` is a 404, never the app page). A build fails if they don't match. If they can't be fetched, a CI build fails (so a pull request finds out), while the deploy's Docker build and `npm run dev` go on without them, with a warning in the build log, and that build's scanner reads with Tesseract: a Hugging Face outage doesn't block a release. Each deploy's build fetches them again (10.3 MB; the Docker build has no cache for them). They are fetched only when the scanner first reads text, and the service worker keeps them like the rest of `/ocr/`. The worker shares the art recognizer's ONNX Runtime WASM file.

What it changed: it reads the bullet, the artist's name and small type almost exactly ("C 0145 WOC•EN DAN SCOTT"), so the set code and number come through in the rough, far and skewed captures where Tesseract read noise. Against Tesseract on the refined quad it gained 20 cards and lost one (the handheld battle, whose title runs sideways). Wrong answers stayed below the 0.8 confidence line in every run. Reading titles through the detector too (rather than the row split) took the whole-card score from 95% to 97% and upside-down scans from 88% to 100%, for about 45 ms more per frame. In a title close-up, a read now has to reach confidence 50 before the scanner stops trying crops: a text band found on the card's frame read as three unsure letters (38) and hid the real title (93 and up); Tesseract's close-up titles are unchanged by this (20 of 26).

What it costs: a collector read takes about twice Tesseract's time on one WASM thread, nearly all of it the recogniser (about 50 ms per line; the collector crop holds two or three lines, the artist's name included) and 43 ms for the detector. On a phone that is likely 300 to 600 ms per collector read [inferred: the 2 to 4 times laptop rule above]. The first text read in a scanner session also waits for the 10.3 MB of models (at most 90 s, then Tesseract); on a slow connection that is a long first wait, once per device, since the service worker keeps them. Reading with Tesseract while the models download would hide it, at the cost of downloading both. Ways to cut the read time, not yet tried: int8 quantisation of the recogniser (smaller and faster in WASM, needs its own accuracy run), WebGPU where the browser has it, and cropping the artist's name off the collector line before reading.

Also considered: Chrome's `TextDetector` (Shape Detection API) isn't exposed in desktop Chrome 157 without a flag (`"TextDetector" in self` is false; `BarcodeDetector` is there) [ran], so it can't be the browser's engine; Android Chrome wasn't checked. PP-OCRv5's own server detector is 84 MB; the v3 mobile detector works fine with the v5 recogniser on these crops.

## Summary

- **Recommendation: a hybrid.** Use an art-embedding lookup to say *which card* it is, and OCR of the bottom-left collector line to say *which printing*. Build it in two steps:
  1. **Now (built):** OCR only. The collector line (`R 0214` over `MOM • EN`) is looked up with the existing `GET /api/printings/lookup`. The title is read too: it confirms the lookup, and it is the fallback for cards that print no set code. About 6 MB of downloads, all Apache-2.0 or MIT, no third-party model. In simulated captures it gets **82% of cards right, with 0 confident wrong answers** [ran]. It is weak on 1993-2003 frames and on dark, blurred, glary shots.
  2. **Next:** add the MPL-2.0 ManaVault/oracle embedding bundle as the first stage. On the same simulated captures it got **96% top-1 on card identity in every condition, including the rough ones, in about 108 ms** [ran]. It does not tell reprints with the same art apart, and it misses sideways cards (battles). OCR covers both gaps.
- **Card position:** a fixed on-screen card guide, then a small edge finder (no OpenCV) that snaps the guide to the card's real edges. With the snap, corner error drops from 6.5% to 0.6% of the card width in handheld shots, and OCR accuracy rises from 78% to 82% overall [ran]. It can't find a black-bordered card on a black mat; the guide is used as is then.
- **CollectorVision stays out.** Its code and its model weights are both AGPL-3.0 (the Hugging Face model card says `license: agpl-3.0`) [read]. Its catalog is MIT, but the catalog is only useful with the AGPL model.
- **Not yet tested on a phone.** Everything ran in Node and desktop Chromium on synthetic captures made from Scryfall scans. See "Left for a real phone test".

## The art recognizer (built 2026-10-28)

```
camera frame, short side 640 ─▶ worker: detector on the middle 640 px square (then the whole frame if nothing)
      ─▶ detector again on a window where the card fills 60% ─▶ card quad, top first, any rotation
      ─▶ embed (the graph warps the card itself) ─▶ search: top 5 of 52,350 illustrations
      ─▶ lock at score ≥ 0.82, or ≥ 0.62 on two agreeing frames
      ─▶ GET /api/scan/printings?art=<illustration id>: the printings that share the art
      ─▶ one English printing: done. Several: OCR the collector line (full-resolution crop at the quad)
         and pick among them (pickPrinting). Still unsure: the person picks from thumbnails.
art score < 0.5 for 3 frames ─▶ the OCR pipeline below, at the detector's quad (a quarter turn per try, for battles)
```

- **Bundle:** `scanner-bundle-gallery-20261106T183001884398Z` (since 2026-11-15; before that `retrain-20261026T052522727512Z`) from `cfbender/manavault` releases, pinned with SHA-256 sums in `server/src/scan.rs`. The server downloads it once into `data/scan-bundle/<version>/` (on startup when imports run, or on the first `GET /api/scan/bundle`), drops the 126 MB `printings.json` it doesn't need, and turns the 18 MB `arts.json` into a 6 MB column form (3.2 MB gzipped). It serves the files at `/scan-bundle/<version>/` as immutable. Phones never fetch from GitHub. Once a new version is ready, the server deletes the folders of earlier versions (`remove_other_bundles`).
- **On the phone:** the first scanner open downloads about 39 MB over the wire (detector 12.6 MB, search 14.2 MB, embed 5.1 MB, artwork index 3.2 MB, ONNX Runtime WASM 3.9 MB, gzipped where it helps), with a progress line, into the Cache API (`yama-scan-v1`, kept by `sw.js`). Later opens load from the cache. The OCR models (10.3 MB, "Browser OCR: PP-OCRv5") are loaded only when a collector line has to be read.
- **Printings that share art:** `printings.illustration_id` and `back_illustration_id` (migration 0036, filled by the `default_cards` import, importer version 3, so a running server re-imports on its next pass). Until a printing has illustration data, the endpoint returns every printing of the card (`same_art: false`); the collector line then has to read the set code exactly to settle it.
- **Cards the index doesn't know:** a bundle doesn't know cards printed after it (the pinned one is from 6 November), and the bundle leaves out battles and art series. These score below 0.6 (wrong matches scored at most 0.58 in the eval), so the scanner reads their text instead. Move to a newer bundle by changing the tag, sizes and sums in `scan.rs`, checking the search graph's inputs against `recognizer.ts`, and rerunning the recognizer eval. ManaVault publishes a bundle every week or two.

### Bundle gallery-20261106 (2026-11-15)

Bryan was about to scan the Star Trek Commander decks, released 13 November after the pinned 26 October bundle, so they would have fallen back to reading text. `gallery-20261106T183001884398Z` was the newest published bundle (`gh release list -R cfbender/manavault`, 15 Nov). What changed [ran]:

- **Same models, larger gallery.** `detector.onnx` and `embed.onnx` are byte-identical (same SHA-256); the manifest names the same recogniser checkpoint (`retrain-20261026…`) and detector. 52,350 artworks, up from 51,843: 291 `trc`, 132 `trk`, 26 `ttrk`, 25 `sds`, 22 `sld`, 7 `ttrc` and a few promos are new. Checked against Scryfall (`e:trc` and `e:trk`, with extras and variations): all 556 Star Trek Commander cards' artworks are in it (67 were in the old bundle) and all 586 Star Trek ones (419 before). The art series (`atrk`, `atrc`) aren't, as usual.
- **The search graph takes a mask.** The manifest says `search_mask: true`: `search.onnx` now needs a second input, `mask` (float32, one value per artwork in `arts.json` order; above 0 competes, 0 scores −3), and fails without it ("input 'mask' is missing"). oracle's `docs/pipeline.md`, "Search mask": ones reproduce the unmasked graph exactly. `recognizer.ts` now feeds ones when the session's `inputNames` include `mask`, and only `embeddings` otherwise (unit test in `printings.test.ts`). ### The bundle's layout, for extending the gallery ourselves (2026-11-15)

Read from the pinned bundle's graphs [ran: protobuf dump of both bundles' `.onnx` files] and from oracle at `b681392` (`docs/pipeline.md`, `cardid/graphs.py`, `degrade.py`, `data.py`, `detect.py`, `index.py`, `model.py`) [read]. For a later slice where Yama embeds artworks the bundle lacks and serves a supplement the worker merges.

- **Versions.** All three graphs are ONNX IR 8, opset 17 (`ai.onnx` only), exported by PyTorch, in both the 26 Oct and the 6 Nov bundle. A `gallery-…` release re-embeds the art with the published checkpoints and re-exports (oracle's `new-set`, `retrain --gallery-only`); a `retrain-…` release trains new weights. This one is a gallery release: `embed.onnx` and `detector.onnx` are byte-identical to the 26 Oct pin, and the manifest names the same recogniser checkpoint (`retrain-20261026T052522727512Z/last.pt`, sha256 `eb655846…`). Vectors we compute with this `embed.onnx` are therefore comparable with every row of this gallery, and stay so until a `retrain-…` bundle.
- **`embed.onnx`, the embedding graph** (679 nodes, 109 initializers). Inputs `scene` (uint8 H × W × 4 RGBA, any size) and `quad` (float32 4 × 2, the card's corners in printed order, scene pixels). Output `embeddings`, float32 F × 128, L2-normalised, one row per frame cut in `manifest.constants.frame_names` order (F = 17 now). Inside: a projective warp of the card to 250 × 350 (`GridSample`, bilinear, rounded to whole values), then for each frame its art box cut from that card (boxes in oracle's `detect.FRAMES`: modern is x 0.08 to 0.92 from y 0.115, at the art's aspect), turned upright for the two-part frames, resized to 128 × 128 (bilinear, aspect squashed), divided by 255, ImageNet-normalised (mean 0.485, 0.456, 0.406; std 0.229, 0.224, 0.225), then MobileNetV3-Small, average pool, a 576 → 128 linear layer and L2 normalisation.
- **How a gallery row is made** (oracle `index.ArtIndex`, `degrade.clean_view`): Scryfall's `art_crop` JPEG, RGB, resized straight to 128 × 128 with OpenCV `INTER_AREA` (aspect squashed, no padding), the same /255 and ImageNet normalisation, the same MobileNet and head. So the gallery side is the embedder alone on the whole art crop; the query side is the warp and cut in front of it. To embed an art crop with the published graph, either cut out the embedder subgraph (from the normalised 1 × 3 × 128 × 128 tensor to `embeddings`; a modified MPL file, whose source we'd then publish) or feed `embed.onnx` a synthetic card: a 250 × 350 scene with the crop resized into the frame's box and the quad at the scene's corners, taking the row of the art's frame. The second goes through bilinear resampling where oracle uses `INTER_AREA`, so it would differ slightly; measure either way by re-embedding a few hundred arts the bundle already has and comparing with their gallery rows (cosine near 1 expected).
- **`arts.json`, the gallery index.** A JSON array in gallery order (row i is gallery row i): `id` (Scryfall ID of a representative printing; `<id>-1` for face 1), `name`, `set`, `collector_number`, `layout`, `face`, `lang`, `illustration_id`, `url` (Scryfall `art_crop`), `frame` (one of `frame_names`) and `printing_count`. One row per distinct illustration (two for transform and modal DFCs, one per half for split, flip and Room cards); reprints share it. Our server keeps `id, name, set, number, illustration_id, layout, printings` as `arts.slim.json` (`slim_arts` in `scan.rs`). `printings.json` (126 MB, representative ID → sibling printings) we don't use; `/api/scan/printings` answers from our own database by `illustration_id`.
- **`search.onnx`, the search graph** (11 nodes; 15 with the mask). Initializers `gallery` (float16 N × 128, the gallery vectors), `frames` (int64 N × 1, each row's index into `frame_names`) and `penalties` (float32 N: 0.02 for the rare frames tall, token_tall, emblem, right, left and the two-part halves, plus each row's hub penalty, at most 0.1). Inputs `embeddings` (F × 128, `embed.onnx`'s output) and, since this bundle, `mask` (float32 N). Computes `score_i = gallery_i · embeddings[frames_i] − penalties_i`, masked rows set to −3, then top 5: outputs `indices` (int64) and `scores` (float32).
- **How `gallery.ts` reads it.** `readGallery` walks the ONNX protobuf by hand (ModelProto field 7 is the graph, GraphProto field 5 the initializers; a TensorProto's dims are field 1, data type 2, name 8, raw data 9), finds the three initializers by name, checks their types (float16, int64, float32) and lengths, and scores any subset of rows with the same formula in JavaScript (a half-to-float lookup table). That is how the set lock searches only its sets' artworks. It reads this bundle unchanged: on random embeddings its top 5 matched the graph's to six decimals.
- **Adding rows from a supplement: yes, alongside the graph, not inside it.** N is baked into the graph (the `gallery` initializer and the mask's shape), so new rows can't go into `search.onnx` without re-exporting it. But a supplement file with the same three arrays (vectors as f16, frame index, penalty) plus arts rows can be scored by the code `gallery.ts` already has, against the same `embeddings` the worker already computes, and merged with the graph's top 5 by score, its rows numbered from N up so `arts[index]` works. A few thousand rows cost well under a millisecond. Things to settle: the frame of each new art comes from its crop's aspect and layout (oracle's `frame_of`: below 0.6 left or right, below 1.1 tall, below 1.3 old or token, below 1.44 modern, above that extended, with emblem and token_tall cases), and has to use the bundle's `frame_names` order; the penalty is the 0.02 rare-frame prior, but the hub penalty needs oracle's 3,000-card scan set, so supplement rows would go without it (about half the gallery pays a few thousandths; a few arts up to 0.1); skip any illustration the bundle already has; and version the supplement by bundle version plus its own hash, since rows embedded with one bundle's weights are meaningless with another's.
- **Re-arming:** after Add (or Not this), the same artwork isn't found again until the card leaves the view or another card shows, so one card is never added twice by accident. Copies (1 to 10) are chosen on the result.
- **Keep adding (Bryan, 2026-10-28):** a switch under the camera, remembered per device. A card the camera found whose printing the scanner proved (the only printing, or the collector line read with proof) is added at once, one copy, with a buzz and "Added …" on the camera, and scanning goes on (`addsItself`). A typed name, a guessed or tapped printing still stops for Add. The same card isn't added twice until it has clearly left the view (`hold.ts`: four frames in a row with no card or only a weak match, or another card matched), so a second copy needs the first moved out of frame; one missed detection doesn't re-arm it. A card found by its text (no art match) is held by name the same way (`hold.ts`): until the detector sees no card, or another card is read twice since the held one was last read (once if near-certain). In text-only mode (no detector) four empty reads in a row, with the card unseen for 3 s, also end it; reads alternate orientations, so empty reads alone aren't proof. One misread doesn't free it, the title close-up doesn't start while it's held, and a photo skips the hold.
- **Fixing what was added:** "Added N: review" opens this session's cards, newest first, with copies, finish, Change printing and Remove. Adds merge into rows the person may already own, so a fix (`scanner/session.ts`) finds the row the add went into (Near Mint, English, same box) and takes back only this session's copies, then adds them again the new way; copies owned before are never touched. Copies of one printing are interchangeable, so the session also remembers what its own writes left in each row (`noteWrite`); if a row holds fewer when a fix is made (some taken out on another page or device), the fix is refused and points to the Collection page rather than risk taking an older copy. The session's writes run one at a time, each followed by its count lookup (three tries), and a fix's lookup, check and write run in the same queue, so overlapping saves (Keep adding the same printing twice quickly) can't muddle the counts; a fix is refused if the count couldn't be had. A row whose count was lost (lookups failed, or a retry the server reports as already applied) stays unchecked for the rest of the session, so fixes to it are refused; a later count could already miss copies taken out meanwhile. Known limit: the check and the fix are separate requests, so copies taken out on another device in the moment between them aren't caught; closing that needs a conditional op on the server, which offline-queued ops (applied later, by design) can't carry. The Collection page's own edits have the same window. The camera pauses while the list is open. Every write stays on its entry until the server confirms it, and the entry takes no other change meanwhile, from before the fix looks up its row (closing the list doesn't unlock it); a failed or unanswered write is only sent again unchanged (same op ids, applied once), so a lost reply can't double a fix. Keep adding says "Added" only after the save; a failed one stays in the list to try again.
- **Typing instead:** "Or type the name" opens the same printing picker.
- **Close-up step (Bryan's idea, 2026-10-28):** a whole card in view gives the collector line only about 1 to 2% of the frame's height, so when the first read can't settle the printing, step 2 starts by itself: a wide box replaces the card guide and asks for the phone close to the bottom-left corner. Each frame, just that box is read at full camera resolution (scaled to 200 px tall) and matched against the printings that share the art. A read of both set code and number settles it at once; set code or number alone needs the same answer twice. The picker stays below, so tapping is always an option. A printing tapped among several wasn't read from the card, so Add first asked to check the set code and number in the corner (Bryan, 2026-10-28): "Add anyway", or read it up close, which replaced the pick when it settled. Superseded 2026-11-15: tapping a printing files it ("Tap to file", above). Whenever the next step is a tap rather than the camera (picking a printing without a live camera), the camera's status chip turns white and says so, the phone buzzes, and the panel scrolls to it. For cards whose art isn't known, after two failed text reads (or with "Read the name up close") the scanner asks for the title the same way, then goes on to the printing.
- **Close-ups are a switch (Bryan, 2026-10-31):** "Close-ups" under the camera, on by default, remembered per device (`yama.scan.closeups`, shared by the collection and deck scans; `ScannerView`'s `closeups` prop). Bryan likes the two-step scan for handheld use, but a camera that stays put (a stand, or a feeder that drops cards under it) can't be moved to the corner. With it off, the scanner is one-step again: it reads the collector line from the whole-card view (since 2026-11-15, frame after frame while the card is held; "Hold and read" above), and if that doesn't settle the printing, it asks for a tap in the picker; an unknown card's name keeps being read from the whole card. Neither close-up starts by itself, but the "up close" buttons still start one. Turning it off ends a close-up in progress; the switch stays shown during both close-ups. Keep adding behaves the same either way: only a printing the scanner proved is added without a tap. A native app would do this differently.
- **Camera resolution:** the scanner asks for 3840 × 2160 (browsers give the nearest they have) and continuous focus where the browser exposes it. The resolution actually delivered is shown under "What the scanner saw". Frames are scaled to 640 px for recognition; only text crops use the full resolution.

### Extending the gallery: Yama's art supplement (2026-11-15)

Bryan (15 Nov 2026): none of the Star Trek cards scanned by art, because the pinned bundle predated them. The 6 November bundle fixed Star Trek (above); this keeps the next set from waiting for ManaVault. The server embeds the artworks the bundle lacks itself and the scanner searches them next to the bundle's (`server/src/scan_art.rs`; ARCHITECTURE.md, "The scanner's own art supplement").

```
card import (scheduler or import-cards) ─▶ scan_art: English paper illustrations in printings, not in arts.json
   ─▶ Scryfall art_crop JPEG (100 ms apart; the stored WebP crop if the JPEG is gone)
   ─▶ INTER_AREA to 128 × 128, /255, ImageNet-normalised ─▶ embed.onnx from node /Div_5 to embeddings (tract)
   ─▶ scan_arts (one 128-float vector per illustration and bundle version; no image kept)
   ─▶ data/scan-bundle/<version>/supplement-<hash>.json(.gz), named by GET /api/scan/bundle (files.supplement)
art worker: search graph's top 5 ─┬─▶ merged by score ─▶ matches
supplement rows, scored in JS ────┘   (set lock pool: both, indices from N up)
```

- **Which artworks:** English, non-digital printings with an illustration ID and an art crop, in the layouts the bundle embeds (normal, saga, class, case, token, emblem, adventure, prepare, mutate, prototype, leveler, meld, and each face of transform, modal and reversible cards and double-faced tokens). Not split, flip or Room halves (the bundle cuts those from the whole card), battles, art series, or inserts typed just `Card`. One printing per illustration, the newest. A back face is `<id>-1`, as in the bundle.
- **The same numbers as the bundle's.** A gallery row in ManaVault is the art crop resized straight to 128 × 128 with OpenCV's `INTER_AREA`, ImageNet-normalised, through the recogniser (oracle `degrade.clean_view`, `index.ArtIndex`). `embed.onnx` contains that recogniser after its warp and cuts, so the job runs the graph from the node that makes the normalised crops (`/Div_5`) to `embeddings`, with the stored wire shapes cleared (the export recorded a batch of 17 cuts). `resize_area` is OpenCV's area rule (`computeResizeAreaTab`). Every run first re-embeds three artworks the bundle has and stops, serving nothing new, unless each comes out at cosine 0.97 or more with its gallery row: a later bundle with a retrained model can't silently make vectors that don't compare.
- **Frame and penalty:** the frame from the crop's aspect and the card's layout (oracle `detect.frame_of`), indexed in the manifest's `frame_names`; the manifest's 0.02 penalty for rare frames. No hub penalty: it needs ManaVault's 3,000 background card scans, and in the bundle half the rows pay none and the median modern row about 0.003.
- **The file:** `{bundle, dim, columns, rows, frames, penalties, vectors}`, rows in the slim `arts.slim.json` shape (`parseArts` reads them), vectors float16 like the bundle's, base64. Its name is a hash of its contents, so it is served immutable; `supplement.current` points at the newest, and the one before it stays for a phone that asked a moment ago. Every file (the JSON, its `.gz`, the pointer) is written to a unique temporary name in the same folder, synced and renamed, the pointer last; a file already there under the content-addressed name is checked byte for byte (the `.gz` unzipped) and rewritten if a crash left it damaged, and leftover temporaries are deleted (review of #147, 2026-11-15). About 470 bytes per artwork (825 artworks: 390 KB, 262 KB gzipped).
- **In the worker:** `parseSupplement` checks it is for the loaded bundle; `galleryOf` scores its rows with the formula `readGallery` uses for the graph's own (dot product with the embedding for the row's frame, less its penalty), and `createRecognizer` merges them into the top 5 by score, numbered after the bundle's rows, so the set lock's `poolIndices` (over the bundle's arts, then the supplement's) and `/api/scan/printings?art=` work unchanged. A supplement that fails to download or parse is skipped with a warning; the bundle alone still works. Other supplements are dropped from the Cache API.
- **When:** at start, after each `default_cards` import (`scan_art::nudge`), every six hours, and after `import-cards`; `yama-server scan-arts [--sets trk,trc] [--limit N]` runs it by hand, and `--include-known --out DIR` writes one that includes artworks the bundle has (for evals; nothing served changes). At most 3,000 new artworks a run. A failure is retried after a day, five times at most. Rows of other bundle versions are deleted when the pin moves. One run at a time across processes: the server's job, `scan-arts` and `import-cards` take an advisory lock on `data/scan-bundle/scan-arts.lock` (`flock`), and a second runner skips with a log line.
- **Scryfall's terms:** one request at a time, 100 ms apart, with Yama's User-Agent; images are decoded in memory and dropped. Only vectors are stored or served. Only a missing image (404, 410 at every URL), a refused one (another 4xx) or one that won't decode counts against the artwork. A 429, 408, 5xx or network failure is about Scryfall, not the artwork: the run waits (Retry-After, in seconds or as an HTTP date (RFC 9110; a past date means no wait of its own); else 2 s doubling to a minute), tries the same artwork again, and after four such answers in a row (or a Retry-After over five minutes) stops, charging nothing; the next run resumes.
- **Switch:** `YAMA_SCAN_SUPPLEMENT=0` stops the job and leaves the supplement out of `/api/scan/bundle`.

### Yama's query embedder (2026-11-18)

The bundle's model scores Bryan's real scans far lower than the synthetic eval (median 0.78 against 0.97; "Art that leads settles the card", above): they are mostly sleeved cards in a blue deck box, with matte-sleeve sparkle, glare and colour casts the bundle never trained on. So Yama trains the phone's side of the match itself. `yama-art-20261118.onnx` (sha256 `52420d900728e25e2c4733c7fce45dbea0ae7f4f244fa027421a0f00cdd8faee`, 5,139,404 bytes):

- **What it is.** A drop-in for the bundle's `embed.onnx`: the same inputs (`scene` uint8 H × W × 4, `quad` float32 4 × 2) and output (`embeddings` F × 128, L2-normalised). Its front half, the warp and the 17 frame cuts up to `/Div_5`, is the bundle's graph byte for byte. Its embedding half (MobileNetV3-Small with folded BN, the 576 → 128 head, L2 norm) is fine-tuned from the bundle's weights.
- **Query side only.** The gallery vectors (in `search.onnx`), `arts.json` and the art supplement stay as they are. The supplement keeps embedding new art with the bundle's **original** `embed.onnx` (`scan_art.rs`): its vectors stand in gallery rows, and the fine-tuning moved only the query side towards them. That the query model scores supplement rows as well as the bundle's is inferred, not measured: supplement rows match the bundle's own rows for the same art at a median cosine of 1.0000 ("Extending the gallery", Measured), and no supplement eval was run with the query model. The model is valid only with galleries made by the recogniser checkpoint `retrain-20261026T052522727512Z` (manifest `recogniser.sha256` `eb6558461672b30c8b699570cc1669c4baa1a31d5fd28d617800e8a1f4d2e2c3`, which the pinned `gallery-20261106` bundle names); a bundle from a newer checkpoint (ManaVault published `retrain-20261117T065312257582Z` the day before) needs it trained again, and until then moving the pin turns it off.
- **How it was trained** (18 Nov 2026, M3 Pro GPU, about 6 minutes; `scanner-lab/`, below). 24,000 Scryfall `normal` card images, random gallery rows in the modern, old, extended, tall, token and token_tall frames, with every card name in Bryan's real photos held out. Each rendered 2 to 4 times as a phone capture at roughness U(0.2, 2.0) by `sim.py`: sleeves (75%: textured matte, a mesh or stipple sparkle with haze following a glare field, or glossy specular blobs, with a coloured sleeve edge), foil sheen, a blue box with cards behind, a table, a mat, paper or a hand, casts, exposure, defocus, motion blur, noise, sharpening, JPEG and the downscale to 640 px; the quad is the detector's with its error, or the sleeve's edge. The crops are cut by the bundle's own front graph, so training saw exactly what the phone sees: 49,240 crops, 1,874 of them (cards whose id's md5 % 25 is 0) held out. Loss: CosFace (scale 30, margin 0.15) of the query embedding against all 52,350 frozen gallery vectors, with the 990 rows of the test card names masked. AdamW, lr 2e-4, weight decay 1e-4, batch 256, cosine schedule after a 300-step warm-up, 8 epochs, light brightness and contrast jitter and a random occluding patch (30%). Epoch 6 of 0 to 7 shipped, chosen by the safety sweep below; 4, 6 and 7 were close.

A failed download is logged, and the next scanner open (or server start) at least ten minutes later tries again; meanwhile phones get the bundle's model. It's fetched at start (with `YAMA_AUTO_IMPORT=1`) when the bundle is already there, else on the first scanner open after the bundle is: on a new server that first phone still gets the bundle's model, and the next open switches it. When a new pin replaces it, the server keeps the one before (the newest other query file on disk) for phones that read the bundle info just before (as the supplement does).

Once a load has succeeded, it drops cached files under `/scan-bundle/` that the server no longer names (`bundleCache.ts`), so the bundle's `embed.onnx` doesn't linger; and only then does it keep the bundle info for opening offline (`artClient.ts`). So a cut-short download of the new file leaves the previous info and its cached files, and the scanner still works offline.
- **Telling the models apart.** Each art recognizer knows the bundle and embed file it runs (`ArtRecognizer.embed`, `<bundle version>/<file>`); a kept scan photo and a diagnostic record take it when they're made (`"none"` while the scanner reads text only), and the server records the bundle the phone ran, with `+yama-art-20261118` when it was the query model (`recorded_version` in `scan.rs`): `gallery-20261106T183001884398Z+yama-art-20261118`. Another pin's query file, from a tab open across a change, is recorded as `+query-<sha>`, never as the current model, and a tab open across a bundle change keeps its own bundle version; anything unrecognised is `+unknown`. Only a client that doesn't send it at all (a web build from before this) is recorded by what the server serves at that moment.
- **Switch:** `YAMA_SCAN_QUERY_MODEL=0` serves the bundle's `embed.onnx` again (on by default), as does `YAMA_SCAN_WITHDRAW=yama-art-20261118`. Phones then download that once more. ## Speed (2026-11-20)

Bryan, 20 Nov 2026, pointing at CardVision (app.mtgscan.org, Florian Drechsler's free, closed-source iPhone app): "want to be able to do as well as this guy. He is really fast". Its own pages describe a YOLO pose detector for the four corners (it places a corner a finger covers), a ResNet-50 trained with ArcFace on card art matching about 61,000 artworks stored on the phone, then the set symbol and collector number to choose among about 100,000 printings, all in Core ML on the iPhone [read]. Its code and models aren't available, and nothing here touches its app or servers.

**Where Yama's time goes** [ran, 20 Nov 2026]: `scanner-lab/speed/` runs `recognizer.ts` (the art worker's code) in Chrome 157 on an M3 Pro, on 160 frames of 40 held-out cards simulated by `sim.py` (sleeves, glare, blue boxes) in a 640 × 1138 portrait frame, four frames per card so tracking runs as it does live. Milliseconds, medians over the last 156 frames (the first card warms up); "tracked" frames reuse the last card window (one detector pass), "fresh" ones search for the card (two passes). Every configuration put the right card first on all 160 frames.

| Backend | Detector pass | Art embedding | Search | Frame, tracked | Frame, fresh |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WASM, 1 thread: what Yama runs (it isn't cross-origin isolated) | 50 | 61.5 (69 before the card window) | 9.8 | 122 (130) | 174 (180) |
| WASM, 2 threads | 27 | 34 | 5.2 | 67 | 95 |
| WASM, 4 threads | 21 | 27 | 3.4 | 52 | 71 |
| WebGPU, `onnxruntime-web/webgpu` (asyncify) | 14.6 | 16.5 | 3.1 | 36 | 51 |
| WebGPU, the JSPI build | 13.3 | 14.6 | 2.9 | 33 | 47 |

- **Why the models cost what they do.** The detector runs four rotated copies of its 256 px window as one batch of four (they vote on which way is up), so each pass is four CNN runs. The embed graph warps the card out, cuts it 17 ways (one cut per frame style: modern, old, extended, tall, token and twelve rarer ones) and runs MobileNetV3-Small on all 17; 92% of the gallery's 52,350 rows are modern or old. Native onnxruntime on the same Mac (one thread) gives about the same: 54 ms a detector pass, 65 ms an embedding, so the browser's WASM isn't the problem; the work is.
- **The card window.** The embed graph converted the whole frame to float before warping the card out of it (a 640 × 1138 slice, cast and transpose: about 6 ms natively). `recognizer.ts` now gives it only the quad's bounding box and a few pixels beyond (`cardWindow`): the same embeddings (largest score change 7e-5 over the 160 frames, the same top card on all of them) from about a third of the pixels. The graph normalises its warp by the scene's own size, so a cut scene only shifts it. A quad folded over itself (a corner dragged past the diagonal) samples outside its corners' box, so it keeps the whole frame.
- **WebGPU** gives the same answers (the same top card on all 160 frames; scores within 3e-4 on a fresh frame) three to four times faster on this Mac, but only with onnxruntime-web's newer WebGPU build: the default `onnxruntime-web` bundle's WebGPU kernels (JSEP) reject both bundle graphs (a reversed Slice in the detector's rotations comes out 255 px wide, and the embed graph's dynamic slices fail). The WebGPU build's WASM runtime is 28.7 MB (the JSPI one 17.9 MB) against 14.8 MB.
- **Reading a frame** back (a synthetic 2160 × 3840 canvas drawn at 640 × 1138 and read; the camera's video element may cost more) takes 1 to 2.5 ms on this Mac.
- **Per scan**, a card that leads settles on its second frame (`LEAD_FRAMES`): one fresh and one tracked frame, about 0.3 s here before any printing read.
- **On a phone** [inferred, not measured]: Bryan's Pixel 10 Pro (Tensor G5) runs one WASM thread perhaps 1.3 to 2 times slower than an M3 Pro core, more on a mid core, so about 160 to 250 ms a tracked frame and 0.4 to 0.7 s to settle a card, before the printing read. Whether its WebView offers WebGPU is unknown. Nothing records a phone's frame times yet (Scanner diagnostics keep only struggling scans).
- **What makes CardVision faster** [inferred from its description]: Core ML on the iPhone's Neural Engine runs a CNN in a few milliseconds instead of tens; its detector makes one pass and its embedder one crop per frame, where Yama's makes four and seventeen; and it reads the camera natively, with its printings catalogue on the phone.

## How the OCR pipeline works

```
frame ─▶ grey ─▶ guide quad ─▶ snap to the card's edges (locate.ts)
      ─▶ collector crop, upscaled ─▶ OCR (block, uppercase whitelist) ─▶ parse set, number, language
      ─▶ title crop, cropped to its text band ─▶ OCR (single line) ─▶ fuzzy match over ~37k names
      ─▶ decide: lookup(set, number); the title confirms it, or picks the printing from the set or number
```

- The crops are sampled straight from the camera frame through the card's homography, at 2400 px per card height for the collector line and 1200 for the title. Nothing is straightened at full size.
- The parser fixes OCR lookalikes (`M0M` to `MOM`), anchors the set code on the language code after it (`MOM • EN`), takes the number only from the line above, and ignores copyright years.
- Confidence: collector line plus a matching title is 0.97. A clear title plus the set code or the number is 0.8 to 0.9. The collector line alone is 0.85 only when the set code was read exactly and the number is zero-padded or has a total (`0214`, `030/280`); otherwise 0.5. A title alone is at most 0.9 and gives no printing. `ScannerView` locks on at 0.95, or when the same card comes back twice within 2.5 s at 0.6 or more.
- Upside-down cards: the live view alternates upright and 180° reads between frames. `scanFrame` retries at 180° itself when nothing sure was found (used for photos).

### Why these numbers are optimistic [inferred]

- The scenes are Scryfall's flat, evenly lit scans. Real photos add sleeves, foil glare, focus breathing, motion blur and rolling-shutter skew.
- The embedding gallery was built from the same Scryfall art crops the scenes were made from, so retrieval is near-duplicate matching. ManaVault does train on synthetic phone scenes and real captures [read], which should help, but measure on real photos before trusting 96%.
- Phone CPUs are slower than an M3 Pro, and a web view without cross-origin isolation gets one WASM thread.

```ts
<ScannerView onAccept={(card, copies) => addToCollection(card, copies)} onClose={() => navigate(-1)} />
```

`card` is a `ScanCandidate`: `{ name, set?, number?, lang?, scryfall_id?, image?, confidence, method }`. `method` is `"art"`, `"collector+name"`, `"collector"` or `"name"`. Unless `identityOnly` is set, `onAccept` is only offered once the printing is settled, so `scryfall_id` is always there.

`identityOnly` is for uses where only the card matters: the plan's swap check, scanning a whole deck (`web/src/decks/DeckScanPage.tsx`, `/decks/:id/scan`) checking one (`web/src/decks/DeckCheckPage.tsx`, `/decks/:id/check`) and scanning a box (`web/src/pages/BoxCheckPage.tsx`, `/collection/check/:id`). It looks up no printings. With `autoAdd` (Keep adding) it adds any card the camera found unless the art also looks like another card (`addsByIdentity`); a typed name still waits for Add. `NameEntry.tsx` is the type-a-name box, also used by the deck scan page.

For other uses, the pipeline is pure:

```ts
const res = await scanFrame(imageData, { ocr, catalog, names, guide });
// res.candidates (best first), res.quad, res.raw (OCR text), res.timings
```

`OcrEngine` and `ScanCatalog` are injected: `createScannerOcr()` (`ocrEngine.ts`: ML Kit in the Android app, PaddleOCR in the browser, Tesseract as the fallback) and `createServerCatalog()` for the app, `eval/dbCatalog.ts` (read-only SQLite) for tests.
