**Art that leads settles the card (2026-11-18).** Bryan: the collection and deck scanners get "stuck about 50% of the time". Measured [ran] on 124 of his own scan photos from production's `scan_samples` (640 px card crops with their quads and labels; the art bundle `gallery-20261106T183001884398Z` run offline with the same `embed.onnx` and `search.onnx` and the same scoring, which reproduces the scores the phone logged): the right card is the best match on 96% of them, but scores a median **0.78** (10th percentile 0.65), against 0.97 on the repo's synthetic eval. Only **36%** reach 0.82 (`LOCK_ONCE`, `ART_SURE`). Below that the scanner read the title on every frame (`needsTitle`), and a fair match (0.62) locked only after four frames whose title couldn't be read (`UNTITLED_FRAMES`): that was the stall.

What separates right from wrong on real photos is the **lead**: the best match's score minus the best score of a *different* card (other printings and illustrations of the same card, and either face of a two-faced card, aren't rivals). Every wrong best match had a lead under 0.06, the foil Westvale Abbey taken for Wei Night Raiders among them (0.645, with Honor the Fallen 0.611 next). A rule "best score at least *a*, lead at least *m*", on one frame:

|---|---|---|---|
| *a* ≥ 0.82, *m* ≥ 0.03 (before: `ART_SURE`, `ART_CLOSE`) | 36.3% | 0 | 0 |
| *a* ≥ 0.65, *m* ≥ 0.10 (**chosen**) | **84.7%** | **0** | **0** |
| *a* ≥ 0.65, *m* ≥ 0.08 | 87.9% | 0 | 0 |
| *a* ≥ 0.62, *m* ≥ 0.08 | 90.3% | 0 | 2 |
| *a* ≥ 0.60, *m* ≥ 0.05 | 93.5% | 1 | 12 |

"Unknown cards" hides every artwork of the photo's card, as if the bundle lacked it: the best (wrong) match then scored a median 0.584 (max 0.726), with a median lead of 0.018 (max 0.102). The phone sees only the search's best 5 artworks (with the set lock's pool and the supplement merged in), so when no other card is listed, the 5th score stands in for the rival (nothing unlisted scores above it); on these photos that changed nothing, as no photo had five artworks of one card on top.

Now (`LEAD_FLOOR` 0.65, `LEAD_MARGIN` 0.10, `artLead`, `leads` in `fuse.ts`): a match that leads is treated like a sure one. Its title isn't read, and it settles which card. The live camera takes it once the same card has led on two frames in a row (`LEAD_FRAMES`, `LeadRun`: beyond what the single-frame data asks, and cheap with no title read between them; counted by card, not artwork, as the set lock can swap the best match between a card's in-set and outside artworks); a photo takes it on its one frame. Never when locked to sets whose art the index mostly lacks (`leadCounts` in `setlock.ts`: a card from the same set could lead for one it doesn't know, a case the photos didn't test), nor while a new lock's pool is still being set and that share isn't known yet (`poolReady`), nor while a card filed by its name is still held; the 0.82 rule is as it was, and the two-frame fallback at 0.62 with `UNTITLED_FRAMES` stays for matches that don't lead. In the collection the printing is then settled exactly as before. In identity-only scans (deck check, deck scan, box scan) a leading match has no lookalike close enough to need its title read, and Keep going counts it by itself (`Found.leads`, as for `titled`; where it has lookalikes, like `titled` it waits for a tap under the name just filed until the view has been clear). Scanner diagnostics now note each frame's lead, and which rule settled the card (`lock`: sure, leads, twice, title or text) in the record's `scan`, so slow-find records can show how often each fires. Since 20 Nov 2026 their timing records give the same for every card settled, not only the slow ones (`get_scan_timings`).

**Deck checks: Keep going, speed and slow finds (2026-11-17).** Bryan, checking a deck on his phone: about a third of the cards waited for "Count it and check next", scanning was "crazy slow" and "laggy", it sat on "Hold still", and the last card's "Belongs" stayed up while the scanner was on the next card. Causes found in the code, not measured on the phone [inferred]:
- **Lookalikes stopped Keep going.** In an identity-only scan (deck scan, deck check, box scan, swap check) a card found by its art was counted by itself only if no other card's art scored within 0.2 of it. With his right cards at 0.64 to 0.70, nearly every card had one, even when a clear title read had named it. Now a card whose art and title agree (`Found.titled`) is counted by itself, and in identity-only scans art sure on its own (0.82) but with lookalikes has its title read first, falling back to the old untitled find when the title isn't clear. A card found by art and title under the name just filed waits for a tap until the view has been clear of cards (`filedName`), so the art hold letting go on one blurry frame can't count the same card twice.
- **Weak reads agreeing now count.** A title read short of clear (0.7, 0.04 ahead of the next name: `TITLE_AGREES`, `AGREE_MARGIN` in `fuse.ts`) settles the card when it names one of the art's matches, and so does a whole-card text read at 0.6 or more (`TEXT_VETO`). Before, both were ignored. Only in identity-only scans, and not while a card filed by its name is held: in the collection, a lookalike with one printing would be filed by itself (review of 17 Nov 2026).
- **The endless "Hold still".** The art's own fallback (four frames of the same match) and the title close-up both waited for a whole-card text read that found nothing at all; a noisy read naming some card at low confidence held both back for as long as the card was held. Now, in identity-only scans, only a read at `TEXT_VETO` or more holds them back, and a fair match (0.62) gets its four frames before the close-up opens; the collection scanner is unchanged.
- **Speed.** The camera streamed 4K and every frame cut the card at up to 2400 px to read its title, for the collector line that identity-only scans never read. They now ask for 1080p and cut the card at 1200 px (`IDENTITY_SIDE`); the title is read at `NAME_DENSITY` either way.
- **The verdict over the camera** shows for 2.5 s after a card is counted (`VERDICT_SHOWN_MS`) and goes as soon as the scanner reads another card (the `overlay` prop's `another`).
- **Slow finds are kept** by Scanner diagnostics ("slow-find"): a card that took over 3 s to find has what each frame read while looking (the art's top three, the title reads and their match, the whole-card read) in its record's `seek`, and a card never found is kept with no card when it leaves the view or the scanner pauses. At most 25 a session, so they don't push the other records out (the server keeps 200 per account); a look started before diagnostics were turned off, or under another account, is never sent. Before, a record started only once a card was found, so a stall before that left nothing to look at.

**Hold and read (2026-11-15).** Bryan, on his Pixel 10 Pro: "I want to just hold and scan the card and get all the details of the card... I don't want to have to move the card", with the close-up kept as something to ask for, not a step every time. So when several printings share the art and the first read of the collector line doesn't settle it, the scanner keeps reading (`heldRead.ts`, the hold loop in `ScannerView`): each frame it finds the card again with the art recognizer, cuts the card from the camera's full-resolution frame (`around`, up to 2400 px; the camera asks for 3840 × 2160) and reads the collector line the three ways `readCollectorLines` does. A read that proves the printing settles it. Otherwise three frames must name the same printing (`AGREE`; the close-up asks for two, from several times more pixels). Frames that read nothing don't break a run; a frame naming another printing starts a new one. With Close-ups on, the close-up box takes over after six frames (`CLOSEUP_AFTER`); with it off, the scanner reads for up to 40 frames and then leaves the picker. The held card has to stay the best match in view (within 0.02 of it, or 0.06 with the set lock), so a different card held up next isn't read against this card's printings. If the card is out of view for four frames in a row, or four reads in a row fail, it stops; it also pauses while "Wrong card" is open. The picker and "Read the collector number up close" stay shown throughout. A card found by its text (no art match) still goes to the close-up at once. Not measured on a phone yet; the frame counts are guesses to tune with real scans. Scan settings also names the text reader in use (ML Kit, PaddleOCR or Tesseract; `ENGINE_LABEL` in `ocrEngine.ts`), so you can tell which one a device has.

- **Evidence, not a run of agreeing frames** (`evidence.ts`). - **Camera picker** (`cameras.ts`, Scan settings). The cameras' labels are blank until the camera is allowed, so the list is read once it opens. They get plain names:
  - Android's "camera2 0, facing back": the lowest-numbered back camera is "Main", other back cameras are "Back camera N", and the front one is "Front";
  - Safari's lens names: "Back Camera" is "Main", plus "Ultrawide", "Telephoto", "Front", and "Back (switches lens)" for the dual and triple virtual cameras.

- **What a Pixel lists** [read: Android's logical multi-camera docs; inferred for the Pixel 10 Pro, not seen]. The + and − buttons and a pinch stop at 4× (`HAND_MAX`, whatever the camera's own top end), and auto-zoom stops at 2.5× (`AUTO_MAX`) [inferred].
- **What the camera delivers** is logged to the console when it opens (`cameraInfo`: the settings and capabilities, width and height, zoom range, focus modes, torch, and whether focus points are supported), and kept in the scanner's state for the diagnostics to come.   - It aims for the card at 60% of the visible view's height. It steps in under 45% and out over 85%, by at most 1.15× at a time, after 3 steady frames and at least 0.7 s apart, and never past 2.5×.
  - A pinch, + or − hands zoom to the person for the rest of the session.
  - The numbers are guesses [inferred]: the view and the hand will tell.

- **One still photo when reads stall.** After 5 frames read without settling, the scanner takes one still with `ImageCapture.takePhoto()` where the browser has it (Chrome on Android; the app's web view may not [inferred]), finds the card in it with the art recognizer, and reads it into the same evidence as a frame. - **Switching camera** stops everything reading the old one at once (review of 15 Nov 2026): the scanning loop, hold and read and the close-up each register how to stop (`haltReaders`), and a switch calls them before any result from the old camera can be used. A pinch still queued for the last camera is dropped when another opens; every zoom is clamped to the open camera's range (and 4×) right before it's applied, and the zoom shown is the one the camera reports (`getSettings().zoom`) where it reports one. A camera request is checked for cancellation before it's made, and a stream that arrives after the scanner moved on is stopped. A saved camera whose id has changed is found again by its label (`resolveSaved`) and remembered under its new id; it's forgotten only when neither matches.
- **Constraints one at a time.** Focus, zoom and the torch go through one queue (`serialApplier`), each change merged with whatever was applied last when it runs, so a focus point and a zoom step can't undo each other.
- **The still photo** is given 2 s (`withTimeout`) and runs alongside the frames rather than holding them up; the attempt's time cap ends the attempt whatever the still is doing.
- **Native code**: none needed for this. A native camera (CameraX, with a fixed physical lens, focus metering and full-resolution stills) is still the consult's item 6, only if the web view's numbers fall short; it would need an APK update.

    - the candidate printings (`options`);
    - the camera's name, settings and capabilities, its zoom and torch, and the text reader;
    - what was misread, and how it ended (`final`).

## Glare and foils, and going wide again (2026-11-23, night)

Bryan, after scanning Star Trek (`trk`) cards that afternoon: "the glare is really killing me. It was really bad for foils", "It'd be great if there was some sort of filter we could put on the picture to get rid of the glare", and the step out to 0.5x no longer seemed to happen "to try to scan the card better". Measured [ran] in the scanner lab (outside the repository) on that day's 402 shared scan photos (392 named and filed, each with its full-resolution card crop and the 640 px cut the phone read) and his 104 Scanner diagnostics records, with the art model and gallery the phone runs (`yama-art-20261118`, `gallery-20261106`), name-level:

- **Where glare hurts.** Across all 392, the right card was the art's top match 95.4% of the time and locked (lead rule) 89.0%. On the fifth with the most blown-out art, 88.6% and 79.7%; the rest, 96 to 98%. These are photos of cards that were named; frames that never named a card aren't kept, so the real gap is larger.
- **No filter on one picture helps.** Fourteen variants on the card before the art read: filling the glare in (OpenCV inpainting at three thresholds, two methods), contrast (CLAHE, two strengths), highlight compression, gamma, grey-world white balance, taking out part of each pixel's white (specular-free, two strengths), greying bright rainbow pixels, and two chains. None locked more right cards; filling the glare in locked 79.3% against 89.0%, and on the glariest fifth 62.0% against 79.7%. Gamma and white balance were within noise. The model was trained on clean art with simulated glare; a patched-over spot is further from the gallery than the glare was.
- **Another view does.** 77 printings were scanned two to four times (142 pairs of views). The better view of each pair was the top match 99.3% of the time and locked 97.2%; the worse, 85.9% and 75.4%. Merged views, so each spot came from the view with less glare there (a per-pixel minimum, a least-luminance pick, and soft weightings), locked 82 to 87%: two views of a hand-held card never line up exactly, and the blend is softer than either. So the scanner's way past glare is to get another view: the card tilted, the wide lens, or the light changed.
- **The flashlight is the glare.** Of the struggle records with a card picture, 44 had the flashlight on and 28 off. With it on, a median 2.3% of the art was blown out (`artGlare`) against 0.0% with it off; 18% of them had 4% or more against 7%. Its hot spots sit in the same place, left of the art's middle (their centroid 36% across, 49% down the card), where the flash's reflection off a card held flat under the phone lands; with it off, what's bright is the card's own white text box. How often the flashlight was on for cards named quickly isn't recorded; timing records now keep it (`torch`).
- **Going wide had stopped.** In his session after #205 shipped (35 records), the step out to 0.5x came 9.4 to 11.4 s into a struggle (three cards), or never: a foil Tricorder was named by its title after 5.1 s at 1x. Two causes. The card filed last counted as named until another card's art led at 0.62 twice (`filedName`, review of #205), which a glary foil laid down next never does; and any frame without a steady card started the 3 s again, which on a foil the finder misses now and then (the next frame counted as a jump too, since a missed frame cleared the card's last place).
- **Upside-down cuts are rare.** 4 of the 392 read better turned half way (all named by their text in the end).

What changed:

- **Glare on the art** (`artGlare`, glare.ts): 384 points over the art box of the frame the art was read from, each a 3 x 3 mean, blown out at luminance 235. On the 392 photos it follows the full-resolution measure closely (correlation 0.98). At `GLARE_ART` (3% of the points) the art's top match was right 87.0% of the time against 97.2% below it; at 4%, 70.8%. `GlareWatch` calls a card glary on two of its last three frames over the line, or one at twice it.
- **What the camera says.** A card the scanner is reading but can't name, glary for a second (`GLARE_HINT_MS`): "Glare on the card: tilt it a little" in place of "Hold still, reading the name" (with the flashlight on: "tilt it, or turn the flashlight off").
- **The flashlight off** (`TorchOnGlare`). Glare for 600 ms on a card it can't name with the flashlight on: the flashlight goes off, and the chip says "Flashlight off: it put glare on the card" for 2.5 s. Once a session: turned back on by the person, it stays on.
- **Going wide again** (`WideOnStruggle`, zoomPolicy.ts; ScannerView's `seeking`). A card counts as named only while the read loop holds it (the card just added or filed, read as itself) or has named it; a card it's reading and can't name is a struggle whatever is held. The 3 s start again only after three frames in a row without a steady card over 500 ms (`LOST_FRAMES`, `LOST_MS`), and need the card there on two frames of three (the finder's "cards" on an empty table jump on most frames). A glary card goes wide after 1.5 s (`WIDE_GLARE_MS`). The new test fails on the old code (a foil laid where Arcane Signet was filed never went wide).
- **The filed card's hold** keeps the artworks it was seen as only from frames that showed it (`stillHeld`): the weak art of a glary card laid down next was added too, and that card could then never end the hold by its art.
- **Records.** Each slow find's frames keep `glare` and `torch`; each timing record keeps `glare` (the most on that card), `torch`, `zoom`, and `torch_off_glare` once the flashlight went off for glare that session.

Not done:

- **Foil or not, from the sheen** (Bryan: "I think it'd be possible for foil identification"). The colour spread of a card's text box (the paper between its letters, in Lab) tells the finish it was filed with: AUC 0.98 on 334 photos (29 filed foil). At one line, 26 of the 29 foils and 21 of 305 non-foils were called foil, and most of those 21 show a rainbow (foils filed as non-foil, the session's finish setting) or are full-art lands and upside-down cuts whose "text box" is art. Whether it should set the finish or only say "Looks foil" is Bryan's to choose; the corpus (scan photos with the finish filed) is the test set.
- Exposure (lower it when the art clips) and the flashlight's strength step (Android 13's torch levels) both need the app's camera plugin and a new APK.
- Retraining the art model with stronger flashlight glare (a hot spot left of the art's middle) would be the next model step; these 392 photos are its test set.

## Card first, printing after, border, tie and feedback by price (2026-11-22)

Stage 5 of [the CardVision plan](plans/scanner-match-cardvision.md) (items 22, 24, 26 and 28), approved by
Bryan on 22 Nov 2026 ("let's go ahead and do all of this"). The scanner's rule stays: a scan never files a
printing that no rule the person can see settled, and every settle can be undone.

**Card first, printing after** (`later.ts`, Scan settings, off by default). With it, a card named before its
printing is settled becomes a provisional row at once and the scanner goes on to the next card: with Keep
adding, as soon as the camera names it; without, when Add is pressed (Add no longer waits on "Finding the
printing"). The printing is worked out after from what the scanner kept of the card, by the rules that settle
one on the sheet, whichever settles first: the only printing (in the locked sets), its border, the collector
line (the text reader's read, the view the card was named in, and up to six frames over four seconds while it
still lies there, read by the read loop as it waits for the next card), and the printing look on the frames
kept when it was named (which settles only under the look option that settles when sure); then a tie by price
when that's on. A provisional row is in the session and the strip ("Finding…", then "Pick printing" if
nothing settled, with a dashed ring) but nothing of it is saved: the Collection page saves it, in place, once
its printing settles, by the scanner or by a tap on its tile or its Added-list row. Undo (or Remove) before
then simply drops it; closing the scanner with rows still waiting asks first, and leaving any way (the
page's own "Leave without saving" included) drops them. Move all sends a waiting row to the new box when it's saved, and Clear the list keeps it (it isn't
saved yet). Its scan photo is labelled with the printing it settles on, or kept unconfirmed if it's taken
out. On the sheet, printings a border read ruled out are listed last, never as the scanner's guess. Its scan photo is kept with the
printing it settled on (the label is final), and its records end then. Typed names, corrections, Wrong
printing and rescans of one card aren't filed first; deck and box scans (identity only) don't have it.

Measured before (`get_scan_timings`, Bryan's 42 collection records of 22 Nov 2026, Pixel, the default
settings) [ran]: a card is named in a median 791 ms (p90 3.5 s), and its printing settles a median 992 ms
after that (p90 3.0 s); the 58% settled from the collector line take a median 1.4 s (p90 3.2 s). Until now the
card's row appeared only then, so card first should bring the row forward by about a second for most cards
and three for the slow ones. Timing records now say which way each card went (`detail.rows`: `card-first` or
`printing-first`) and when its row appeared (`detail.row_ms`, from the card being named), and
`get_scan_timings` compares the two (`rows`: time to name, printing time, row time). It stays an option until
the Pixel's records show the printings it settles after the card has left are as often right.

**Border colour** (`border.ts`). The band just inside the card's edge (1.2% to 2.4% of its width in, along the
middle of each side) is read from the view the card was named in. It says black or white only when all four
sides are plainly dark or plainly light and even, and at least three differ from what lies just outside the
card, so a table seen through a sleeve's clear margin or a loose outline is never taken for a border; and
only when the band further in (3.5% to 5%) isn't plainly the other colour on any side, so a coloured sleeve's
margin (its back shows through the clear front around the card) isn't taken for the card's own border. A
black border rules out white-bordered printings and a white one black-bordered printings (Scryfall's
`border_color`, which `/api/scan/printings` already sends); borderless, silver and gold are never ruled out,
nor every printing. When one printing is left it's settled ("border"); Wrong printing on it lists every
printing with the art again, and the border isn't read again for that card. Measured [ran] on 120 of Bryan's
own scan photos (the lab's data/own, his blue mat; 116 black-bordered, 3 borderless, 1 white) and on 160
clean Scryfall pictures of white- and black-bordered printings drawn on a dark and a light table: it never
said the wrong colour. On the clean pictures it read 35 of 35 white borders on the dark table and 44 of 45
black borders on the light one (among them five 7th Edition foils, which Scryfall lists as black-bordered),
and nothing where border and table were alike. On Bryan's dark mat it said
nothing on any photo: a black border is too close to the mat to tell from it. Without the outside check it
read 67 of the 116 black borders and never white, but then a white-bordered card in a sleeve on a dark mat
could read black, so the check stays. Black-against-white printings sharing an artwork are mostly old core
sets and Mystery Booster 2's playtest cards, so this settles few cards; timing records keep what each card
read (`detail.border`).

**Tie by price** (`price.ts` `priceTie`, Scan settings, off by default). When the scanner has stopped trying
by itself (reading the held card gave up or asked for a close-up, there's no camera to read from, or a
provisional row's work is done) and no look is under way, the printings that share the card's art and that
the collector line hasn't pointed away from are weighed (none when it read a set code none of them is in, or
the set code of one the set lock or the border left out, or that one's own number on two frames) (printings drawn differently can be told apart by
eye, so a card named by its title alone never ties): if every one has a market price in the finish it would be filed in and the dearest is at
most 15% above the cheapest, the dearest is settled ("price") and the card is marked "by price" in the strip,
the Added list and its large view. English printings only when there are any; never with a price missing (an
unknown price is never near another), for a proxy, for a typed name, or a printing said to be wrong. Keep
adding files it; Wrong printing undoes it, and a changed printing drops the mark. Its scan photo names only
the card, since nobody read which printing it is. Timing records count it as its own way (`price`), so how
often it's then called wrong can be read.

**Feedback by price** (`sounds.ts`, Scan settings, on by default; the collection only). A short buzz (12 ms)
when a card is found or its printing settles; when it's filed, a double pulse for a card worth the sound's
amount ($2 by default) and a short one otherwise. The chime is pitched by the market price in the finish
filed, on a log scale from 523 Hz at $0.10 or less to 1568 Hz at $50 or more, with the bright third note for
a card worth the amount. A missing price is unknown: an even two-note chime at 880 Hz, never the low one.
Off, the scanner chimes and buzzes as before.

## The first read starts when the card is named (2026-11-22)

Bryan, 22 Nov 2026, on his Pixel, with Stridehangar Automaton (DRC 19 and 35 share the art) and "R 0019 / DRC • EN" plain in the sheet's corner while it still said "Reading the printing": "we very clearly can see the printing number and I don't know why it can't read it", then "It's identifying the text there. That's very clearly 0019 but it didn't recognize it." `parseCollector` reads that text right (DRC, 19, strong), so the fault was in when, and from what, the reader read, not the parser. His 15 test scans that evening, with the app's own camera, card finder and art read: printing by the collector line on 13, a median 1.8 s after the card was named and 6.6 s at the 90th percentile.

What was found [read in the code and in Bryan's records]:

- **The first read waited.** `settle` read nothing until the card's printings had come from the server, its border had been read and the printing look had started, then read all four crops. The printings are one round trip (the look's own ask of the server took 116 ms that evening, more on a slow connection), and none of it needed to come before the reader.
- **A card named by its title had no first read.** `fromName` didn't pass on the view its title was read in, so its printing waited for hold and read to find the card again in new frames, cut it from the full frame and read it: in Bryan's Island record (236fb260) its two held frames finished 0.2 s and 2.4 s after hold and read began.
- **The records couldn't say where the rest went.** Vineglimmer Snarl (record 81b25936) was named 5.2 s after the scanner began looking for it, and its first read, one clear frame ("R O183 / DRC • EN SAM BURLEY") that settled it, finished 6.5 s after that; the title read that named it had also taken about 5 s, while Island's first held frame (finding the card, cutting it and four crops) took under 0.23 s. No record timed the reader, the crossing into the app, or the printings' fetch, and a card whose printing was merely slow kept no struggle record, so Stridehangar's reads weren't kept at all.

What changed:

- **The read starts at once** (`startFirstRead` in `ScannerView`). When a card is named, its collector line is read from the view it was named in while its printings load (on the sheet, and for a card-first row, `workOut`), and what it read is weighed once they're known, by the same rules as before: every crop read, as one frame of evidence, a field read exactly, the twin guards. When the printings need no read (one printing, the only one in the locked sets, none in them, the text reader's line proving it), it stops at the next crop; it first waits one turn of the event loop, so printings the device already has are weighed before anything is read. Art the bundle knows in only one printing waits for the server's printings before reading (a reprint newer than the bundle is then read as before). A camera switch, or the card being let go or replaced, stops it at the next crop, and nothing it read counts. A read that fails no longer empties the printing list with an error: what was read before it is weighed, and hold and read goes on.
- **A card named by its title** (from the camera or a photo) gets the same first read, from the view its title was read in, and again after "another set?" under the set lock. Its border isn't weighed there: every printing of the card is a candidate, not one artwork's few. A typed name has no view; hold and read reads it as before.
- **Timings** (Scanner diagnostics). A timing record's `detail` has `options_ms` (the printings' fetch), `first_wait_ms` (from the card being named, or settled again after "another set?", to its first crop going to the reader), `first_ms` (to the read being weighed), `first_ocr_ms` (each crop, from being handed over to answered), `first_engine_ms` (ML Kit's own time for each, the `ms` `TextReaderPlugin` already returned; the difference is the crossing into the app and waiting its turn) and `first_settled`. A struggle record notes `namedAt` (when the card was named, in its frames' clock); its first frame has `wait`, and every frame read `ocrMs` and `engineMs`; hold and read's frames also have `locateMs` (finding the card again) and `cutMs` (cutting it from the full frame).
- **A slow printing is kept** ("slow-printing", `SLOW_PRINTING_MS`): a printing the scanner settled itself 3 s or more after the card was named, on the sheet or for a card-first row, keeps a struggle record with its images and every read, and `printingMs`. Not when the person had a hand in the wait (Wrong card, another set, Wrong printing: the time runs from the first naming, and those are kept already). At most 25 a session, counted apart from slow finds.
- **Not changed:** the evidence rules and thresholds, the crops and their order, hold and read, the close-up, and the app's native code (no new APK).
- **Tests.** `ScannerView.firstRead.test.tsx` (read before the printings arrive and settled from it, nothing read for a card with one printing, art the bundle knows in one printing read only once the server says there are more, a card-first row read while its printings load, the read stopped when the card is let go, a title-named card settled from its view without its border, the slow-printing record with its timings, for a card-first row too, none for a quick one), `pipeline.timed.test.ts`, `mlkit.timed.test.ts`, `diagnostics.test.ts`.

Left [inferred]: what the first read takes now on the phone, which the next test scan's timing records say (`get_scan_timings`). If `first_ocr_ms` is large where `first_engine_ms` is small, the time is in getting to and from ML Kit, not reading: its answers come back through the app's main thread, which also hands the camera's frames to the page twenty times a second while the sheet is open, and a region cut from the app's camera waits behind the frame being converted. Regions are still cut from the camera's newest frame, not the one the card was found in ("The card finder in the app").

## The number that matters (2026-11-21)

Bryan, 21 Nov 2026: "when it's reading the bottom left text, I think it's trying to be too perfect. It struggles with the spaces and the little brush stroke thing really throws it off… Even though it sees the number, like the 286, if that's enough to know for a particular card, we're good." And: "It also seems to give up really fast", and the outline "feels a teeny bit laggy".

- **The number alone** (`numberAlone`, `evidence.ts`). The printings to tell apart are the ones sharing the card's art, which nearly always have different numbers. So it settles without the set code when all of these hold. The leader's number is read exactly in its printed form, at least three characters ("0022", "286"; a bare "22" is as likely power and toughness glued together, and a year, 1900 to 2099, the copyright line's), on two frames (`NUMBER_ALONE_FRAMES`). No other candidate's number matches it through lookalikes or one edit away (a dropped or misread digit), nor any printing with that art the set lock left out of the candidates (the card may be one of them). Nothing read names another candidate's set code, even through lookalikes, or its number exactly. And no set code read where it's printed, before the language, contradicts the leader's (a card from a set the candidates leave out, say under the set lock). The leader isn't a Mystery Booster printing, whose line prints another printing's number. Every other threshold (total, margin, posterior) and the twin guards apply as before. Its verdict carries `alone: true` in Scanner diagnostics. One frame is never enough on the number alone, since one frame can be a misread.
- **Spacing** (`tokens`). Besides the runs OCR read, a zero-padded number split by a space on one line ("02 86") is read whole (two plain numbers side by side are more often power and toughness without the slash), and a number with the printing's own rarity letter glued on ("0286M" for a mythic 286) counts as the number; a glued letter that isn't the printing's rarity stays part of it, so a read 139S still proves the 139s promo. Set codes split or glued by OCR ("M 19", "MOMEENJRYAN", the bullet or the brush mark read as a letter) are never proof: built from runs that may be something else (the rarity letter and the number, an artist's name such as "DOMENICO"), they could prove a set code nobody read (Codex reviews of #180). They only hold a number-only settle back when they name another set (`printedSets`); a card whose set code is lost that way settles on its number when that's enough.
- **Waiting** (`HOLD_BUDGET`). Bringing the phone close to the corner can put the art half out of view, where it isn't found as the card; the attempt now stops after 6 s without the card (was 2.5 s), and the close-up box takes over after 12 s (was 8 s). These are guesses as before [inferred].
- **What the sheet shows.** Each printing to pick leads with its number, then its set code and year (pre-2015 cards print no set code). The close-up shows what was read ("Read the number (286), not the set code yet"), not the raw text, which changed with every stray space; the raw text is there with Scanner diagnostics on.
- **The outline.** The corners come from the card finder the art recognizer runs anyway, so the box costs no extra recognition; but it was drawn through React state, re-rendering the whole scanner on every frame. It's now drawn straight onto the SVG. (Later the same day it became corner marks that show before the card is named and glide between the finder's passes: "Outline, focus, frame rate and card backs".)
- **Not measured on real strips.** Kept photos are 640 px (the line is about 7 px), and no diagnostic records with full-resolution strips are kept yet, so the change is covered by `evidence.test.ts` only. With Scanner diagnostics on, records of real scans can be replayed through `eval/real.test.ts`.

## The printing look (2026-11-20)

Most collection scans need the printing among several that share one artwork: 98 of the 120 test photos ("Scanner models of our own", docs/plans/scanner-models.md). The collector line settles it when it can be read; otherwise the person picks, from a list that put the newest printing first. A model that compares the card with each candidate printing's own picture already does better than that order, so it now orders the list. By default it doesn't settle a printing or change when the collector line does: one person's 640 px photos are too little evidence for that, and a later change may add it as evidence once full-resolution scans are measured. An opt-in option settles the sure ones ("Settling by look").

- **The models.** Two release assets of [`scanner-look-20261120`](https://github.com/bryanroscoe/yama/releases/tag/scanner-look-20261120), each 9,292,149 bytes, fp32, ONNX opset 17, pinned in `MODELS` (server/src/scan.rs) and downloaded into the bundle's folder as `look-<sha>.onnx`: `yama-look-v1.onnx` (sha256 `152f52c100f0080c98e0ce7063b6fe9444e0c8654b1142772339af84138b79e7`), the look slot's default `look-v1`, and `yama-look-v0.onnx` (sha256 `58acfa166105c3001445589ec11db98d5ed61f1a70a63dc133dca00fa6bd271d`), the option `look-v0`. Both have the same graph; the weights differ. Same interface as the bundle's `embed.onnx`: `scene` (H × W × 4 RGBA uint8) and `quad` (4 × 2 float32, the card's corners from top-left, clockwise, in scene pixels); outputs `symbol` and `card` (1 × 128, L2-normalised). Inside it warps the card itself (bilinear GridSample): the set symbol's area (card y 0.525 to 0.605, x 0.76 to 0.975) to 160 × 80 and the whole card to 320 × 448, standardises each channel of each, and runs two MobileNetV3-Small networks with GeM pooling and a 128-number head. Trained by Yama in the scanner lab from torchvision's ImageNet MobileNetV3-Small weights, on Scryfall pictures and simulated sleeved phone captures (InfoNCE among each artwork's printings): v0 on 19,475 printings that share art; v1's set-symbol network on the graph's own crops of all 78,063 English paper printings that share an artwork (the real photos' artworks and card names held out; 79.5% on a held-out simulated set), with v0's whole-card network. **Licence:** the models are Yama's ([LICENSE](../LICENSE)); their base weights are torchvision's, BSD-3-Clause (Copyright (c) Soumith Chintala 2016); the licence's full text, which goes with the models, is in [COPYRIGHT](../COPYRIGHT) and the release notes. The training scripts and their guide are in `scanner-lab/` (`look_*.py`, [LOOK.md](../scanner-lab/LOOK.md)). A new model is a new release asset with the same interface, added as an option (and made the default once measured better).
- **The card's side.** Once the look model has loaded, the art worker keeps the last 3 recognizer frames with a card in them (the same 640 px frame and quad the art match used, copied into buffers it reuses, so no garbage at camera rate), under each artwork the frame matched at 0.5 or more and within 0.15 of its best match anywhere and in the set lock's pool, whatever the bundle says about its printings (an artwork reprinted since the bundle counts one there). So a card settled on a match that wasn't the best (a title read, Not this card) has its frames too (Codex review of #172), while a card merely on another's list doesn't. A frame with no card, or one led by another card, clears them, and so does a photo (a reset of the recognizer, as after filing, doesn't): the next card may be another copy of the same art in another printing, while a card filed and then said to be the wrong printing is still in view. When a card is found whose art several printings share and the art's list doesn't settle it (`settle`, ScannerView.tsx), its frames are handed to the look's own worker (`look.worker.ts`, a second WASM instance, so the comparing never holds up the camera's frames in the art worker), embedded, and averaged (each part renormalised; look.ts, `meanEmbedding`). Frames from the hold-and-read loop count too.
- **The printings' side.** Each printing's vector comes from the device's own cache, else from Yama's server, which makes them ahead of time ("Vectors from the server", below), else from its Scryfall `normal` picture made here: the picture (the back's for a back face) is fetched from cards.scryfall.io (which sends `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, so a worker can read its pixels), decoded with `createImageBitmap` onto an `OffscreenCanvas`, and run with its own corners as the quad, three at a time. The fetch says `no-store`: the service worker serves a picture it already keeps but doesn't add these to the offline image cache (`public/sw.js`), which a big scan would otherwise churn. Each printing's embedding is kept per model file in memory (the last 1,500) and in a Cache API of its own (`yama-look-v1`, `/look-cache/<model>/<picture URL>`, about 1 KB each, the newest 4,000, trimmed when the look loads and every 100 added; another model's are dropped once, when the model changes). A browser without `OffscreenCanvas` or `createImageBitmap` in workers (iOS before 16.4) doesn't load the look, and Scan settings says so; a failed load ends the look's worker.
- **Score and order.** 0.8 × the symbol's cosine + 0.2 × the whole card's (look.ts, `lookScore`). The scores are kept apart from the found card's state, whose changes would restart the hold-and-read loop (review of #172). Once the look has compared every printing it could, the list is reordered, once: the printings a collector-line read put first stay first (`Choice.readFirst`), then the rest by look score, best first (`lookOrder`), and the best is marked "Looks closest" (a quiet ring and those words; `lookBest`). Nothing moves while scores come in, a list touched before the look finished keeps its order, and a look that was stopped orders nothing. The look stops when another card is found, the printing is chosen (settled or tapped), or the card is done with; Wrong printing compares the list again from the card's frames if it's still in view, or reuses the look that finished for the view the card was found in (a Scan anyway copy shares its first copy's), and the printing said to be wrong goes last and is never the guess. Reached by keyboard, as by touch, the list keeps its order. Scanners where only the card matters (deck checks, deck and box scans) don't load it.
- **Recorded.** A Scanner diagnostics record of the card has `look`, written as the scores come (so a record sent before the look ends has what it had, with `stopped`): the model, each option's score (null if not scored), and once it ends how many frames were averaged and how long that took, how many came from the device's cache, how many the server was asked about (`asked`), how many of its vectors were used (`server`) and how long its answer took (`serverMs`), and the time spent fetching, decoding and embedding the rest; `models.look` names the option that ran. Since 21 Nov 2026 the card's timing record also keeps `look_ms` (from the look starting to every printing scored), `look_scored`, `look_server`, `look_cached` and `look_made` (embedded on the phone), and `get_scan_timings`' `looks` gives, per look option, `look_time` (all looks, those with nothing made on the phone, those with some) and `vectors` (how many came from each, and the server's share).
- **Measured [ran, 20 Nov 2026].** On the 98 real sleeved photos (one tester's 640 px kept scan photos with the phone's own quads; people's data, outside the repository) of artworks several printings share: the right printing scores highest **86.7%** with v1 (85 of 98) and 57.1% with v0 (56 of 98), in the lab's Python onnxruntime and the same counts when replayed in Node through onnxruntime-web 1.31 (WASM, one thread) with the app's own `look.ts` code (`eval/look.test.ts`). With v1 the right printing or a strict visual twin of it (the same picture and the same set-symbol area, which only the collector number separates; 30 of the 98 have one) scores highest 95.9% (the lab's figure). The list's old order put the right one first 40.8%; chance is 24.7%; CollectorVision, run privately as a benchmark, 75.5% and 84.7%. Parity on two synthetic scenes (no card images in the repository; the expected outputs, `eval/look-parity-v0.json` and `-v1.json`, one per model picked by its SHA-256, come from `scanner-lab/look_parity.py` with Python's onnxruntime 1.32, which remakes them byte for byte): largest difference 3.3e-6 for each. With the photos decoded the same way, scores agree to 1.1e-4; decoded by jpeg-js instead of OpenCV they differ by up to 0.03, with the same ranking.
- **Cost [ran, M3 Pro, Node, one WASM thread].** About 24 to 83 ms to embed one printing's picture or one camera frame, either model (the higher with the machine busy; Python's multithreaded CPU run takes about 13 ms), so a typical group of 4 printings with 3 frames is under a second here, and slower on a phone, in the look's own worker. A `normal` picture is 107 to 156 KB (about 0.5 s each on a cold connection from this Mac; browsers reuse the connection). The model is a 9.3 MB download once per device, cached; not converted to fp16. Not measured: a phone's speed, and the decode in a browser.

- **Vectors from the server [ran, 21 Nov 2026].** Bryan (21 Nov 2026) wants scanning as fast as CardVision (app.mtgscan.org), where a card tossed down scans at once; the pictures were most of a look's time. So `yama-server` makes each printing's vectors itself and the phone asks for them first (`server/src/scan_look.rs`).
  - **Which, and when.** A background job embeds every printing picture of an artwork two or more printings share (the face it's on), for the look slot's default model: English paper printings first, then by release, newest first; at start, after each `default_cards` import and every six hours, until done. A picture a phone asked about that isn't made (a newer printing, or another look model on offer, such as v0) is made first; between those whole runs, an ask wakes a run that makes only the asks, with no background list or tidying (a picture that failed in the last day isn't queued at all). Scryfall's fetching and pace are the art supplement's, shared: cards.scryfall.io only, one request at a time between the two jobs, each 100 ms after the last ended, backing off on a 429 or a 5xx and stopping after four in a row; a picture that won't come or decode is tried again after a day, five times at most. One embedding at a time on a blocking thread. Kept in `scan_looks` (migration 0104) by the model file's SHA-256 and the picture's URL (what the phone's cache keys by: a picture Scryfall replaces has a new URL and is made again, and the old row goes once a run gets through everything; rows of models no longer pinned go too), as 256 float16 numbers, symbol then card: about 600 bytes a picture with its key, some 80,000 pictures for a model (about 10 GB downloaded once). `YAMA_SCAN_LOOKS=0` stops making them; those made are still served. `yama-server scan-looks [--limit N]` runs it by hand.
  - **`GET /api/scan/looks?model=look-152f52c100f0&ids=<id>,<id>-1`** (signed in, like the other scan routes; at most 64 printings; `-1` for a back face, as the art gallery names faces): `{model, looks: [{id, url, vector}], missing}`, the vector as base64 of the float16s. `model` is null for a model the server makes nothing for (unknown, withdrawn, without a parity file, or one whose check failed in this server process). The printings asked about that aren't made are queued (up to 2,000). Any signed-in account can ask for any printing; what it can make the server do is bounded by the pace and the queue, and is at most the same work as the background job's for each look model on offer.
  - **The phone** (look.worker.ts, artClient.ts). A look first checks its own memory and Cache API; for the printings it hasn't got (64 at most), its worker says `need`, and artClient asks the server (not offline; given up after 4 s) and hands the answer back, while the worker embeds the card's side. Its printings wait for that answer at most until 0.4 s after the look began (so a slow server costs a look at most the part of those 0.4 s the card's side doesn't take; a failed ask or offline costs nothing), then fetch and embed what's still missing; a printing whose vector came is never fetched, an answer that comes later still stops those fetches (look.ts, `serverOrOwn`, `ServerAnswer`), and a fetch that fails still takes the server's vector when it comes. A server vector is used only when it was made from the very URL the phone would fetch, and is kept in the device's cache like its own. The ask needs only the printings and the kept frames, not the scan sheet, so a card filed at once and given its printing afterwards gets it the same way.
  - **The same numbers.** The card data's `normal` pictures, which the phone fetches, are Scryfall's WebP `grid` pictures (since Scryfall added them on 14 Oct 2026; a printing imported before then may still have the JPEG). Chrome, and so Android's WebView, decodes a WebP with libwebp and a JPEG with libjpeg-turbo, and its canvas keeps pixels premultiplied by their alpha, so a translucent pixel (the card's rounded corners) comes back from `getImageData` with its colour rounded and a transparent one black. The server decodes a WebP with the image crate's decoder (image-webp, the same pixels as libwebp on every picture measured) and puts it through that round trip (`canvas_round_trip`: libwebp's premultiply, `(c × a × 32897) >> 23`, then divided back out, rounded), and a JPEG with libjpeg-turbo's own code (MozJPEG's decoder, built from source by the `mozjpeg` crate, no SIMD). tract 0.23.9 runs the look graph as it is (largest difference from Python's onnxruntime on the parity scenes 7.2e-6 for v1, 1.4e-5 for v0), but its GridSample takes about 290 ms an image on the M3 Pro against 10 ms for the two networks, so the server runs the graph split at its GridSample nodes: tract computes each one's image and sampling grid from the model's own nodes, `grid_sample` samples them as onnxruntime's CPU kernel does (bilinear, zeros outside, the node's own `align_corners`), and tract runs the rest. The same parity, about 15 ms an image on the M3 Pro, the decode a few more. Each model is checked against its parity file (`eval/look-parity-*.json`, to 2e-4, the web test's bound) when it's loaded, and makes nothing if it fails; a new look model needs its parity file before the server makes its vectors.
  - **Measured** against Chrome 148 decoding the pictures as the look's worker does (`createImageBitmap` onto an `OffscreenCanvas` in a worker) and onnxruntime-web 1.31 in Node, on the 644 printings of the 98 shared-art test photos, both as Scryfall's WebP `grid` pictures (what the card data names; byte for byte the lab's `data/printings`) and as its JPEGs, and 48 more JPEGs (ten cards' shared artworks).
    - **Pixels.** WebP: libwebp's and image-webp's straight pixels are Chrome's on every opaque pixel, but differ on 0.05% of values, the translucent corners, by up to 255 (a transparent pixel is black on the canvas); with the canvas round trip, 0.0001% of values differ, by one level. JPEG: the server's pixels are Chrome's exactly (so are OpenCV's); the image crate's JPEG decoder (zune-jpeg) differs on 8.5% of values (up to 9 levels; symbol cosine as low as 0.9983, one photo's top printing changed), jpeg-decoder on 3.1%.
    - **Vectors.** The server's: cosine 1.000000 with the phone's on every picture, WebP or JPEG, with both models.
    - **Rankings.** On the 98 photos, scored with the server's vectors (stored as float16) or the phone's, with the WebP pictures: the same order of every photo's printings (98 of 98; the eval counts printings scored within 1e-3 of each other as tied, as a basic land's dozens of printings of one picture can be, a few millionths apart) and the same settle decision under the option that settles when sure (98 of 98), largest score difference 1e-4, for both models. v1 puts the right printing first on 85 of the 98 and settles 45 alone with none wrong, the lab's figures; v0 on 57 (the lab's 56 read the corners' colours without the canvas round trip). With the JPEGs instead, v1 puts it first on 83 and v0 on 62, the same with either side's vectors.
    - **Not measured:** a phone's decode (Android's WebView is Chrome's, with the same decoders, but no phone was run; Safari on an iPhone decodes with Apple's own, whose pixels may differ a little, so there the vectors are close rather than equal), saget's speed (a different CPU), and the time saved on a phone (the timing records will show it: `look_time`).
    - **To check again:** `yama-server scan-looks --embed MODEL --out server.json <the pictures>`, then the look eval with `SCANNER_LOOK_SERVER=server.json` and the pictures decoded by Chrome as `SCANNER_LOOK_PRINTINGS` (`eval/look.test.ts`).

## Settling by look (2026-11-20, opt-in)

Bryan (20 Nov 2026): "We can get more… get all of these merged and move it along as much as you can", and "we want to be able to try a lot of things here and switch between models when things break". The lab found a rule an app can run that would have settled about half the shared-art photos with no wrong ones (docs/plans/scanner-models.md, "The printing look"), so it's built as an option to try, not as the default.

- **The option.** `look-v1-sure` in `MODELS` (server/src/scan.rs), "Yama's look model v1, settles when sure": v1's own pinned file (`look-152f52c100f0.onnx`), so nothing more to download and the same embedding cache, and switching between it and v1 changes nothing in the look's worker (`ArtRecognizer.useLook`). Not the default. The server lists it only to builds that run the rule (`GET /api/scan/bundle?can=settle`; it marks it `settles`): a build from before it would run it as plain v1 and record it as this. That still happens on an older build opened offline from bundle info this one kept on the device (an Android app rolled back), which is rare and leaves records naming the option that didn't run the rule. `YAMA_SCAN_WITHDRAW=look-v1-sure` withdraws it like any option, and a device that chose it runs the look's default meanwhile; withdrawing `look-v1` alone leaves it on offer (the same weights), so withdraw both if v1's weights are at fault.
- **The rule** (look.ts, `lookSettles`; `LOOK_SETTLE_LEAD` 0.10). When the look has finished and scored every printing in the list (the pool it orders: a printing whose picture couldn't be fetched or scored, or a look that stopped, means no settle), and the closest leads the next by 0.10 or more, the closest is settled. It's the raw lead over the next printing, visual twins included, that `scanner-lab/look_eval.py`'s first table measures: at 0.10 v1 settled 45 of the 98 photos alone, none wrong; at 0.08, 54 with one wrong. A strict twin scores close to its twin, so twins stay with the collector line.
  - **Never against the collector line.** Not when anything it read for this card points to another printing: another printing's set code or number read exactly as printed where the look's printing's differs, or evidence settled on another (evidence.ts, `pointsAway`, over every evidence gathered for the card: the text reader's line, the first read, hold and read, the close-up). Lookalike-only reads point nowhere. So the rule waits while the first read of the collector line is under way, and is weighed once, when the look has finished and that read is done.
  - **The collector line first.** A printing the collector line settled stays its (`how: "collector"`): it can settle before the look finishes, and an agreeing read changes nothing.
  - **Once settled**, it's `how: "look"`, with `sure` and `settledId` as a collector-line settle has: the list closes, Keep adding files it by itself, "Wrong printing" is under More (and beside Undo once filed), and the collector line stops reading the card (the hold-and-read loop and any close-up of the collector line end). The "?" says it looks most like that printing, well ahead of the others.
  - **Wrong printing** works as for a collector-line settle: the list reopens in the look's order with the printing said to be wrong marked "Read as this" and last, the collector line reads again, and a tap files the right one. The look never settles that card again.
  - Not weighed while Wrong card is open or the scanner is paused (it is once that ends), and never in scanners where only the card matters (deck checks, deck and box scans), which run no look at all. Not settled once the person has touched the list before the look finished (they're choosing; review of #178).
  - **The option running now decides** (Codex review of #178): a switch in Scan settings while a look runs applies to that card. Only an option that settles, on the same model file the look ran on, settles it; a switch to plain v1 settles nothing.
- **Recorded.** The rule is weighed under every look option, so the default's scans say what it would have done. A Scanner diagnostics record has `lookSettle` (`settled`, the option, the printing's id, set and number, and the lead; or why it didn't: `unscored`, `lead`, `read-other`, `option` for an option that doesn't settle, `touched`), its `look` note has the lead whenever every printing was scored, and `final.how` and the `misread` note say `look`. The timing record's `printing` is `look` (the server accepts it beside `only`, `set`, `collector`, `picked` and `corrected`), and `detail` keeps `look_lead` (the closest printing's lead over the next, rounded, whenever every printing was scored), `look_option` (the look option whose rule was weighed: a switch since the card was named shows here, while `models` keeps the choice when it was named), `look_sure` (what the rule said, on the raw lead and the collector line's reads) and `look_top_filed` (whether the look's closest is the printing filed first). A Wrong printing after a look settle is counted as `printing_rejected: "look"`, before filing or after. `get_scan_timings` adds `looks`: per look option (`look_option`, else `models.look`; the collection's records only), its cards, the time to settle the printing overall and by way, how often each way was then called wrong, `sure_leads` (cards the rule would have settled, by `look_sure`) and `sure_leads_filed_otherwise` (of those, filed as another printing than the look's closest at first, or changed by Wrong printing after filing). So the option's time to a printing and its corrections compare with the default's.
- **What it rests on.** One tester's 98 sleeved photos at 640 px, through the lab's Python onnxruntime (the app's own code gives the same rankings: "The printing look", "Measured"). 0 wrong of 45 is still consistent with about one in 15 wrong (the 95% upper bound), and the threshold was chosen on the same photos. It becomes the default only once Scanner diagnostics' timing records from real scans support it: `sure_leads_filed_otherwise` against `sure_leads`, under the option and under the default.
- **Tests.** `look.test.ts` (the lead, every printing scored, twins, a read pointing elsewhere), `evidence.test.ts` (`pointsAway`), `ScannerView.lookSettle.test.tsx` (settled and filed by Keep adding, Wrong printing after it, twins and an unscored printing left to the list, a conflicting or agreeing first read, a collector settle kept as the collector line's, a touched list, a switch either way while the look runs, the default only ordering, deck scans running no look, and the timing records), `artClient.look.test.ts` (switching between options on one file), server/src/scan.rs and scan_timings.rs (the option and the records).

So each part of the scanner that has a model, or a rule that could have been a model, is a **slot** with **options**, listed by the server and chosen per device.

- **The registry** is `MODELS` in `server/src/scan.rs`: each option has its slot, id, label, a sentence on what it is, its licence, where its model comes from (the app's own code, one of the bundle's files, or a file of ours pinned by URL, size and SHA-256), the bundle recogniser it fits (Yama's art model fits only galleries from checkpoint `eb655846…`), whether it's the slot's default, and whether it's the slot's **base** (what the slot does with nothing of ours: `bundle-detector`, `bundle`, `off`, `auto`, `sure`), which can't be withdrawn, so a slot always has an option. A file of ours is downloaded once into the bundle's folder as `<prefix>-<first 12 of the sum>.onnx` (never `embed.onnx`), checked as the bundle's files are, and served from there immutable; each downloads in the background on its own (a failure is retried after ten minutes and only means that option isn't on offer meanwhile), and a download that finishes drops the files of pins no longer listed, keeping the newest other one of each kind for a phone that read the bundle info just before. A test checks every slot has one base and at most one default, ids are unique and plain, and every file is pinned whole.
- **`GET /api/scan/bundle`** lists, under `models`, each slot's options on offer (id, label, note, licence, file and size) and its default. On offer means: not withdrawn, fitting the bundle (its manifest's `recogniser.sha256`), and with its file downloaded; with no bundle yet, only the slots that need nothing downloaded (text, lock, the look's Off). The default is the slot's default if it's on offer to this app, else what stands in for it (`STANDS_IN`, below), else its base. `files.embed` is the art model a build that asks for nothing more runs by default (since 22 Nov 2026 the cascade's stand-in, `yama-art-20261118`), for web builds from before this.
- **Withdrawing one.** `YAMA_SCAN_WITHDRAW=look-v1,paddle` (option ids, comma-separated) in `yama.env` on the server, then recreate the container (`docker compose up -d` in its directory): no new code. A withdrawn option isn't listed or downloaded, so every phone that chose it runs its slot's default from its next scanner open, and goes back to it once it's listed again (the choice stays on the device). Withdrawing a text reader also takes it out of Automatic's order; Tesseract stays the last resort whatever is withdrawn, as it needs nothing but the app. `YAMA_SCAN_QUERY_MODEL=0` still works, and now withdraws every art model of ours. A base option can't be withdrawn. When a slot's default is withdrawn or not downloaded yet, the default before it stands in if it's on offer (`STANDS_IN`: `look-v0` for `look-v1`, `yama-art-20261118` for `yama-art-20261118-cascade`), else the base. The same stand-in runs for an app that can't run the default (one that doesn't say it can feed the cascade `cuts`); an option needing something isn't a default without one. Withdrawing an id withdraws that option only: `yama-art-20261118-cascade` has `yama-art-20261118`'s weights, and `look-v1-sure` has `look-v1`'s, so withdraw both if those weights are at fault. Options that share a file (`look-v1-sure` runs `look-v1`'s) download it once.
- **On the phone** (`models.ts`, `ModelsSetting.tsx`): Scan settings has a "Models" part listing each slot with more than one option this device can run, as "Default (its name)" or a named option, with the option's note and what runs now ("Running Yama's art model, 18 Nov."). The choice is kept per device (`yama.scan.models`) and applies at once: another art model or card finder is loaded into the running art worker and swapped in once it's compiled and warmed (`ArtRecognizer.useArt` and `useDetector`, `Recognizer.setEmbed` and `setDetector`; a new scanner starts with the ones chosen, and falls back to the defaults if they don't load; frames meanwhile use the one before, switches run one after another so the last one asked for wins, and one that fails to load leaves the one before running and says so); another text reader stops the one running and starts with the next read, keeping the card names loaded; the lock rule applies from the next frame. Nothing reloads and the session (the cards added, the set lock, the camera) stays. A choice the server doesn't offer now (withdrawn, not downloaded yet, or a reader this device hasn't got, such as ML Kit in a browser) runs the default and says so; a scanner opened offline whose chosen art model isn't cached starts with the bundle info's own. The phone runs any card finder, art or look option that has a file (a card finder or art model with the bundle graph's interface, every look model look.ts's; the server lists one that needs more, such as the cascade's `cuts` or the rule of the look that settles when sure (`settle`), only to apps that say they can: `GET /api/scan/bundle?can=cuts,settle`; an option with `needs` is never a slot's base nor `files.embed`, and is a default only with an option every app runs standing in for it, which an app that doesn't say it can gets instead: since 22 Nov 2026 the cascade, with `yama-art-20261118` standing in) but only text readers and lock rules this build has code for, so a newer server can add them without breaking older apps; builds from before the look stage (20 Nov 2026) offer the look's Off only. When a choice made here isn't on offer, its slot is shown with it marked "not on offer now" even if the default is the only option left. The art worker's cache keeps every file the server lists in a slot, so switching back needs no download.
- **Records.** Every kept scan photo and Scanner diagnostics record names each slot's choice when it was made, as `models` (`{"detector": "bundle-detector", "art": "yama-art-20261118", "look": "off", "text": "auto", "lock": "lead"}`; `"none"` for the card finder and art while the scanner reads text only), kept in a `models` column next to `bundle` (`scan_samples`, `scan_diagnostics`; migration 0101), returned by the owner's diagnostics routes and MCP tools, and in `scan-samples-export`'s manifest. The server keeps known slots with plain ids only, and an id it doesn't know as said (a tab open across a change). `bundle` still names the art model by its file, as before.

It's offered once the server has downloaded it; make it the default only once it's measured better (the server then makes its vectors for every shared-art printing, a few hours' work).

## Outline, focus, frame rate and card backs (2026-11-21)

Bryan (21 Nov 2026), after trying CardVision's Android beta: "It's by far the best one I've seen and it's the one that we should use as our gold standard." The plan (docs/plans/scanner-match-cardvision.md) lists what it does; these are the parts of its stage 1 that need no native code and are on for everyone. Ideas only: none of CardVision's code, models or data is used.

- **The outline** (`outline.ts`, `CardOutline.tsx`; the plan's item 1). The card finder's corners used to reach the screen with the frame's whole result, after the art was matched. Now the recognizer says where the finder placed the card as soon as it has (`Recognizer.identify`'s `onSeen`, the art worker's `seen` message, `ArtRecognizer.identify`'s second argument), sooner by the art match's time, tens of milliseconds a frame ("Speed"), and the outline is drawn from that. Each card is a track in `OutlineTracker`: it eases towards the corners last reported (closing 63% of the gap every 70 ms), fades in over 120 ms and out over 220 ms, and is stepped by `requestAnimationFrame`, so it moves at the screen's rate though the finder answers a few times a second. `CardOutline` writes the SVG's paths directly, with no React render per frame, and stops asking for frames when nothing is drawn. The shape is four corner marks, each a curve through the corner (a card's corner radius, a twentieth of its short side) with two short arms, in white over a darker line so it shows on a white-bordered card; `outlinePath(quad, "whole")` gives the whole rounded outline instead. The tracker takes up to four cards a pass (the web finder gives one; the native finder will give more), keeps each card its own outline by nearness, and turns a quad whose "top" the finder flipped so the outline doesn't spin. With `prefers-reduced-motion` it neither eases nor fades. It is an overlay: the camera picture is never filtered or faded, and nothing in it feeds back into a verdict.
- **The focus pin** (`cameraFocus.ts`, `camera.tsx`; item 14). Where the camera's track offers a `manual` focus mode, a `focusDistance` range and an automatic mode to go back to (Chrome on Android; not Safari, not desktop webcams as a rule), the camera view has a focus button under zoom. Off is continuous autofocus, as before. On pins the lens and shows a slider from the camera's nearest focus to one metre, even in the ratio of distances so the close end has as much room as the far end; it starts where it was last pinned, else where the camera is focused now. The pin and its distance are kept on the device (`yama.scan.focus`) and go with the next camera opened, within that camera's range. While pinned, the scanner's own tap-to-focus on the collector line is left alone. The pin is sent with the flashlight and zoom in one `applyConstraints`, as those are (`serialApplier`). A pin remembered from before that the camera won't hold goes back to autofocus and shows as off.
- **60 frames a second** (`frameRate.ts`; item 14). After the camera opens, if its capabilities say 60 and it's delivering less, the scanner asks for `frameRate` 60 (at least 50) with the width and height it's delivering now as the least it will take, so a camera that can only do 60 on a smaller picture refuses and stays as it is; if the picture came back smaller all the same, the rate is given back. A browser won't take the frame rate in the same call as the flashlight, zoom and focus, and each call replaces the last, so the request runs in turn with those (`serialApplier.exclusive`) and they are sent again after it. A deck or box scan's 1080p stream is the likely place for it; the collection's 4K stream rarely has it. Scan settings shows "Ask the camera for 60 frames a second" where the camera offers it (on by default, per device, `yama.scan.fps60`), since a shorter exposure is grainier in a dim room.
- **The frame rate delivered** is measured from the video's own presented frames (`requestVideoFrameCallback`, over the last two seconds; `FrameRateMeter`), because a browser can accept a rate and deliver another. Scan settings says it ("Camera in use: Main, 1080 × 1920, 60 frames a second"), a struggle record's `camera` carries it as `fps` beside the setting (`frameRate`, `maxFrameRate`) and `focusPinned`, and a timing record carries `detail.fps` and `detail.focus_pinned`. No server change: both are free-form JSON there.
- **Card backs** (`cardBack.ts`; item 18). A standard back is a flat orange-brown oval in a darker olive-brown frame, with nothing bluer than the oval but the logo and the Deckmaster box. `backSigns` samples a 24 × 24 grid inside the card's quad on the frame the finder already looked at and measures six things, each the same whichever corner the finder calls the top: the share of the middle that is orange-brown, the share of the middle within a quarter of its median brightness (the oval is flat; artwork isn't), the share of the card whose weakest channel is blue, whether all four corner blocks are warm, how bright they are against the middle, and the share of the card plainly bluer than the middle. The thresholds (`BACK`) are numbers in code. They were set [ran] against Scryfall's scan of the back, fetched at build time and not kept: it passes at 672 to 112 px wide and at every turn of the quad, and 358 of 432 variants of it pass (red and blue gains 0.75 to 1.6, half to one and a half times the brightness, sensor noise to σ 16, a grey lift; the ones that fail are dim and washed out together). Against 883 scans of card fronts (gold, old brown artifacts, red, green, lands, white, colourless and full art), each under five colour casts, none passes [ran]; before the teal share and the frame's lower bound were added, 8 old brown lands and one other passed under a strong warm cast. The repository holds no card image: the tests draw a back and fronts from flat shapes in the real proportions (`eval/syntheticCards.ts`). A back is not a blank surface to the recognizer: the real one scores 5.2 to 12.4 on `cardDetail` at those sizes, over `MIN_DETAIL` (4). The scanner says "Card back, turn the card over" only when the card looks like a back **and** the art's best match scores under 0.62 (`saysCardBack`, `LOCK_TWICE`), so a front the art knows fairly well is never called a back. The title and text are still read, as for any card the art doesn't know, so a front that only looked like a back is found by its name; what changes for a back is the message in place of "Hold still, reading the name", no close-up asked for, and no slow-find record. A back in an opaque sleeve is a plain surface and stays "no card". Not built: the plan's item 20 (reading upside down only when upright is doubtful), which is the card finder's own four turns and belongs with the native finder.

Checked [ran]: unit tests for each part, and the outline, the focus slider and the message drawn over a still picture in Chrome at phone size. Not checked: a phone camera. Whether Chrome on Android keeps 60 frames a second (and the size asked as the least) after a later flashlight, zoom or focus change, which replaces the constraints, is not known, which is what the measured rate in the records is for.

## The camera source, and the app's own camera (2026-11-21)

Bryan, 21 Nov 2026, after trying CardVision's Android beta: "Maybe we need to make a better app here, like a native app for that portion of the app instead of the web view… The rest of it might be able to be a web app." This is the first native stage: the Android app can open the camera itself. Finding the card, naming it and everything on screen are still the web scanner; only where the frames come from changed. No model runs in native code yet.

**The seam** (`cameraSource.ts`). The scanner asks a camera source for four things and doesn't care which camera answers:

- `frame()`: the frame to look at now. Something it can draw from, with `videoWidth` and `videoHeight`, standing in the camera box so on-screen boxes map into it.
- `around(quad, …)` and `region(box, …)`: part of that frame at the camera's full resolution. Text is read from these.
- `track()`: the camera's capabilities and controls as a `MediaStreamTrack`, so zoom, flashlight and focus go through `camera.tsx` as they always did.
- `info()` and `fullScale()`: what the camera is and delivers (for Scan settings and Scanner diagnostics), and how many full-resolution pixels one frame pixel stands for.

Two cameras answer. `browserCameraSource` is the `<video>` fed by `getUserMedia`: its frames are already full resolution, so a region is drawn from the video, exactly as before. `openNativeCamera` (`nativeCamera.ts`) is the app's own.

**The app's own camera** (`ScannerCameraPlugin.java`, `ScannerFrames.java`; CameraX 1.6 with Camera2 interop). The Android app uses it by default if its APK has the plugin. Scan settings shows "Use the app's own camera" on those installs and remembers an explicit choice per device (`yama.scan.nativeCamera`). Until PR #193 (22 Nov 2026) the option was opt-in, and changing anything in it saved `on: false` beside the lens or rate, so a phone that had ever touched it stayed on the browser camera after the default changed (Bryan's Pixel, 22 Nov). The saved option now carries a version (`v: 2`): one saved without it keeps its lens, rate and flashlight strength but takes the default for on or off, and only a choice saved since counts as turning it off. The browser camera's `<video>` has a see-through poster, because without one Android's web view draws its own grey "play" picture until the first frame arrives. With it on:

- **Preview.** Drawn by the app in a view behind the web view. While the camera is open the web view has no background and the page lets it through: `html.native-camera` (scanner.css) clears the grounds of the page, `.scan` and `.scan-camera`, and hides everything but the scanner and dialogs. The sheet, the strip and the controls sit over the preview unchanged. Closing the camera takes the view away and puts the web view's ground back. The preview follows the camera box as the sheet moves or the phone turns.
- **Frames.** One camera stream at full size (3840 × 2160 asked for; 1920 × 1080 where only the card matters) feeds both. The newest frame is kept in memory as the camera delivered it (YUV), and only while the page is asking. The page pulls a small frame (short side 640, what the recognizer reads) up to 20 times a second into a canvas that stands where the `<video>` does and isn't painted. When the scanner wants the card or a close-up at full resolution, native code cuts that region from the frame last pulled. Frames go on being pulled while the recognizer works, so that is a frame or two newer than the one the card was found in, as a browser's video is. Nothing is written to a file.
- **Lens.** Automatic by default since 23 Nov 2026, the only lens that reaches 0.5x when a card struggles (Main from 22 Nov 2026; see "No scanning a card twice, Scan next card, one outline, and Automatic by default"). Automatic is the phone's back camera as Android presents it; whether it changes lens when a card is close is the phone maker's choice, so nothing here assumes it (Bryan's Pixel does: its ultra-wide up close). Scan settings says which lens the phone is really using where Android reports it (Android 10 on). Main, Ultra-wide and Telephoto choose that lens by itself, where the phone lists its lenses: named by focal length as a 35 mm camera's (under 20 mm ultra-wide, over 40 mm telephoto, nearest 26 mm of the rest main) [inferred from focal lengths, not checked on a phone]. A lens the phone doesn't offer falls back to Automatic and says so.
- **Capture rate.** 30 or 60 frames a second, as the exposure's frame-rate range in an ordinary session. 120 and 240 are high-speed video sessions, which can't hand the scanner frames, so they aren't offered. Scan settings shows the rate asked for and the rate the camera delivers, measured from the frames arriving.
- **Focus.** Continuous autofocus, or held at a distance with the camera view's focus control (`cameraFocus.ts`, above): the control reads the lens's range from the track and sets a distance in metres, which the plugin turns into the camera's dioptres and pins (autofocus off). A tap-to-focus point from the scanner is passed on while focus is automatic.
- **Flashlight.** On or off, and its strength where the phone offers one (CameraX's torch strength; Android 15 hardware support).
- **Zoom.** The camera's own zoom ratio, through the same + and −, pinch and auto-zoom as the browser camera.
- **If it won't open** or no usable frame arrives within three seconds of binding, the scanner goes back to the browser's camera for that visit and Scan settings says why. If the native camera bound without delivering frames, the app releases it before trying the browser camera.

**Why frames cross as they do.** Capacitor's bridge carries JSON, so a frame through a plugin call is base64. Where the web view supports it (WebView 111 or so and later [read: androidx.webkit's `WEB_MESSAGE_ARRAY_BUFFER`]), the plugin instead answers over a web message channel with one ArrayBuffer per frame: a header (48 bytes until 22 Nov 2026; 104 since, the rest saying what the app's card finder found on the frame, below) and RGBA pixels. Measured in the Android 16 emulator on an M3 Pro, 21 Nov 2026 [ran], with "Time the camera's frames" in Scan settings on a made-up 3840 × 2160 frame (the emulator's camera delivered no frames that day), medians of 15, for a 640 × 1138 frame:

| How it crosses | Size | Native convert | Crossing | Into the canvas |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ArrayBuffer, RGBA (used) | 2.9 MB | 35 ms | 3 ms | 0.2 ms |
| ArrayBuffer, JPEG | 17 kB | 29 ms | 1 ms | 2.2 ms |
| base64, RGBA (fallback) | 2.9 MB | 25 ms | 125 ms | 0.2 ms |
| base64, JPEG | 17 kB | 27 ms | 1 ms | 2.1 ms |

A card cut from the full frame over the ArrayBuffer channel: 1200 px tall, 2.4 MB, 19 ms to convert and 2 ms to cross; 2400 px tall, 9.7 MB, 33 ms and 5 ms. So raw pixels over the ArrayBuffer channel cost about 3 ms to cross and nothing to decode, and base64 costs forty times that; JPEG crosses fastest but adds a compress and a decode and loses detail the recognizer was trained with (and the made-up frame, a plain ramp, compresses far better than a real one). The cost that matters is not the crossing but scaling the 4K frame down in Java, and these convert times are an emulator under load, before the loop was trimmed to read colour once per pixel: read the real ones on a phone with the same button. Stage 3 of the plan (the card finder in native code) removes the small frame's crossing altogether.

**Records.** A timing record's camera reads "2160×3840 native main 60fps" (the full frame, the lens, the rate delivered) against "2160×3840" for the browser's camera, and a diagnostics record's camera carries `source`, `lens`, `activeLens`, `fpsAsked`, `frameRate`, `frameWidth` and `frameHeight`, the transport and the bridge's cost per frame. So the same stack of cards scanned each way can be compared with `get_scan_timings`.

**Checked in the Android 16 emulator, 21 Nov 2026:** the option appears in Scan settings, Android's own camera prompt comes from the plugin, the camera opens, the page draws the scanner's controls and sheet over the preview, and frame requests cross the ArrayBuffer channel. The emulator delivered no camera frames that day.

**Live Pixel comparison, 21 Nov 2026:** in the collection scanner with the same default models, the native camera took a median of 0.76 s to name a card across 33 records at about 30 fps. Two WebView reads in that session took 3.48 s and 4.00 s. An earlier WebView batch on the same settings had seven records and a median of 2.33 s. These were unpaired scans and the timing records cover cards the scanner identified. They support the native camera default alongside the tester's report that it felt much better. Lens switching, 60 fps, manual focus, torch strength, rotation and resume still need broader hardware coverage ("Left for a real phone test", 8).

### ONNX models for the native scanner

The native finder can use the released [`yama-corners-20261120.onnx`](https://github.com/bryanroscoe/yama/releases/download/scanner-models-20261120/yama-corners-20261120.onnx) as published. The graph takes `window` (uint8, `[256, 256, 4]`, RGBA in row-major order, no batch dimension). It returns four float32 tensors, `quad` (`[4, 2]`, window pixels starting at the printed top-left), `up` (`[2]`, length divided by four gives the card presence probability), `centre` (`[2]`, window pixels), and `short` (scalar, estimated short-side length in window pixels). The caller still chooses windows and maps the corners back into the scene as `recognizer.ts` does.

For the first native art implementation, the released [`yama-art-20261118.onnx`](https://github.com/bryanroscoe/yama/releases/download/scanner-art-20261118/yama-art-20261118.onnx) is the reference. It maps `scene` (uint8 RGBA `[height, width, 4]`) and `quad` (float32 `[4, 2]`, scene pixels) to `embeddings` (float32 `[17, 128]`). It includes the perspective warp and frame-style cuts. To embed exactly one cut after native preprocessing, extract the same weights with the lab environment from [`scanner-lab/README.md`](../scanner-lab/README.md):

```sh
python scanner-lab/native_art.py yama-art-20261118.onnx yama-art-20261118-single-crop.onnx
```

The export accepts `crops` (float32 `[1, 3, 128, 128]`, planar RGB) and returns `embeddings` (float32 `[1, 128]`, L2-normalised). The input is one row of the released graph's `/Div_5_output_0` tensor. Match the graph's perspective warp (`GridSample`, bilinear, zero padding, aligned corners), then round to the nearest even integer on ties and clip to `[0, 255]`. Slice the frame style, resize with half-pixel bilinear sampling and round again. Divide RGB by 255, subtract `[0.485, 0.456, 0.406]`, then divide by `[0.229, 0.224, 0.225]`. Keep the cut's frame-style index for gallery scoring. The faster web cascade tries `modern` and `old` first, then tries the remaining styles if the first pass is uncertain.

Verified with ONNX Runtime 1.32 on macOS. The released finder ran with the shapes above. The one-crop export matched the full art graph exactly on 714 crops from 42 synthetic scenes, including 40 simulated card captures. The card finder's Android parity is in the next section, the art read's after it.

### The card finder in the app (2026-11-22)

Stage 3 of [the plan](plans/scanner-match-cardvision.md) (items 1 to 4 and 20). Bryan, 22 Nov 2026, after the native camera: "it is night and day", and "let's go ahead and do all of this". In the Android app, with its own camera open, the card finder runs in native code on every frame the camera analyses, and draws the outline there too. The web scanner is then handed each frame with the card's corners already found on it and matches the art at those corners, so the card is found once, in the app. Nothing of CardVision's is used.

- **What runs** (`NativeFinder.java`, `CardFinder.java`, `OrtDetector.java`, `CardOutlineView.java`). The same file the Card finder slot names (the bundle's `detector.onnx` by default, or `yama-corners-20261120`), with ONNX Runtime for Android 1.32 on the CPU (two threads, full graph optimisation). The web view hands the app the file from its own cache the first time (`finderModel`); the app keeps a copy in its private files (`scanner-models/`, at most four, a content-addressed name checked against the bytes' SHA-256), so later sessions load it at once. A thread of its own takes the newest camera frame, scales it to the frame the web scanner reads (short side 640, the same conversion as before) and runs the web recognizer's window logic, ported to Java: a tracked window from the last card's box, re-searched at least every eighth frame, else the middle square and, when that sees nothing, the whole frame, then a second pass around the card; a card is in view at a vote of 0.5, a short side of 60 px and print on it (`cardDetail`, 4). It keeps the frame's pixels and the corners; the page's next frame request (`found: true`) gets those, with the corners, vote, the find's time and passes in the reply's header. Regions for text are cut from the finder's latest frame: as before (from the frame last pulled), that is the newest frame by the time the read asks, about a read's time after the one the card was found in, so a card moving fast may sit a little off the cut; stage 4's art read runs on the finder's own frame. The page's status and the camera's start and stop never wait on a model compiling (it compiles outside the finder's lock) or on a detector run (stopping only tells the loop, which publishes nothing more; Codex review of #196), and a region cut is never read from a buffer the camera is refilling (a lock the finder takes only to swap frames).
- **The outline.** Drawn natively over the preview and under the transparent web view, from the latest corners, every screen frame while it moves or fades: four rounded corner marks, eased and faded as outline.ts does, one card. The web view's own outline is hidden while the app's draws.
- **The art at the corners** (`matchAt`, recognizer.ts). No card is nothing: no finder runs in the web view. A card is matched at its corners; when that read is doubtful (not 0.70 and 0.10 ahead, the cascade's own test; 0.68 until 22 Nov 2026), it is read once more with the card turned half round and the better kept (item 20). Which way it was read is remembered for the card lying there (`TurnMemory`), so a doubtful card isn't read twice on every frame. The memory only ever chooses between the finder's own corners and those turned half round, and only while the corners are where they were (within 15% of the short side): a card turned a quarter in place, a new card, or a doubtful read naming another card is read both ways again. With the placement gate the finder alone becomes the app's corners (`locate` with `handed`: sharpness, thumbnail and card back at those corners), and the gate's read is `matchAt`.
- **Where it runs.** Only in the Android app with its own camera open, and only on phones whose processor ONNX Runtime ships for: arm64-v8a and x86_64 (Bryan, 22 Nov 2026). The APK leaves out the 32-bit ARM library, so on those phones `finderAvailable` says so and the web view's finder runs as before. It runs while the scanner reads (the outline's own rule) and rests while a card's sheet or Scan settings is open.
- **The option** (`nativeModels.ts`). Scan settings, Models, under Card finder: "Find the card in the app's own code…", on by default where it can run, per device (`yama.scan.native`). Unticking it runs the web view's finder.
- **Falling back.** The model not loading, a run failing, or anything else stopping the app's finder gives the finding back to the web view's finder for the rest of the session, which has the same model loaded all along (the art worker keeps it, for photos and for this). Scan settings says why under Card finder; ticking the option again tries once more.
- **Records.** What found the card is named: a timing record's `models.detector` is the option's id with `-native` (`bundle-detector-native`) when the app's finder placed the card, so `get_scan_timings` sets it beside the same model in the web view. `detail.finder_ms` is the median find in native code over the last frames, `detail.finder_fps` the frames it found a second, and `detail.finder_fallback` why the web view's ran instead, when it failed this session.
- **The speed test.** Each configuration is also timed with its card finder in the app (`runtime` "native-cpu", `runtimes` naming the finder's and the art's), on the same frames: the frame goes to the app's finder (`find`), timed there (its copy across the bridge isn't something the camera pays), then the art is matched at its corners in the same worker. `parity` compares its corners with the web view's on each frame (both found a card, or only one; the furthest corner; the same best match). Where the device's scanner runs the app's finder, its configurations come first, so "your models" is what the scanner really does.
- **Checked, emulator** [ran], 22 Nov 2026, Android 16 arm64 emulator on an M3 Pro (the same ONNX Runtime library as an arm64 phone): `CardFinderDeviceTest` on the parity set `eval/finderDevice.test.ts` writes (36 frames of drawn cards: held and drifting, sideways, upside down, face down, too close, too far, empty), with `yama-corners-20261120`. On the 50 windows the web's detector was shown, ONNX Runtime for Android answered within 0.007 window px of onnxruntime-web, votes within 0.00001. Run as a whole, the same decision on every frame (how many passes, a card or none); corners within 0.11 px on fresh searches, and drifting to at most 4.1 px (1.25% of the card's short side) along a track, since each tracked window comes from the frame before's answer. A detector pass took 11 to 18 ms on the emulator. The same with the bundle's own `detector.onnx` (four views a window; the default card finder): on 57 windows within 0.0004 window px, the same decision on every frame, corners within 0.31 px, votes drifting at most 0.02 along a track; a pass took 76 ms on the emulator. (The emulator ran on a busy laptop, so its times are no guide to a phone's.) `CardFinderTest` (JVM, on every build) checks the port against `finderParity.test.ts`'s fixture bit for bit: every window, corner and decision on 18 frames with a made-up detector. `NativeFinderTest` feeds camera frames through the loop and a detector that fails.
- **Not checked, needs Bryan's Pixel.** Live frames: the emulator's camera can't be relied on headless, so the loop, the outline and the bridge have run only on frames fed in. What to look at is under "Left for a real phone test", 9.

### The art read in the app (2026-11-22)

Stage 4 of [the plan](plans/scanner-match-cardvision.md) (items 17 and 30). With the card finder running in the app, the art is read there too: the art model the Art match slot runs (`OrtEmbedder.java`), with ONNX Runtime for Android on the CPU. The web worker still does everything else of the match (the cascade's decision, the search, the set lock's pool, the supplement), so it settles exactly as before; only the embedding comes from the app.

- **What it reads.** When the page takes a frame the app's finder found a card on, the app keeps that card's crop of it a moment (the last eight such frames): the box recognizer.ts's `cardWindow` would cut, at the recognizer's scale, from the frame the finder looked at. A read (`embed`, with the frame's number, the corners and the cuts) runs the art graph on that crop, as the worker would on its own copy of the frame, and hands back every row. So the answers are the worker's own: a read of the very pixels it would have read, by the same file. (With the browser-style digital zoom, which the app's camera doesn't use since it zooms the camera itself, the worker reads a resampled middle of the frame and the app the frame itself.) Not a cut at more of the full frame's pixels: the graph's warp samples without smoothing, so a near card cut at 4K would alias, and a far card might gain; that needs an evaluation of its own before it changes what's read.
- **The cascade** (`yama-art-20261118-cascade`) asks for its two first cuts, then the rest only when they don't settle the frame, each a read in the app; the default model reads all 17 cuts at once. A doubtful upright read's half turn is a read in the app too.
- **The worker asks, the page answers.** The worker's `matchAt` with `remote` posts an `embed-request` for each read; the page asks the app (`nativeEmbed`) and posts the rows back. A read of a frame the app no longer keeps is read by the worker, and nothing is wrong. Any other failure (the model not loading, a read failing, an answer that isn't an embedding) gives the art back to the worker for the rest of the session, which has the same model loaded all along.
- **The option**: Scan settings, Models, under Art match: "Read the card's art in the app's own code too", on by default where the finder runs in the app (`yama.scan.native`, `art`).
- **Records.** `models.art` is the option's id with `-native` when the frame's art was read in the app; `detail.art_ms` is the median read there, `detail.art_fallback` why the worker read it. The speed test's configurations in the app read the art there too (`runtimes.art` "native-cpu", "card finder and art in the app"), and `parity.scoreGap` says how far the best match's score came from the worker's on the same card.
- **Checked, emulator** [ran], 22 Nov 2026, Android 16 arm64 emulator: `ArtEmbedderDeviceTest` on the set `eval/artDevice.test.ts` writes (32 frames: eight cards' Scryfall pictures, fetched at run time and never kept in the repository, each laid four ways, upside down among them). With `yama-art-20261118` (the default) and with its cascade, every cut's embedding (and the cascade's first stage) within a cosine of 1.000000 of onnxruntime-web's; the bundle's search put the same card first on all 32 frames from the app's embeddings, its score within 0.00002. A read of all 17 cuts took 32 to 38 ms on the emulator (on a busy laptop, so no guide to a phone). `CardFinderTest` checks the crop against `cardBox` on the shared fixture.

### The finder's speed on the live camera (2026-11-22)

Bryan, 22 Nov 2026, after scanning with app 0.2.104 on his Pixel: "it's a little bit slower than I want it to be. I'm not sure what we can do to speed it up even more. It's massively better and is resolving pretty much every card."

- **What the Pixel said.** Test scanner speed (two runs): the app's card finder took a median 41 to 42 ms a frame with the default models and 14 to 15 with the faster ones; the art read 47 to 55 and 26 to 30. Scanning the same day: the finder took 140 to 230 ms a frame (`finder_ms`) and found 1.2 to 3.6 frames a second (`finder_fps`); scaling each 4K camera frame to the recognizer's 640 × 1138 took 66 ms (a diagnostics record's `bridge.convert`, which with the app's finder running is the finder's own scaling); the frame then took 22 ms to reach the page; the art read took 80 to 104 ms (`art_ms`). So the same code ran three to five times slower scanning than in the speed test.
- **Why: the finder's thread was turned down.** It ran one priority below normal, meant to keep it under the camera's and the page's threads. Android turns Java's priority 4 into a niceness of 10, not 0 (libartpalette's `kNiceValues`, read 22 Nov 2026): with the processor busy, about a ninth of a normal thread's share. (Older Androids also moved such a thread into the background group; today's code only sets the niceness.) The speed test's finder runs on a normal thread. In the Android 16 emulator [ran], with four normal threads keeping its four cores busy, scaling a 4K frame took a median 228 ms at priority 4 and 80 ms at normal priority (the thread's niceness read 10 and 0; Android moved neither out of the foreground group). The finder's loop now runs at normal priority (`NativeFinder.LOOP_PRIORITY`; `NativeFinderTest` checks it).
- **Why: the scaling read the frame across its rows.** A phone held upright turns the camera's frame a quarter, so the samples along a row of the upright frame came from different rows of the camera's buffer, each a row or two (3,840 or 7,680 bytes) from the last: 1,280 different buffer rows for one row out, which a phone's small memory pages and caches handle badly. It now works in 32 × 32 tiles, so a tile's samples lie along a few dozen buffer rows. The arithmetic is the same in another order and the bytes are exactly the same: `ScannerFramesTest` compares it with the old loop on frames of every turn, stride and size, and on a 4K frame scaled for the finder. Emulator [ran], nothing else busy: a median of 30 to 53 ms before and 19 to 25 after (on a busy laptop, so its times vary from run to run; on an M3 Pro's own JVM, with its larger memory pages, the two orders tie at about 10 ms). Regions cut for text (the collector strip, the card at full resolution) use the same code, so they're quicker too, and the finder waits less while one is cut.
- **Records.** Timing records add, as medians over the last frames:
  - `finder_convert_ms`: scaling the camera's frame for the finder;
  - `finder_wait_ms`: waiting for the camera's next frame, timed whole across a camera that stalls;
  - `finder_cut_wait_ms`: waiting while a text region is cut from the frame before;
  - `camera_copy_ms`: the camera thread's copy of each frame it keeps (16 MB at 4K).

  With `finder_ms` and `finder_fps`, that accounts for the loop's time a frame. They come from the app's status, asked every 2.5 s. Scan settings, Models gives the scaling time after the finder's.
- **What it costs.** Nothing limits the finder's rate but the camera's. At normal priority, with two ONNX Runtime threads, it can keep about two cores busy for as long as the scanner reads. That's level with the art read's own two threads and with the web view. Watch `art_ms`, and whether a long session heats the phone; if either suffers, the next step is to cap the finder's rate.
- **Not changed, and why.** The page still takes each frame the finder found a card on as RGBA (2.9 MB at 640 × 1138), because the art worker reads more than the art from it: the card-back check, the placement gate's sharpness and thumbnail, the printing look's frames, the border and the scan photo all come from the page's frame. Taking only the corners and asking for pixels when one of those needs them [proposal] would save the 22 ms crossing and about 2 ms drawing a frame, not the 66 ms, which was the finder's own scaling.

## The placement gate (2026-11-21, opt-in)

The second half of the plan's stage 1 (docs/plans/scanner-match-cardvision.md, items 6 to 11): decide **when** to read a card from how it moves, rather than reading every frame and counting frames. It is the lock option `lead-gate`, "Art that leads, read when the card is still", under Scan settings, Models, Settling by art; off unless chosen on a device. Ideas only from CardVision; the code is ours.

**What the gate is** (`gate.ts`, pure: no DOM, no camera, no clock of its own).

- **Moving or still.** The card's speed is the distance its fastest corner moved, in the card's own short sides a second, against the newest sighting at least 100 ms old (`Motion`, `cardSpeed`; corners are matched by nearness, so the finder flipping its idea of the top isn't movement). At or under 0.35 it is still. A card can't be called still until it has been watched for 100 ms. When nothing was seen for over 500 ms (a read that took that long), speed can't be measured, and the card is at rest if no corner is more than 0.08 of a short side from where it was.
- **A placement** is one stay of a card at rest. It waits up to 120 ms or three frames for a sharper frame (`sharpEnough`, off by default, reads a frame that sharp at once), then asks for a read of the sharpest frame it saw. A read that names nothing is tried again 50 ms later from the sharpest frame since, up to five times; then the placement says "No match, move the card" until the card moves, which starts a new placement with its five tries.
- **Latched.** A placement that was named is not read again, however long the card lies there. A 48 × 48 grey thumbnail of the card (its border left out) tells the same card, nudged, lost for a pass or seen in other light, from another card put in its place: at a correlation of 0.85 or more it is the same card and stays latched; under it, it is another card and is read at once, with no empty frame needed between (when the card comes to rest after moving, or, for a card swapped into the very same place with no movement seen, after two passes in a row that don't look like it). The thumbnail is compared either way up, since the finder may call the card's other end the top. An empty view for 350 ms lets the latch go, so a second copy of the same card set down afterwards is read and counted; only empty passes that follow one another count (a gap between two passes for at most 150 ms), so one missed pass after the scanner was paused, or after a card's sheet was open, isn't the card leaving.
- **The same card within 3 s** that has not left the view is not reported again (`suppresses`, `named`), which covers a thumbnail that changed beyond recognition (a hand's shadow).

**Its input is an interface, not the browser camera.** The gate takes `GateObservation`s and nothing else, so the native Android camera (stage 3 of the plan) feeds the same gate:

```ts
interface GateObservation<F> {
  at: number;                    // when the frame was taken, ms on any steady clock
  cards: readonly GateCard<F>[]; // every card the finder sees; none when it sees none
}
interface GateCard<F> {
  quad: Quad;         // the card's four corners in the source's own pixels, any consistent order
  sharpness?: number; // how sharp this frame's picture of the card is; higher is sharper, any scale
  thumb?: Uint8Array; // 48 × 48 grey thumbnail of the card (THUMB_SIDE), upright as the corners give it
  frame?: F;          // the frame, or a handle to it; handed back with a "read", never looked into
}
interface GateSource<F> { watch(onObservation: (o: GateObservation<F>) => void): () => void }
```

`PlacementGate.observe(o)` answers each observation with a decision: `none`, `moving`, `steadying` (at rest, waiting for a sharper frame or between tries), `read` (with the try's number and the sharpest frame's `quad` and `frame`), `reading`, `latched` (with the key it was named as) or `no-match`. After a `read`, the caller says `named(key, at, placement)` (false when it is the same card again within 3 s), `missed(at, placement)` or `dropped(placement)`, with the `placement` number the read came with: an answer that arrives after the card has moved on (a source that keeps watching while a read is out) latches nothing and counts no try. `released` on a decision says, once, that the latched card has gone; `since` is when the card in view first appeared. `unlatch()` lets a latch go from outside (Scan it again). A source that can't make thumbnails leaves them out and the card at rest is taken for the same one; one that can't measure sharpness leaves it out and the first frame of the wait is read. The native plugin has only to send corners (and, when it keeps the frames itself, its own sharpness, thumbnail and a handle) to get the same behaviour; `sharpness` and `thumb` for the browser come from `gateFrame.ts` (`frameSharpness`: the variance of the Laplacian, `quality.ts`, over the card warped to a fixed size; `cardThumb`).

**How the browser scanner uses it** (`ScannerView.tsx`, `gateLoop.ts`), with the option on:

- Every frame goes to the card finder alone (`Recognizer.locate`, the art worker's `locate` message, `ArtRecognizer.locate`), which also gives the frame's sharpness, thumbnail and whether it is a card back, and hands the frame back for the gate to keep. The outline follows it as before. The art is matched only on a `read`, on the kept frame at the corners the finder gave (`identify(frame, undefined, quad)`: the finder isn't run again). So a card lying there after it was read costs the finder's pass and nothing else.
- The rules for when to read go: not every frame but when the card rests; a card the art doesn't know has its title read on every read (not every third frame); and the title close-up is not asked for after two failed reads. Five tries, then "No match, move the card"; "Read the name up close" stays under More.
- What counts as settled stays, all of it: 0.82, the lead rule (0.65 and 0.10 ahead, on two reads in a row, which with the gate are two tries 50 ms apart), the title and text rules, the set lock, and the 0.62 fallback with its four untitled reads. So does everything about the printing: the collector line's evidence, its time budgets, the look, the picker. **Nothing is filed before its printing is settled by today's rules.**
- Holds stay as a second guard, by their own rule (`hold.ts`, unchanged). The card just filed is still known by its art: a read whose matches include it is that card (latched again, not filed again); the hold ends on another card matched at 0.62 or more, or after four reads in a row that don't show it; a weak read meanwhile is a miss, tried again. An empty view for 350 ms ends the holds, as four empty frames do without the gate.
- While the collector line is read from a held card, a frame in which the card is moving is skipped like a blurred one (`Motion` in the hold loop), and counts as a skipped frame in the budgets as a blurred one does. The budgets' times and counts are unchanged.
- The camera says "Hold the card still" while the card moves, "Hold still, reading the name" once it rests, "No match, move the card" after five tries, and for a card lying face down "Card back, turn the card over" for as long as it lies there, after one read.
- **Records.** A timing record's `models.lock` is `lead-gate`, so `get_scan_timings`' settings table sets its medians and wrong calls beside the default's. Its time runs from when the gate first saw the card, not from the read, and `detail.gate_attempts` is the try that named it.

**Not measured.** Every number above (0.35 short sides a second, 120 ms, 50 ms, five tries, 350 ms, 0.85) is a starting value [inferred], to retune from timing records on a phone. Checked [ran]: the gate's unit tests, fed times and corners as the native camera will; the thumbnail on drawn cards (the same card smaller, dimmer and with corners a pixel or two off 0.95, other cards 0.5 to 0.7); and the live loop with a faked camera and recognizer (matches nothing while the card moves, once when it rests, never again while it lies there, five tries then "No match"). Not checked: a phone camera, where the finder's own jitter decides whether 0.35 is right.

## Set lock, Skip, flashlight and zoom, scan photos (2026-11-06)

Bryan (2026-11-06), after trying Convoke's webcam scanner: the scanner should be "more consistent, less finicky, and a little bit more user-friendly"; the close-up step for the printing "was just not good". Convoke publishes nothing about its recognition beyond marketing (100,000+ labelled real game frames, retrained from players' reports); its model is paid and closed (DeckCheck licenses it). So we build on what we have, and start collecting our own labelled photos.

- **Set lock** (`setlock.ts`, `SetLockPicker.tsx`). The chip at the top of the camera says "Any set" or "Only ECC". Pick up to four sets (search by name or code, newest first; `GET /api/scan/sets`), kept per device (`yama.scan.sets`). The server sends each set's printings with their artworks (`GET /api/scan/set?code=`). The recognizer then also searches just those artworks each frame (`Recognition.pool`, below). The scanner goes on the best in-set match while it scores within 0.06 of the best match anywhere (`judgeLocked`): the right card scores the same in both, since the pool is part of the gallery, and a card that isn't in the set scores well above any lookalike in it. The printing is then chosen among the locked sets' printings with that art. Usually that's one, so it's filed with no corner to read ("It's the only printing of it in …"), and Keep adding adds it by itself. A card whose printings (of that art) aren't in the locked sets shows "X isn't in …. Is it a card from another set?". Yes settles it as usual, ignoring the lock; Wrong card offers the alternatives. A typed name never asks. When the art isn't known (a set newer than the bundle), the title is read against the locked sets' card names only, which is far easier than against all 35,000. After three cards in a row from one set, the scanner offers to lock to it.
- **Searching part of the gallery** (`gallery.ts`). The bundle's search graph is `score_i = gallery_i · embeddings[frames_i] − penalties_i`, then top 5. `readGallery` reads those three tensors out of `search.onnx` (a small protobuf reader) and scores any subset the same way in plain JavaScript. Checked against the graph on the pinned bundle with random embeddings: the same top 5 and the same scores to four decimals, about 20 ms for all 51,843 artworks on an M3 Pro [ran]. ## Browser OCR: PP-OCRv5 (2026-11-13)

Bryan (2026-11-13): "Tesseract.js was so-so but we need something better... it'd be great if we could do something even better just from the browser. I know other apps can." Decision: the browser's OCR is now PaddleOCR's PP-OCRv5 English recogniser with the PP-OCRv3 text detector, run by onnxruntime-web (already loaded for the art recognizer) in its own worker. Tesseract stays as the fallback: when the build has no models, when they fail to load, or when the worker dies or fails three reads in a row. The Android app's ML Kit is chosen in the same place (`ocrEngine.ts`), ahead of both; in the app without it, PaddleOCR reads from the models bundled in the APK.

How it reads (`paddle.ts`): the detector (2.4 MB) finds the words in the crop as slightly turned boxes; boxes on one line are merged and the recogniser (7.8 MB) reads the line in one run, straightened and scaled to 48 px high, with greedy CTC decoding. A whitelist (the collector line's uppercase set) masks the classes that may win, like Tesseract's. A title read keeps the line with the most text; a collector block keeps its lowest six lines, top to bottom. A line's direction comes from its longest words (a short word's is mostly noise). When the detector finds nothing, the crop is split into lines by rows of ink. Confidence is the mean probability of the kept characters, on Tesseract's 0 to 100 scale. Only the title close-up reads it (to decide whether to try another crop); the printing is settled by the catalog lookup and the title check, as before.

Serving: `web/scripts/paddle-models.mjs` pins the three files (recogniser, its dictionary, detector) to one commit of `monkt/paddleocr-onnx` on Hugging Face with their sizes and SHA-256 sums. The build (`copy-ocr.mjs`) downloads them once into `node_modules/.cache/yama-ocr` (or `YAMA_OCR_CACHE`), checks them, and copies them to `/ocr/paddle-<checksums>/` with a NOTICE, so a new model gets a new address (served immutable; a missing path under `/ocr/` is a 404, never the app page). A build fails if they don't match. If they can't be fetched, a CI build fails (so a pull request finds out), while the deploy's Docker build and `npm run dev` go on without them, with a warning in the build log, and that build's scanner reads with Tesseract: a Hugging Face outage doesn't block a release. Each deploy's build fetches them again (10.3 MB; the Docker build has no cache for them). They are fetched only when the scanner first reads text, and the service worker keeps them like the rest of `/ocr/`. The worker shares the art recognizer's ONNX Runtime WASM file.

## Summary

- **Recommendation: a hybrid.** Use an art-embedding lookup to say *which card* it is, and OCR of the bottom-left collector line to say *which printing*. Build it in two steps:
  1. **Now (built):** OCR only. The collector line (`R 0214` over `MOM • EN`) is looked up with the existing `GET /api/printings/lookup`. The title is read too: it confirms the lookup, and it is the fallback for cards that print no set code. About 6 MB of downloads, all Apache-2.0 or MIT, no third-party model. In simulated captures it gets **82% of cards right, with 0 confident wrong answers** [ran]. It is weak on 1993-2003 frames and on dark, blurred, glary shots.
  2. **Next:** add the MPL-2.0 ManaVault/oracle embedding bundle as the first stage. On the same simulated captures it got **96% top-1 on card identity in every condition, including the rough ones, in about 108 ms** [ran]. It does not tell reprints with the same art apart, and it misses sideways cards (battles). OCR covers both gaps.
- **Card position:** a fixed on-screen card guide, then a small edge finder (no OpenCV) that snaps the guide to the card's real edges. With the snap, corner error drops from 6.5% to 0.6% of the card width in handheld shots, and OCR accuracy rises from 78% to 82% overall [ran]. It can't find a black-bordered card on a black mat; the guide is used as is then.
- **CollectorVision stays out.** Its code and its model weights are both AGPL-3.0 (the Hugging Face model card says `license: agpl-3.0`) [read]. Its catalog is MIT, but the catalog is only useful with the AGPL model.
- **Not yet tested on a phone.** Everything ran in Node and desktop Chromium on synthetic captures made from Scryfall scans. See "Left for a real phone test".

## The art recognizer (built 2026-10-28)

```
camera frame, short side 640 ─▶ worker: detector on the middle 640 px square (then the whole frame if nothing)
      ─▶ detector again on a window where the card fills 60% ─▶ card quad, top first, any rotation
      ─▶ embed (the graph warps the card itself) ─▶ search: top 5 of 52,350 illustrations
      ─▶ lock at score ≥ 0.82, or ≥ 0.62 on two agreeing frames
      ─▶ GET /api/scan/printings?art=<illustration id>: the printings that share the art
      ─▶ one English printing: done. Several: OCR the collector line (full-resolution crop at the quad)
         and pick among them (pickPrinting). Still unsure: the person picks from thumbnails.
art score < 0.5 for 3 frames ─▶ the OCR pipeline below, at the detector's quad (a quarter turn per try, for battles)
```

- **Bundle:** `scanner-bundle-gallery-20261106T183001884398Z` (since 2026-11-15; before that `retrain-20261026T052522727512Z`) from `cfbender/manavault` releases, pinned with SHA-256 sums in `server/src/scan.rs`. The server downloads it once into `data/scan-bundle/<version>/` (on startup when imports run, or on the first `GET /api/scan/bundle`), drops the 126 MB `printings.json` it doesn't need, and turns the 18 MB `arts.json` into a 6 MB column form (3.2 MB gzipped). It serves the files at `/scan-bundle/<version>/` as immutable. Phones never fetch from GitHub. Once a new version is ready, the server deletes the folders of earlier versions (`remove_other_bundles`).
- **On the phone:** the first scanner open downloads about 39 MB over the wire (detector 12.6 MB, search 14.2 MB, embed 5.1 MB, artwork index 3.2 MB, ONNX Runtime WASM 3.9 MB, gzipped where it helps), with a progress line, into the Cache API (`yama-scan-v1`, kept by `sw.js`). Later opens load from the cache. The OCR models (10.3 MB, "Browser OCR: PP-OCRv5") are loaded only when a collector line has to be read.
- **Printings that share art:** `printings.illustration_id` and `back_illustration_id` (migration 0036, filled by the `default_cards` import, importer version 3, so a running server re-imports on its next pass). Until a printing has illustration data, the endpoint returns every printing of the card (`same_art: false`); the collector line then has to read the set code exactly to settle it.
- **Cards the index doesn't know:** a bundle doesn't know cards printed after it (the pinned one is from 6 November), and the bundle leaves out battles and art series. These score below 0.6 (wrong matches scored at most 0.58 in the eval), so the scanner reads their text instead. Move to a newer bundle by changing the tag, sizes and sums in `scan.rs`, checking the search graph's inputs against `recognizer.ts`, and rerunning the recognizer eval. ManaVault publishes a bundle every week or two.

### The bundle's layout, for extending the gallery ourselves (2026-11-15)

Read from the pinned bundle's graphs [ran: protobuf dump of both bundles' `.onnx` files] and from oracle at `b681392` (`docs/pipeline.md`, `cardid/graphs.py`, `degrade.py`, `data.py`, `detect.py`, `index.py`, `model.py`) [read]. For a later slice where Yama embeds artworks the bundle lacks and serves a supplement the worker merges.

- **Versions.** All three graphs are ONNX IR 8, opset 17 (`ai.onnx` only), exported by PyTorch, in both the 26 Oct and the 6 Nov bundle. A `gallery-…` release re-embeds the art with the published checkpoints and re-exports (oracle's `new-set`, `retrain --gallery-only`); a `retrain-…` release trains new weights. This one is a gallery release: `embed.onnx` and `detector.onnx` are byte-identical to the 26 Oct pin, and the manifest names the same recogniser checkpoint (`retrain-20261026T052522727512Z/last.pt`, sha256 `eb655846…`). Vectors we compute with this `embed.onnx` are therefore comparable with every row of this gallery, and stay so until a `retrain-…` bundle.
- **`embed.onnx`, the embedding graph** (679 nodes, 109 initializers). Inputs `scene` (uint8 H × W × 4 RGBA, any size) and `quad` (float32 4 × 2, the card's corners in printed order, scene pixels). Output `embeddings`, float32 F × 128, L2-normalised, one row per frame cut in `manifest.constants.frame_names` order (F = 17 now). Inside: a projective warp of the card to 250 × 350 (`GridSample`, bilinear, rounded to whole values), then for each frame its art box cut from that card (boxes in oracle's `detect.FRAMES`: modern is x 0.08 to 0.92 from y 0.115, at the art's aspect), turned upright for the two-part frames, resized to 128 × 128 (bilinear, aspect squashed), divided by 255, ImageNet-normalised (mean 0.485, 0.456, 0.406; std 0.229, 0.224, 0.225), then MobileNetV3-Small, average pool, a 576 → 128 linear layer and L2 normalisation.
- **How a gallery row is made** (oracle `index.ArtIndex`, `degrade.clean_view`): Scryfall's `art_crop` JPEG, RGB, resized straight to 128 × 128 with OpenCV `INTER_AREA` (aspect squashed, no padding), the same /255 and ImageNet normalisation, the same MobileNet and head. So the gallery side is the embedder alone on the whole art crop; the query side is the warp and cut in front of it. To embed an art crop with the published graph, either cut out the embedder subgraph (from the normalised 1 × 3 × 128 × 128 tensor to `embeddings`; a modified MPL file, whose source we'd then publish) or feed `embed.onnx` a synthetic card: a 250 × 350 scene with the crop resized into the frame's box and the quad at the scene's corners, taking the row of the art's frame. The second goes through bilinear resampling where oracle uses `INTER_AREA`, so it would differ slightly; measure either way by re-embedding a few hundred arts the bundle already has and comparing with their gallery rows (cosine near 1 expected).
- **`arts.json`, the gallery index.** A JSON array in gallery order (row i is gallery row i): `id` (Scryfall ID of a representative printing; `<id>-1` for face 1), `name`, `set`, `collector_number`, `layout`, `face`, `lang`, `illustration_id`, `url` (Scryfall `art_crop`), `frame` (one of `frame_names`) and `printing_count`. One row per distinct illustration (two for transform and modal DFCs, one per half for split, flip and Room cards); reprints share it. Our server keeps `id, name, set, number, illustration_id, layout, printings` as `arts.slim.json` (`slim_arts` in `scan.rs`). `printings.json` (126 MB, representative ID → sibling printings) we don't use; `/api/scan/printings` answers from our own database by `illustration_id`.
- **`search.onnx`, the search graph** (11 nodes; 15 with the mask). Initializers `gallery` (float16 N × 128, the gallery vectors), `frames` (int64 N × 1, each row's index into `frame_names`) and `penalties` (float32 N: 0.02 for the rare frames tall, token_tall, emblem, right, left and the two-part halves, plus each row's hub penalty, at most 0.1). Inputs `embeddings` (F × 128, `embed.onnx`'s output) and, since this bundle, `mask` (float32 N). Computes `score_i = gallery_i · embeddings[frames_i] − penalties_i`, masked rows set to −3, then top 5: outputs `indices` (int64) and `scores` (float32).
- **How `gallery.ts` reads it.** `readGallery` walks the ONNX protobuf by hand (ModelProto field 7 is the graph, GraphProto field 5 the initializers; a TensorProto's dims are field 1, data type 2, name 8, raw data 9), finds the three initializers by name, checks their types (float16, int64, float32) and lengths, and scores any subset of rows with the same formula in JavaScript (a half-to-float lookup table). That is how the set lock searches only its sets' artworks. It reads this bundle unchanged: on random embeddings its top 5 matched the graph's to six decimals.
- **Adding rows from a supplement: yes, alongside the graph, not inside it.** N is baked into the graph (the `gallery` initializer and the mask's shape), so new rows can't go into `search.onnx` without re-exporting it. But a supplement file with the same three arrays (vectors as f16, frame index, penalty) plus arts rows can be scored by the code `gallery.ts` already has, against the same `embeddings` the worker already computes, and merged with the graph's top 5 by score, its rows numbered from N up so `arts[index]` works. A few thousand rows cost well under a millisecond. Things to settle: the frame of each new art comes from its crop's aspect and layout (oracle's `frame_of`: below 0.6 left or right, below 1.1 tall, below 1.3 old or token, below 1.44 modern, above that extended, with emblem and token_tall cases), and has to use the bundle's `frame_names` order; the penalty is the 0.02 rare-frame prior, but the hub penalty needs oracle's 3,000-card scan set, so supplement rows would go without it (about half the gallery pays a few thousandths; a few arts up to 0.1); skip any illustration the bundle already has; and version the supplement by bundle version plus its own hash, since rows embedded with one bundle's weights are meaningless with another's.
- **Re-arming:** after Add (or Not this), the same artwork isn't found again until the card leaves the view or another card shows, so one card is never added twice by accident. Copies (1 to 10) are chosen on the result.
- **Keep adding (Bryan, 2026-10-28):** a switch under the camera, remembered per device. A card the camera found whose printing the scanner proved (the only printing, or the collector line read with proof) is added at once, one copy, with a buzz and "Added …" on the camera, and scanning goes on (`addsItself`). A typed name, a guessed or tapped printing still stops for Add. The same card isn't added twice until it has clearly left the view (`hold.ts`: four frames in a row with no card or only a weak match, or another card matched), so a second copy needs the first moved out of frame; one missed detection doesn't re-arm it. A card found by its text (no art match) is held by name the same way (`hold.ts`): until the detector sees no card, or another card is read twice since the held one was last read (once if near-certain). In text-only mode (no detector) four empty reads in a row, with the card unseen for 3 s, also end it; reads alternate orientations, so empty reads alone aren't proof. One misread doesn't free it, the title close-up doesn't start while it's held, and a photo skips the hold.
- **Fixing what was added:** "Added N: review" opens this session's cards, newest first, with copies, finish, Change printing and Remove. Adds merge into rows the person may already own, so a fix (`scanner/session.ts`) finds the row the add went into (Near Mint, English, same box) and takes back only this session's copies, then adds them again the new way; copies owned before are never touched. Copies of one printing are interchangeable, so the session also remembers what its own writes left in each row (`noteWrite`); if a row holds fewer when a fix is made (some taken out on another page or device), the fix is refused and points to the Collection page rather than risk taking an older copy. The session's writes run one at a time, each followed by its count lookup (three tries), and a fix's lookup, check and write run in the same queue, so overlapping saves (Keep adding the same printing twice quickly) can't muddle the counts; a fix is refused if the count couldn't be had. A row whose count was lost (lookups failed, or a retry the server reports as already applied) stays unchecked for the rest of the session, so fixes to it are refused; a later count could already miss copies taken out meanwhile. Known limit: the check and the fix are separate requests, so copies taken out on another device in the moment between them aren't caught; closing that needs a conditional op on the server, which offline-queued ops (applied later, by design) can't carry. The Collection page's own edits have the same window. The camera pauses while the list is open. Every write stays on its entry until the server confirms it, and the entry takes no other change meanwhile, from before the fix looks up its row (closing the list doesn't unlock it); a failed or unanswered write is only sent again unchanged (same op ids, applied once), so a lost reply can't double a fix. Keep adding says "Added" only after the save; a failed one stays in the list to try again.
- **Typing instead:** "Or type the name" opens the same printing picker.
- **Close-up step (Bryan's idea, 2026-10-28):** a whole card in view gives the collector line only about 1 to 2% of the frame's height, so when the first read can't settle the printing, step 2 starts by itself: a wide box replaces the card guide and asks for the phone close to the bottom-left corner. Each frame, just that box is read at full camera resolution (scaled to 200 px tall) and matched against the printings that share the art. A read of both set code and number settles it at once; set code or number alone needs the same answer twice. The picker stays below, so tapping is always an option. A printing tapped among several wasn't read from the card, so Add first asked to check the set code and number in the corner (Bryan, 2026-10-28): "Add anyway", or read it up close, which replaced the pick when it settled. Superseded 2026-11-15: tapping a printing files it ("Tap to file", above). Whenever the next step is a tap rather than the camera (picking a printing without a live camera), the camera's status chip turns white and says so, the phone buzzes, and the panel scrolls to it. For cards whose art isn't known, after two failed text reads (or with "Read the name up close") the scanner asks for the title the same way, then goes on to the printing.
- **Close-ups are a switch (Bryan, 2026-10-31):** "Close-ups" under the camera, on by default, remembered per device (`yama.scan.closeups`, shared by the collection and deck scans; `ScannerView`'s `closeups` prop). Bryan likes the two-step scan for handheld use, but a camera that stays put (a stand, or a feeder that drops cards under it) can't be moved to the corner. With it off, the scanner is one-step again: it reads the collector line from the whole-card view (since 2026-11-15, frame after frame while the card is held; "Hold and read" above), and if that doesn't settle the printing, it asks for a tap in the picker; an unknown card's name keeps being read from the whole card. Neither close-up starts by itself, but the "up close" buttons still start one. Turning it off ends a close-up in progress; the switch stays shown during both close-ups. Keep adding behaves the same either way: only a printing the scanner proved is added without a tap. A native app would do this differently.
- **Camera resolution:** the scanner asks for 3840 × 2160 (browsers give the nearest they have) and continuous focus where the browser exposes it. The resolution actually delivered is shown under "What the scanner saw". Frames are scaled to 640 px for recognition; only text crops use the full resolution.


- **Which artworks:** English, non-digital printings with an illustration ID and an art crop, in the layouts the bundle embeds (normal, saga, class, case, token, emblem, adventure, prepare, mutate, prototype, leveler, meld, and each face of transform, modal and reversible cards and double-faced tokens). Not split, flip or Room halves (the bundle cuts those from the whole card), battles, art series, or inserts typed just `Card`. One printing per illustration, the newest. A back face is `<id>-1`, as in the bundle.
- **The same numbers as the bundle's.** A gallery row in ManaVault is the art crop resized straight to 128 × 128 with OpenCV's `INTER_AREA`, ImageNet-normalised, through the recogniser (oracle `degrade.clean_view`, `index.ArtIndex`). `embed.onnx` contains that recogniser after its warp and cuts, so the job runs the graph from the node that makes the normalised crops (`/Div_5`) to `embeddings`, with the stored wire shapes cleared (the export recorded a batch of 17 cuts). `resize_area` is OpenCV's area rule (`computeResizeAreaTab`). Every run first re-embeds three artworks the bundle has and stops, serving nothing new, unless each comes out at cosine 0.97 or more with its gallery row: a later bundle with a retrained model can't silently make vectors that don't compare.
- **Frame and penalty:** the frame from the crop's aspect and the card's layout (oracle `detect.frame_of`), indexed in the manifest's `frame_names`; the manifest's 0.02 penalty for rare frames. No hub penalty: it needs ManaVault's 3,000 background card scans, and in the bundle half the rows pay none and the median modern row about 0.003.
- **The file:** `{bundle, dim, columns, rows, frames, penalties, vectors}`, rows in the slim `arts.slim.json` shape (`parseArts` reads them), vectors float16 like the bundle's, base64. Its name is a hash of its contents, so it is served immutable; `supplement.current` points at the newest, and the one before it stays for a phone that asked a moment ago. Every file (the JSON, its `.gz`, the pointer) is written to a unique temporary name in the same folder, synced and renamed, the pointer last; a file already there under the content-addressed name is checked byte for byte (the `.gz` unzipped) and rewritten if a crash left it damaged, and leftover temporaries are deleted (review of #147, 2026-11-15). About 470 bytes per artwork (825 artworks: 390 KB, 262 KB gzipped).
- **In the worker:** `parseSupplement` checks it is for the loaded bundle; `galleryOf` scores its rows with the formula `readGallery` uses for the graph's own (dot product with the embedding for the row's frame, less its penalty), and `createRecognizer` merges them into the top 5 by score, numbered after the bundle's rows, so the set lock's `poolIndices` (over the bundle's arts, then the supplement's) and `/api/scan/printings?art=` work unchanged. A supplement that fails to download or parse is skipped with a warning; the bundle alone still works. Other supplements are dropped from the Cache API.
- **When:** at start, after each `default_cards` import (`scan_art::nudge`), every six hours, and after `import-cards`; `yama-server scan-arts [--sets trk,trc] [--limit N]` runs it by hand, and `--include-known --out DIR` writes one that includes artworks the bundle has (for evals; nothing served changes). At most 3,000 new artworks a run. A failure is retried after a day, five times at most. Rows of other bundle versions are deleted when the pin moves. One run at a time across processes: the server's job, `scan-arts` and `import-cards` take an advisory lock on `data/scan-bundle/scan-arts.lock` (`flock`), and a second runner skips with a log line.
- **Scryfall's terms:** one request at a time, 100 ms apart, with Yama's User-Agent; images are decoded in memory and dropped. Only vectors are stored or served. Only a missing image (404, 410 at every URL), a refused one (another 4xx) or one that won't decode counts against the artwork. A 429, 408, 5xx or network failure is about Scryfall, not the artwork: the run waits (Retry-After, in seconds or as an HTTP date (RFC 9110; a past date means no wait of its own); else 2 s doubling to a minute), tries the same artwork again, and after four such answers in a row (or a Retry-After over five minutes) stops, charging nothing; the next run resumes.
- **Switch:** `YAMA_SCAN_SUPPLEMENT=0` stops the job and leaves the supplement out of `/api/scan/bundle`.

### Yama's query embedder (2026-11-18)

The bundle's model scores Bryan's real scans far lower than the synthetic eval (median 0.78 against 0.97; "Art that leads settles the card", above): they are mostly sleeved cards in a blue deck box, with matte-sleeve sparkle, glare and colour casts the bundle never trained on. So Yama trains the phone's side of the match itself. `yama-art-20261118.onnx` (sha256 `52420d900728e25e2c4733c7fce45dbea0ae7f4f244fa027421a0f00cdd8faee`, 5,139,404 bytes):

- **What it is.** A drop-in for the bundle's `embed.onnx`: the same inputs (`scene` uint8 H × W × 4, `quad` float32 4 × 2) and output (`embeddings` F × 128, L2-normalised). Its front half, the warp and the 17 frame cuts up to `/Div_5`, is the bundle's graph byte for byte. Its embedding half (MobileNetV3-Small with folded BN, the 576 → 128 head, L2 norm) is fine-tuned from the bundle's weights.
- **Query side only.** The gallery vectors (in `search.onnx`), `arts.json` and the art supplement stay as they are. The supplement keeps embedding new art with the bundle's **original** `embed.onnx` (`scan_art.rs`): its vectors stand in gallery rows, and the fine-tuning moved only the query side towards them. That the query model scores supplement rows as well as the bundle's is inferred, not measured: supplement rows match the bundle's own rows for the same art at a median cosine of 1.0000 ("Extending the gallery", Measured), and no supplement eval was run with the query model. The model is valid only with galleries made by the recogniser checkpoint `retrain-20261026T052522727512Z` (manifest `recogniser.sha256` `eb6558461672b30c8b699570cc1669c4baa1a31d5fd28d617800e8a1f4d2e2c3`, which the pinned `gallery-20261106` bundle names); a bundle from a newer checkpoint (ManaVault published `retrain-20261117T065312257582Z` the day before) needs it trained again, and until then moving the pin turns it off.
- **How it was trained** (18 Nov 2026, M3 Pro GPU, about 6 minutes; `scanner-lab/`, below). 24,000 Scryfall `normal` card images, random gallery rows in the modern, old, extended, tall, token and token_tall frames, with every card name in Bryan's real photos held out. Each rendered 2 to 4 times as a phone capture at roughness U(0.2, 2.0) by `sim.py`: sleeves (75%: textured matte, a mesh or stipple sparkle with haze following a glare field, or glossy specular blobs, with a coloured sleeve edge), foil sheen, a blue box with cards behind, a table, a mat, paper or a hand, casts, exposure, defocus, motion blur, noise, sharpening, JPEG and the downscale to 640 px; the quad is the detector's with its error, or the sleeve's edge. The crops are cut by the bundle's own front graph, so training saw exactly what the phone sees: 49,240 crops, 1,874 of them (cards whose id's md5 % 25 is 0) held out. Loss: CosFace (scale 30, margin 0.15) of the query embedding against all 52,350 frozen gallery vectors, with the 990 rows of the test card names masked. AdamW, lr 2e-4, weight decay 1e-4, batch 256, cosine schedule after a 300-step warm-up, 8 epochs, light brightness and contrast jitter and a random occluding patch (30%). Epoch 6 of 0 to 7 shipped, chosen by the safety sweep below; 4, 6 and 7 were close.

- **Served** (`server/src/scan.rs`, `QUERY_MODEL`). Published as an asset of this repository's release [`scanner-art-20261118`](https://github.com/bryanroscoe/yama/releases/tag/scanner-art-20261118), not marked latest (the Android app's updater reads its `android-app` release by tag). The server pins its URL, size and SHA-256, downloads it once into the bundle's folder as `query-52420d900728.onnx` (never over `embed.onnx`), checks it the way it checks the bundle's files, and drops the query files of earlier pins. `GET /api/scan/bundle` names it as `files.embed` (and its size as `bytes.embed`) only when the bundle's manifest names the recogniser above, `YAMA_SCAN_QUERY_MODEL` allows it, and it's downloaded; otherwise the bundle's `embed.onnx`, as before. A failed download is logged, and the next scanner open (or server start) at least ten minutes later tries again; meanwhile phones get the bundle's model. It's fetched at start (with `YAMA_AUTO_IMPORT=1`) when the bundle is already there, else on the first scanner open after the bundle is: on a new server that first phone still gets the bundle's model, and the next open switches it. When a new pin replaces it, the server keeps the one before (the newest other query file on disk) for phones that read the bundle info just before (as the supplement does).
- **On the phone** nothing changes but the file: the art worker loads whatever `files.embed` names, and since its cache is keyed by URL it downloads the new 5.1 MB once. Once a load has succeeded, it drops cached files under `/scan-bundle/` that the server no longer names (`bundleCache.ts`), so the bundle's `embed.onnx` doesn't linger; and only then does it keep the bundle info for opening offline (`artClient.ts`). So a cut-short download of the new file leaves the previous info and its cached files, and the scanner still works offline.
- **Telling the models apart.** Each art recognizer knows the bundle and embed file it runs (`ArtRecognizer.embed`, `<bundle version>/<file>`); a kept scan photo and a diagnostic record take it when they're made (`"none"` while the scanner reads text only), and the server records the bundle the phone ran, with `+yama-art-20261118` when it was the query model (`recorded_version` in `scan.rs`): `gallery-20261106T183001884398Z+yama-art-20261118`. Another pin's query file, from a tab open across a change, is recorded as `+query-<sha>`, never as the current model, and a tab open across a bundle change keeps its own bundle version; anything unrecognised is `+unknown`. Only a client that doesn't send it at all (a web build from before this) is recorded by what the server serves at that moment.
- **Switch:** `YAMA_SCAN_QUERY_MODEL=0` serves the bundle's `embed.onnx` again (on by default): it withdraws every art model of ours. Withdrawing these weights by id takes both options that carry them, `YAMA_SCAN_WITHDRAW=yama-art-20261118,yama-art-20261118-cascade`; `yama-art-20261118` alone leaves today's apps on the cascade, the same weights. Phones then download the bundle's model once more. It was the art slot's default from 20 Nov 2026, and stands in for the cascade (the default since 22 Nov) for an app that can't feed `cuts`; a device can choose another in Scan settings ("Models you can switch").
- **Retraining:** `scanner-lab/README.md`. The scripts (`sim.py`, `train.py`, `export.py`, `sweep.py`, `calib.py` and the rest), their package versions, and how to get the inputs. Bryan's photos and the database copy they're labelled with are people's data and stay outside the repository; anyone retraining uses their own (`yama-server scan-samples-export`). ## Speed (2026-11-20)

Bryan, 20 Nov 2026, pointing at CardVision (app.mtgscan.org, Florian Drechsler's free, closed-source iPhone app): "want to be able to do as well as this guy. He is really fast". Its own pages describe a YOLO pose detector for the four corners (it places a corner a finger covers), a ResNet-50 trained with ArcFace on card art matching about 61,000 artworks stored on the phone, then the set symbol and collector number to choose among about 100,000 printings, all in Core ML on the iPhone [read]. Its code and models aren't available, and nothing here touches its app or servers.

**Where Yama's time goes** [ran, 20 Nov 2026]: `scanner-lab/speed/` runs `recognizer.ts` (the art worker's code) in Chrome 157 on an M3 Pro, on 160 frames of 40 held-out cards simulated by `sim.py` (sleeves, glare, blue boxes) in a 640 × 1138 portrait frame, four frames per card so tracking runs as it does live. Milliseconds, medians over the last 156 frames (the first card warms up); "tracked" frames reuse the last card window (one detector pass), "fresh" ones search for the card (two passes). Every configuration put the right card first on all 160 frames.

| Backend | Detector pass | Art embedding | Search | Frame, tracked | Frame, fresh |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WASM, 1 thread: what Yama runs (it isn't cross-origin isolated) | 50 | 61.5 (69 before the card window) | 9.8 | 122 (130) | 174 (180) |
| WASM, 2 threads | 27 | 34 | 5.2 | 67 | 95 |
| WASM, 4 threads | 21 | 27 | 3.4 | 52 | 71 |
| WebGPU, `onnxruntime-web/webgpu` (asyncify) | 14.6 | 16.5 | 3.1 | 36 | 51 |
| WebGPU, the JSPI build | 13.3 | 14.6 | 2.9 | 33 | 47 |

- **Why the models cost what they do.** The detector runs four rotated copies of its 256 px window as one batch of four (they vote on which way is up), so each pass is four CNN runs. The embed graph warps the card out, cuts it 17 ways (one cut per frame style: modern, old, extended, tall, token and twelve rarer ones) and runs MobileNetV3-Small on all 17; 92% of the gallery's 52,350 rows are modern or old. Native onnxruntime on the same Mac (one thread) gives about the same: 54 ms a detector pass, 65 ms an embedding, so the browser's WASM isn't the problem; the work is.
- **The card window.** The embed graph converted the whole frame to float before warping the card out of it (a 640 × 1138 slice, cast and transpose: about 6 ms natively). `recognizer.ts` now gives it only the quad's bounding box and a few pixels beyond (`cardWindow`): the same embeddings (largest score change 7e-5 over the 160 frames, the same top card on all of them) from about a third of the pixels. The graph normalises its warp by the scene's own size, so a cut scene only shifts it. A quad folded over itself (a corner dragged past the diagonal) samples outside its corners' box, so it keeps the whole frame.
- **WebGPU** gives the same answers (the same top card on all 160 frames; scores within 3e-4 on a fresh frame) three to four times faster on this Mac, but only with onnxruntime-web's newer WebGPU build: the default `onnxruntime-web` bundle's WebGPU kernels (JSEP) reject both bundle graphs (a reversed Slice in the detector's rotations comes out 255 px wide, and the embed graph's dynamic slices fail). The WebGPU build's WASM runtime is 28.7 MB (the JSPI one 17.9 MB) against 14.8 MB.
- **Reading a frame** back (a synthetic 2160 × 3840 canvas drawn at 640 × 1138 and read; the camera's video element may cost more) takes 1 to 2.5 ms on this Mac.
- **Per scan**, a card that leads settles on its second frame (`LEAD_FRAMES`): one fresh and one tracked frame, about 0.3 s here before any printing read.
- **On a phone** [inferred, not measured]: Bryan's Pixel 10 Pro (Tensor G5) runs one WASM thread perhaps 1.3 to 2 times slower than an M3 Pro core, more on a mid core, so about 160 to 250 ms a tracked frame and 0.4 to 0.7 s to settle a card, before the printing read. Whether its WebView offers WebGPU is unknown. Nothing recorded a phone's frame times then (Scanner diagnostics kept only struggling scans); since 21 Nov 2026 the speed test does ("Speed test").
- **What makes CardVision faster** [inferred from its description]: Core ML on the iPhone's Neural Engine runs a CNN in a few milliseconds instead of tens; its detector makes one pass and its embedder one crop per frame, where Yama's makes four and seventeen; and it reads the camera natively, with its printings catalogue on the phone.

### Faster options (2026-11-20)

Two models came of the measurements, each an option in Scan settings, Models ("Models you can switch"); both are the defaults since 22 Nov 2026 ("The faster models as the defaults", below). Release [`scanner-models-20261120`](https://github.com/bryanroscoe/yama/releases/tag/scanner-models-20261120) holds both files; `server/src/scan.rs` pins them (`QUERY_CASCADE`, `CORNERS_MODEL`).

- **Two cuts first** (`yama-art-20261118-cascade`, art slot; `scanner-lab/cascade.py`). The same weights as `yama-art-20261118` with an extra `cuts` input naming the frame cuts to embed. `recognizer.ts` embeds the modern and old cuts first (92% of the gallery's rows) and the other 15 only when those don't put a card 0.70 or more (0.68 until 22 Nov 2026) and 0.10 ahead of every other (`FIRST_STAGE`, `FIRST_FLOOR`); otherwise everything is searched as before, so a frame never costs more than one extra search. Measured offline against all 17 cuts [ran]: on the 1,840 held-out simulated captures the lead rule locked the same 96.2% right, 0 wrong and 9 unknown cards, 89.8% settled by the first stage; on the 124 real photos the same 94.4%, 0 wrong, 87.1% by the first stage. At 0.65 for the first stage it locked 2 simulated captures wrongly, hence 0.68; 0.70 since 22 Nov 2026 (below). With the set lock on, every cut is embedded at once.
- **Yama's card finder** (`yama-corners-20261120`, new detector slot; `scanner-lab/corners_*.py`). torchvision's MobileNetV3-Small (BSD-3-Clause, ImageNet weights) with a small decoder to a 64 × 64 grid; each corner as printed (top-left first) is a softmax-weighted vote of grid cells plus offsets, so a covered corner, or one past the window's edge, is still placed. One view of the window instead of the bundle's four. Same input and outputs as the bundle's `detector.onnx` (`up` carries the presence score), so `recognizer.ts` runs it unchanged. Trained on 27,843 windows cut from about 14,500 `sim.py` captures of cards drawn from 24,000: 20,172 windows as the recognizer cuts them (any rotation, fingers and thumbs over corners and edges, other cards half in view, empty windows) and 7,671 from tight photos of the card windowed with their edges clamped, as the kept photos are. Nine epochs of an 18-epoch schedule from ImageNet weights (the run was stopped there), then 8 more with the tight photos added; about 50 minutes on an M3 Pro's GPU; epoch 6 of the second run shipped.

## How the OCR pipeline works

```
frame ─▶ grey ─▶ guide quad ─▶ snap to the card's edges (locate.ts)
      ─▶ collector crop, upscaled ─▶ OCR (block, uppercase whitelist) ─▶ parse set, number, language
      ─▶ title crop, cropped to its text band ─▶ OCR (single line) ─▶ fuzzy match over ~37k names
      ─▶ decide: lookup(set, number); the title confirms it, or picks the printing from the set or number
```

- The crops are sampled straight from the camera frame through the card's homography, at 2400 px per card height for the collector line and 1200 for the title. Nothing is straightened at full size.
- The parser fixes OCR lookalikes (`M0M` to `MOM`), anchors the set code on the language code after it (`MOM • EN`), takes the number only from the line above, and ignores copyright years.
- Confidence: collector line plus a matching title is 0.97. A clear title plus the set code or the number is 0.8 to 0.9. The collector line alone is 0.85 only when the set code was read exactly and the number is zero-padded or has a total (`0214`, `030/280`); otherwise 0.5. A title alone is at most 0.9 and gives no printing. `ScannerView` locks on at 0.95, or when the same card comes back twice within 2.5 s at 0.6 or more.
- Upside-down cards: the live view alternates upright and 180° reads between frames. `scanFrame` retries at 180° itself when nothing sure was found (used for photos).

## Measured results [ran]

Setup: 26 real card scans from Scryfall's image CDN (`large`, 672 × 936), rendered into 1080 × 1440 "phone frames" near an on-screen guide (78% of the frame width; 50% for "far"). Card mix: 10 from 2023 or later, 5 from 2015-2022, 2 double-faced (a battle and a transform card), 3 full-art or showcase (a full-art Forest, borderless Sheoldred, a Mystical Archive), 3 from 2003-2014, 3 from 1993-2003 (one white-bordered). Five capture conditions, each card once per condition (130 scans):

- **steady**: 1° rotation, 0.5% skew, slight blur, JPEG 90, felt background.
- **handheld**: 3% offset from the guide, scale 0.94 to 1.03, 4° rotation, 2% skew, darker, side lighting, blur σ 1.2, noise, JPEG 75, wood.

### Why these numbers are optimistic [inferred]

- The scenes are Scryfall's flat, evenly lit scans. Real photos add sleeves, foil glare, focus breathing, motion blur and rolling-shutter skew.
- The embedding gallery was built from the same Scryfall art crops the scenes were made from, so retrieval is near-duplicate matching. ManaVault does train on synthetic phone scenes and real captures [read], which should help, but measure on real photos before trusting 96%.
- Phone CPUs are slower than an M3 Pro, and a web view without cross-origin isolation gets one WASM thread.

## Using it

```ts
import { ScannerView } from "./scanner";
<ScannerView onAccept={(card, copies) => addToCollection(card, copies)} onClose={() => navigate(-1)} />
```

`card` is a `ScanCandidate`: `{ name, set?, number?, lang?, scryfall_id?, image?, confidence, method }`. `method` is `"art"`, `"collector+name"`, `"collector"` or `"name"`. Unless `identityOnly` is set, `onAccept` is only offered once the printing is settled, so `scryfall_id` is always there.

`identityOnly` is for uses where only the card matters: the plan's swap check, scanning a whole deck (`web/src/decks/DeckScanPage.tsx`, `/decks/:id/scan`) checking one (`web/src/decks/DeckCheckPage.tsx`, `/decks/:id/check`) and scanning a box (`web/src/pages/BoxCheckPage.tsx`, `/collection/check/:id`). It looks up no printings. With `autoAdd` (Keep adding) it adds any card the camera found unless the art also looks like another card (`addsByIdentity`); a typed name still waits for Add. `NameEntry.tsx` is the type-a-name box, also used by the deck scan page.

```ts
const res = await scanFrame(imageData, { ocr, catalog, names, guide });
// res.candidates (best first), res.quad, res.raw (OCR text), res.timings
```
