Privacy policy

Last updated November 12, 2026 (new decks are public by default; checking a deck without an account; what Cloudflare measures, and its cookies).

Yama (yama.ing) is a free, non-commercial hobby app for playing and tracking Magic: The Gathering Commander games. It is run by one person, Bryan Roscoe, on a home server in the United States. This page says what Yama stores, why, who else is involved, and how to have your data deleted.

What Yama gets from Google

You sign in with Google. Yama asks Google only for your basic profile: your email address, your name and Google's ID for your account. Yama uses them to make and recognise your account and to show your name to people you play with. It does not get your password and has no access to your Gmail, contacts, calendar, Drive or anything else in your Google account.

What you put in

Whatever you create in Yama is stored with your account: decks and their versions, your card collection, life totals and logged games, playgroups, online games and where they stop for you, deck plans and review answers, simulation runs, script keys, and the apps you connect, such as Claude (their keys and sign-in tokens are stored only as a one-way hash).

The card scanner looks at the camera on your device; video doesn't leave it. If you turn on Save scan photos in the scanner (it's off unless you do), Yama keeps a small photo of the part of the frame around each card you add or correct, with what the card was, so the scanner can be tested and improved on real cards. They are stored with your account and used only to test and train Yama's scanner, never shown to other people or sent to another service. Yama keeps the newest 2,000 and deletes them when you tap Delete them in the scanner or delete your account.

The life counter also works without an account. Then nothing about your games reaches Yama: they stay in your browser on that device until you sign in there and choose to save them to your account. Looking up a commander sends only what you typed.

Check a deck works without an account too. The list you paste is kept in your browser on that device. To show the result your browser sends the list to Yama, on each visit and when you pick another commander. Yama works out the answer (legality, brackets, power, stats and market value) and keeps neither the list nor the answer; its log notes only that a check was made, with no address and nothing from the list. Yama doesn't send that list to Commander Spellbook or any other service. If you sign in and choose to save it, it becomes a deck in your account like any other.

Who can see it

  • Each deck is Public, Link only or Private, and you choose. Yama's decks became public by default on November 12, 2026. On an account made after that, a new deck starts Public unless you choose Link only or Private when you save it; your first deck too. Yama remembers that choice for your next deck; "New decks start as" in Sharing settings changes it for every deck you add afterwards, and each deck's Share button changes that deck. A deck added without that step (saved from Check a deck, copied from a precon, brought in by a Mythic Tools deck import, or staged by an assistant you connected) starts as the same setting, and the page or the staged change says so first. A deck Yama makes from a box in your collection or from a plan's list always starts Private.
  • A public deck can be seen by anyone. It is on your profile at /u/ and the handle you pick, it can be listed on Yama (in Browse, and on the pages for its commander and for the precon it started from), and search engines may index it. Every shared deck and profile page has a Report link, and a moderator can take a deck down; it is then private, and stays private after it is restored until you share it again. Until you pick a handle, a public deck is reachable by its link only; picking one puts every public deck you have on your profile, where it can be listed. A link-only deck can be seen by anyone who has its link; it is listed nowhere, and its page asks search engines not to index it. A private deck is seen only by you. Changing a deck or resetting its link takes effect on Yama at once; a search engine may show an older copy of a public page until it reads the page again.
  • Accounts made before that keep the setting and the decks they had. Any public deck on such an account stays on its profile only, and asks search engines not to index it, until you answer the question Yama asks once on your Decks page: "Yama decks are now public by default. Make yours public?" Once you answer, your public decks are listed like any other public deck. The question lets you change them to Link only instead, and no deck changes unless you choose that there.
  • What a shared deck shows. Its name, your handle if you have one, its card list, the printings its exports name (the printing you picked for the deck or, for a card you own, the printing and finish of your copy), its commander's art, the bracket you declare and the description you write for it, with what Yama works out from the list itself: whether it's Commander-legal, the bracket suggestion and the Game Changers and combos behind it, deck stats and its market value. If you turn on "Show simulated win rates on shared decks" and your upgrade plan for the deck has simulated games, it also shows the simulated win rates of this deck's list and of the configurations you've made public, never the other lists or your plan's changes. Signed-in people can find your handle (never your name) when they seat players for a game, and offer your public decks for your seat; a game they log puts nothing on your record unless you share a playgroup with them.
  • Everything else is private to your account unless you share it. Your collection and what you're looking for show on your profile only if you turn them on in Sharing settings: then it lists the names, printings, finishes, conditions and counts of your real cards. A plan is shared only if you share it, by link or on your profile. Nothing you share ever shows your name, email, prices you paid, notes, where cards are filed, proxies, games or playgroups, or a plan you haven't shared.
  • The public precon reference contains stock decklists, dated public source data and deliberately published stock-precon simulation results. It does not publish your decks, collection, plans, games, personal experiments or account details. Reading it requires no account and does not load your private data.
  • The exception is what you choose to share with others: people in a playgroup you join, or players in a game you play together, see that game and the names and decks shown in it.
  • The person who runs Yama can see the database while maintaining the server, and looks only when needed to keep it working or to answer a request from you.
  • To help with a problem, an admin can view your account as you see it, read only, for up to 30 minutes at a time ("View as"). They can't change anything, and your script keys, connected apps and Ask Claude (your conversations and any API key you saved) stay hidden. They can't connect an app to your account.
  • Bug reports. When you report a bug, Yama keeps what you write, the page's address, the dialog you sent it from, the words and button names inside any box you drew round the problem, your window size and your browser's name. No screenshot is taken. Only Yama's admins read reports; they're deleted with your account.
  • Yama doesn't sell, rent or trade your data and shows no ads. The only visit measurement is Cloudflare's, described under Other services; nothing follows you to other sites.

Cookies

Yama sets one cookie, yama_session, which keeps you signed in for up to 90 days. There are no advertising or tracking cookies. Cloudflare may set its own cookies (such as __cf_bm and cf_clearance) to tell people from automated traffic. Google's sign-in button may set Google's own cookies under Google's policies.

Other services involved

  • Google handles sign-in.
  • Cloudflare carries traffic between your browser and the server, so it sees your IP address and requests. It checks that visitors aren't automated, which can show a short check before a page loads, and it counts page visits and measures how fast pages load (Cloudflare Web Analytics, which uses no cookies and doesn't identify you).
  • Scryfall serves card images and card data. Your browser loads images straight from Scryfall, so Scryfall sees your IP address.
  • Commander Spellbook receives a deck's card list (by card ID, with no account details) when Yama checks a deck saved to an account for combos and its Commander bracket. A list checked without an account is not sent.
  • Apps you connect. If you connect Claude (Claude Code, or a connector in Claude's apps) after Yama asks you to allow it, that app reads your decks, plans, Review, collection and lab runs through Yama's tools, under its own terms. Disconnect it any time in Decks, Plans, Settings.

IP addresses and logs

Yama uses your IP address for a short time, in memory, to limit repeated sign-in attempts, new accounts, card searches and deck checks made without an account, and reports of shared pages. It is not stored with your account and is forgotten when the server restarts. The server keeps short technical logs for fixing problems.

If you report a shared deck or profile, Yama keeps which page it was, the reason you picked and anything you wrote, so a moderator can look at it. It does not keep who sent the report. Reports about an account's pages are deleted when that account is deleted.

How long data is kept, and deleting it

Your data stays until you delete it. To delete your account and everything stored with it, open Your profile, Account settings in Yama and choose Delete account, or email bryanroscoe@gmail.com from the address you sign in with; Delete your account says how and what stays for other people's records, without your name. It is removed from the live database at once. Copies of the database taken before server updates, kept to recover from a failed update, may still hold it until they are cleared. You can also remove Yama's access in your Google account's security settings at any time.

Children

Yama isn't meant for children under 13, and doesn't knowingly collect their data.
